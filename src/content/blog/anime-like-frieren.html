The anime most like Frieren, ranked

Here are the anime like Frieren that genuinely share its build, ranked from the closest match to the loosest. The Similarity Rating is our read on how near each one gets to Frieren specifically, not how good the show is overall, and we kept the stars to whole numbers. The build we are measuring against is simple: a slow, contemplative journey carried by atmosphere and quiet emotion, usually about time, memory, and the people we outlive.

1. Mushishi

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll, Prime Video

Synopsis: Ginko is a traveling Mushi master, wandering a timeless, nature-steeped Japan to study and help people troubled by Mushi, strange primordial life-forms that exist between the living and the spiritual. Each episode is a self-contained story about someone whose life has brushed up against these quiet, often melancholy phenomena. There is no grand villain and no overarching war, just people, nature, and the mysteries in between. It is hushed, gorgeous, and deeply contemplative.

For the actual feeling of watching Frieren, nothing comes closer. It shares the unhurried episodic rhythm, the wandering healer moving from one small human story to the next, and the gentle melancholy that lingers after each one. The fantasy here is quiet and grounded rather than flashy, exactly like Frieren's, and its themes of time, memory, and coexistence echo Frieren's reflections on a long life. If you loved the show's stillness, start here.

2. To Your Eternity

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: A mysterious immortal being is sent to Earth with no self and no memory, able to take the shape of anything it has lost. Named Fushi, it slowly learns what it means to be alive by living alongside people, loving them, and watching them die as the centuries pass. Each arc introduces a new place, a new era, and a new set of people to lose. It is beautiful and frequently devastating.

On premise, this is the closest match to Frieren there is: an immortal protagonist processing mortality, memory, and grief across a span of time no human could live. Fushi outliving everyone it loves is Frieren outliving her party, the same ache from the same impossible vantage point. It runs more overtly tragic and plot-driven than Frieren's gentle melancholy, so brace yourself, but the emotional core is identical.

3. The Ancient Magus' Bride

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Chise Hatori, a girl who has only ever been unwanted, is bought at auction by Elias, a towering, bone-headed magus who declares she will be his apprentice and, one day, his bride. In his cottage on the edge of an old, folklore-rich England, she begins to learn magic and, slowly, to heal. The world is full of fae, dragons, and quiet wonder. It is a gentle, atmospheric fantasy about being seen.

The build overlaps on several fronts: a slow, mood-soaked magical world, a mentor-and-student bond at the center, and an organic, mystical approach to magic that rewards curiosity rather than spectacle, much like Frieren teaching Fern. Atsumi Tanezaki even voices both Chise and Frieren, and the two shows share a wavelength, the quiet, bittersweet, healing kind of fantasy. It is more about emotional recovery than mortality, which is the one place it drifts.

4. Spice and Wolf

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Kraft Lawrence, a traveling merchant, picks up Holo, a centuries-old wolf deity who takes the form of a sharp-tongued young woman longing to return to her northern home. Together they travel the roads, working through markets, schemes, and their own growing bond. The series runs on dialogue, economics, and the slow warmth between two very different travelers. It is talky, clever, and quietly romantic.

The Frieren connection is Holo herself: a near-immortal being who has watched generations of humans live and die, carrying the loneliness of a lifespan that outlasts everyone she meets. That is Frieren's exact burden, explored through a slow, dialogue-driven road trip rather than a quest. The measured pace, the bittersweet awareness of time, and the tender odd-couple traveling pair all land for Frieren fans, even with the lighter, warmer tone.

5. Girls' Last Tour

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Prime Video

Synopsis: Chito and Yuuri are the last two people wandering the upper levels of a vast, dead city, puttering along in a little motorized vehicle through a world that has already ended. They search for food and fuel, talk about nothing and everything, and find small pockets of beauty in the ruins. There is no rescue and no grand goal, only the quiet act of continuing. It is spare, melancholy, and strangely peaceful.

What it shares is the mood and the pacing: a slow, contemplative journey through an atmospheric world, where the point is not the destination but the small, human moments along the way. Like Frieren, it sits with loss and impermanence without melodrama, finding gentle warmth in a world shadowed by endings. If what you loved about Frieren was the quiet philosophizing between stops, this is a kindred spirit.

6. Natsume's Book of Friends

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Takashi Natsume can see the spirits, or youkai, that everyone else ignores, a gift that left him lonely and distrusted as a child. Inheriting a book that binds many of their names, he sets about returning those names, and with them their freedom, aided by a grumpy spirit bodyguard. Each episode is a gentle, often bittersweet encounter with a different spirit and its story. It is warm, quiet, and quietly healing.

The overlap with Frieren is the tone and the structure: a soft-spoken, episodic drift through encounters that are small in scale but heavy with feeling, usually about connection, loneliness, and letting go. Natsume moving among the long-lived youkai, who remember people long dead, mirrors Frieren's place among humans whose lives flicker past. For the gentle melancholy and the kindness at Frieren's heart, few shows match it.

7. Somali and the Forest Spirit

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: In a world where humans have been nearly wiped out, a golem who guards a forest finds a lost human child named Somali and sets out to reunite her with others of her kind. The catch is that the golem is slowly running down, with only a short while left to live. Their journey takes them across a strange, beautiful fantasy world full of non-human peoples. It is tender and bittersweet from the first episode.

What lines up is the traveling pair and the shadow of mortality: a long-lived, non-human guardian and the fragile human in their care, making a journey they both know cannot last forever. That ticking clock and the quiet parent-child warmth echo Frieren's reflections on time and the people we cannot keep. It is gentler and more episodic than plot-heavy, which is exactly the Frieren register, even if it is softer around the edges.

8. Kino's Journey

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Kino, a young traveler, rides a talking motorcycle named Hermes from country to country, staying just three days in each before moving on. Every land has its own strange customs, philosophy, or quiet cruelty, observed rather than judged. Kino rarely intervenes, choosing instead to watch, learn, and move along. It is a contemplative, episodic travelogue with a melancholy streak.

The build match is the reflective, episodic journey itself: a quiet traveler passing through a series of self-contained stories, each one a small meditation on people and how they live. Like Frieren, it values observation and stillness over action, and it leaves you turning its quieter moments over long after. It is more philosophical and less warm than Frieren, so it sits mid-list, but the wandering, thoughtful spirit is a close relative.

9. A Place Further Than the Universe

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Four high school girls, each carrying something unspoken, band together on an improbable quest to reach Antarctica. One of them is chasing the memory of a mother who went there and never came back. What begins as a lark becomes a genuine expedition, and a story about grief, friendship, and finally moving forward. It is funny, heartfelt, and quietly devastating.

Here is the one pick on the list that is not fantasy, included because it nails Frieren's emotional build so precisely: a meaningful journey that becomes a way of processing loss, memory, and the people who shaped us. The arc of grieving someone who is gone, of retracing their steps to understand them, is the exact emotional engine of Frieren's travels. If you came for the feelings rather than the magic, this will wreck you in the best way.

10. Wandering Witch: The Journey of Elaina

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Elaina, a gifted young witch, sets out to see the world the way her favorite storybook heroine once did, drifting from town to town with no particular destination. Some stops are whimsical, some are dark, and a few are quietly haunting. She observes more than she fixes, keeping a traveler's distance. It is a pretty, episodic road story with an uneven but memorable emotional range.

The build overlap is the episodic travelogue and the wandering-mage framing: a solitary magic user drifting through self-contained vignettes, some gentle, some bittersweet, in a lovely fantasy world. It leans lighter and less thematically focused than Frieren, and the tone swings more, but when it lands a melancholy story it reaches for the same quiet ache. Think of it as the breezier cousin of Frieren's travels.

11. Flying Witch

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Makoto, a young witch in training, moves in with relatives in rural northern Japan to continue her studies, bringing a little everyday magic into their ordinary lives. Nothing dramatic happens, and that is the point, as she gardens, meets gentle spirits, and drinks tea while the seasons turn. The magic is woven quietly into the slow rhythm of country life. It is one of the most soothing shows around.

The connection is the gentle, slow-life side of Frieren, the quiet appreciation of small, ordinary moments made a little magical. It has none of the mortality or the grand history, so it sits at the loosest end of this list, but for the comfy, unhurried, everyday-fantasy feeling that makes Frieren such a balm after a long day, it is a lovely match. Put it on when you want calm more than catharsis.

A couple of close matches to know about

Two of the shows nearest to Frieren's heart, Violet Evergarden and Delicious in Dungeon, stream on Netflix, where availability swings hard by region, so we kept them off the main list rather than send you somewhere you might not be able to follow. Both are worth seeking out if you have it, Violet Evergarden for the same ache about memory and connection, and Delicious in Dungeon for a warm, worldbuilding-rich party journey, though it trades Frieren's quiet melancholy for food-fueled comedy.

One more comes up constantly: Mushoku Tensei: Jobless Reincarnation. It shares Frieren's love of a deep, lived-in fantasy world and the feeling of growing alongside a character across a lifetime, but it is a louder, more fan-service-heavy isekai, so the real overlap is the worldbuilding, not the gentle, reflective tone.

Sit with the feeling a little longer

Frieren is a hard act to follow, because so much of its magic lives in the silence between moments. Start at the top with Mushishi or To Your Eternity for the purest version of that feeling, lean into the long-lived loneliness of Spice and Wolf, or let the gentle melancholy of Girls' Last Tour and Natsume's Book of Friends carry you, and you will have a quiet, lovely queue to sit with while Season 2 plays out. FAQs

Is there an anime exactly like Frieren?

Not really, and that is part of why it stands out. The closest in feeling is Mushishi, which shares the quiet, episodic, wandering structure, while To Your Eternity comes closest on premise, with an immortal lead processing the same loss and passage of time that Frieren does.

What to watch after Frieren

If you want the smoothest landing, start at the top of this list: Mushishi, To Your Eternity, The Ancient Magus' Bride, or Spice and Wolf. Each one carries over the slow pace, the atmospheric fantasy, or the ache about time and memory that made Frieren so special.

When does Frieren Season 2 come out?

Season 2 premiered on January 16, 2026 on Crunchyroll, with Studio Madhouse returning. It continues Frieren, Fern, and Stark's journey and leans further into the show's themes of time, memory, and connection.

What anime has the same feeling as Frieren?

For the quiet, bittersweet mood specifically, Mushishi, Girls' Last Tour, and Natsume's Book of Friends are the closest. If it is the emotional-journey feeling you are after, A Place Further Than the Universe captures the grief-and-growth side of Frieren beautifully.

Where can you watch anime like Frieren?

Every pick on this list streams on Crunchyroll, including Frieren: Beyond Journey's End itself. Streaming rights shift by region and over time, so a couple of the closest matches sit on other services, and it is worth double-checking what is available where you are before you settle in.
