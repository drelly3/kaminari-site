The anime most like Frieren, ranked

Here are the anime like Frieren that genuinely share its build, ranked from the closest match to the loosest. The Similarity Rating is our read on how near each one gets to Frieren specifically, not how good the show is overall, and we kept the stars to whole numbers. The build we are measuring against is simple: a slow, contemplative journey carried by atmosphere and quiet emotion, usually about time, memory, and the people we outlive.

1. Mushishi

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Ginko is a traveling Mushi master, wandering a timeless, nature-steeped Japan to study and help people troubled by Mushi, strange primordial life-forms that exist between the living and the spiritual. Each episode is a self-contained story about someone whose life has brushed up against these quiet, often melancholy phenomena. There is no grand villain and no overarching war, just people, nature, and the mysteries in between. It is hushed, gorgeous, and deeply contemplative.

For the actual feeling of watching Frieren, nothing comes closer, which makes Mushishi the undisputed atmospheric twin. Ginko travels on foot through misty, rural Japanese wilderness to investigate and harmonize the Mushi, and like Frieren he is a calm, wandering specialist who values quiet observation over conflict. The series shares Frieren's contemplative stillness, its breathtaking nature, and its deep respect for the quiet rhythms of life and death, all delivered in the same unhurried, episodic form. If you loved the show's stillness, start here.

2. To Your Eternity

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: A mysterious immortal being is sent to Earth with no self and no memory, able to take the shape of anything it has lost. Named Fushi, it slowly learns what it means to be alive by living alongside people, loving them, and watching them die as the centuries pass. Each arc introduces a new place, a new era, and a new set of people to lose. It is beautiful and frequently devastating.

On premise, To Your Eternity is the direct thematic counterpart for immortal grief. An immortal entity called Fushi begins as an unfeeling stone and slowly learns what it means to be human by wandering the earth, forming deep bonds with mortal companions and carrying the agonizing, beautiful weight of their memories long after they age and die. Fushi outliving everyone it loves is Frieren outliving her party, the same ache from the same impossible vantage point. It runs more overtly tragic and plot-driven than Frieren's gentle melancholy, so brace yourself, but the emotional core is identical.

3. Maquia: When the Promised Flower Blooms

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll, Prime Video

Synopsis: Maquia belongs to the Iorph, a secluded race who live for centuries and stop aging as teenagers, weaving their long memories into cloth. When her homeland is destroyed, she flees alone and finds an orphaned human baby in the wreckage of a village, and on impulse decides to raise him as her own. As the boy grows into a man and ages past her, she must live with the knowledge that she will outlast him. It is a staggeringly beautiful film about motherhood and loss.

Maquia is a masterclass in the exact asymmetry of time that haunts Frieren. A member of a near-immortal race rescues an orphaned human infant and raises him, and the film follows the bittersweet, impossible reality of loving someone whose entire lifetime is just a brief chapter of your own, which is Frieren's relationship with Himmel and Fern rendered in its purest, most devastating form. The quiet, elegiac tone and the ache of watching a mortal life flicker past from an immortal's vantage point are pure Frieren. It is a single film rather than a wandering series, but emotionally it may be the closest thing here.

4. Somali and the Forest Spirit

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: In a world where humans have been nearly wiped out, a golem who guards a forest finds a lost human child named Somali and sets out to reunite her with others of her kind. The catch is that the golem is slowly running down, with only a short while left to live. Their journey takes them across a strange, beautiful fantasy world full of non-human peoples. It is tender and bittersweet from the first episode.

Somali and the Forest Spirit is a parent-mentor journey lived under the shadow of finite time. A stoic, near-immortal ancient golem with only about a year left to live travels across a whimsical fantasy world to find a safe home for an energetic human child before the end comes. That protective, teaching dynamic between an ageless guardian and the small human in their care mirrors Frieren and Fern directly, and the ticking clock over it all echoes Frieren's reflections on time and the people we cannot keep. It is gentler and more episodic than plot-heavy, which is exactly the Frieren register, even if it is softer around the edges.

5. Kino's Journey

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Apple TV

Synopsis: Kino, a young traveler, rides a talking motorcycle named Hermes from country to country, staying just three days in each before moving on. Every land has its own strange customs, philosophy, or quiet cruelty, observed rather than judged. Kino rarely intervenes, choosing instead to watch, learn, and move along. It is a contemplative, episodic travelogue with a melancholy streak.

Kino's Journey is the quintessential philosophical travelogue. Kino and a talking motorrad named Hermes spend just three days in each of a string of unique fantasy city-states, observing local cultures, customs, and laws without ever passing judgment, then moving on. That patient curiosity about how different places live, their traditions, their quirky histories, their forgotten rules, reflects Frieren's own fascination with local customs, odd historical artifacts, and half-remembered regional spells. It is more philosophical and less warm than Frieren, so it sits mid-list, but the wandering, thoughtful, observe-and-move-on spirit is a close relative.

6. Girls' Last Tour

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Prime Video

Synopsis: Chito and Yuuri are the last two people wandering the upper levels of a vast, dead city, puttering along in a little motorized vehicle through a world that has already ended. They search for food and fuel, talk about nothing and everything, and find small pockets of beauty in the ruins. There is no rescue and no grand goal, only the quiet act of continuing. It is spare, melancholy, and strangely peaceful.

Girls' Last Tour captures the peaceful, post-apocalyptic contemplation of life after the end. Chito and Yuuri travel across the decaying levels of a vast, empty megacity, finding small, quiet joy in scavenging rations, taking photographs, and simply sitting with the ruins of a civilization that is already gone. That elegiac, after-the-adventure feeling echoes the aftermath Frieren lives in, the long, gentle quiet that follows once the great journey is already over. It sits with loss and impermanence without a trace of melodrama, finding warmth in a world shadowed by endings, which is Frieren's register exactly.

7. Spice and Wolf

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Kraft Lawrence, a traveling merchant, picks up Holo, a centuries-old wolf deity who takes the form of a sharp-tongued young woman longing to return to her northern home. Together they travel the roads, working markets, dodging schemes, and slowly growing closer. The series runs on dialogue, economics, and the slow warmth between two very different travelers. It is talky, clever, and quietly romantic.

The Frieren connection is the timeless-wanderer-and-mortal-companion dynamic at the show's heart. The merchant Kraft Lawrence works the market towns alongside Holo, an ancient wolf deity who has outlived generation after generation of humans, and their journey balances the gentle, mundane rhythm of life on the road with the lingering melancholy of knowing Holo will one day be left alone again. That loneliness of a lifespan that outlasts everyone you meet is Frieren's exact burden, explored here through a slow, dialogue-driven road trip. The measured pace and the tender odd-couple pairing land for Frieren fans, even with the lighter, warmer tone.

8. Natsume's Book of Friends

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Takashi Natsume can see the spirits, or youkai, that everyone else ignores, a gift that left him lonely and distrusted as a child. Inheriting a book that binds many of their names, he sets about returning those names, and with them their freedom, aided by a grumpy spirit bodyguard. Each episode is a gentle, often bittersweet encounter with a different spirit and its story. It is warm, quiet, and quietly healing.

Natsume's Book of Friends is a gentle, bittersweet exploration of the gap between mortal and spirit lifespans. Takashi Natsume spends the series returning the stolen names of youkai who once knew his late grandmother, and in doing so he keeps brushing up against how spirits retain centuries of affection and longing for human friends who faded away in what, to them, was the blink of an eye. That is Frieren's perspective turned around, and the soft-spoken, episodic drift through small encounters heavy with feeling matches Frieren's tone beautifully. For the gentle melancholy and the kindness at Frieren's heart, few shows match it.

9. The Ancient Magus' Bride

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Chise Hatori, a girl who has only ever been unwanted, is bought at auction by Elias, a towering, bone-headed magus who declares she will be his apprentice and, one day, his bride. In his cottage on the edge of an old, folklore-rich England, she begins to learn magic and, slowly, to heal. The world is full of fae, dragons, and quiet wonder. It is a gentle, atmospheric fantasy about being seen.

The Ancient Magus' Bride builds a rich European fantasy world of fae, ancient gods, and half-forgotten magic, and sets at its center a bond much like Frieren and Fern's. Elias Ainsworth is a non-human, ancient being slowly learning human emotion, while his young apprentice Chise faces her own mortal fragility and learns obscure ritual magic at his side. The slow, mood-soaked atmosphere and the mentor-and-student relationship are deeply Frieren, and the organic, curiosity-rewarding approach to magic echoes Frieren teaching Fern. It leans more toward emotional recovery than mortality, which is the one place it drifts, but the wavelength is unmistakable.

10. Delicious in Dungeon

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Netflix

Synopsis: After his sister is swallowed by a dragon deep in a sprawling dungeon, the knight Laios leads his ragtag party back down to rescue her, with one problem: they have no money for food. The solution is to cook and eat the monsters they fight along the way, from giant scorpions to walking mushrooms. What starts as a gag becomes a rich, deeply-researched adventure about an entire dungeon ecosystem. It is funny, warm, and surprisingly profound.

Delicious in Dungeon shares Frieren's meticulous, lived-in Western high-fantasy worldbuilding and its tabletop-RPG roots. It treats fantasy ecology, monster biology, and the quiet rhythms of a party traveling together with the same grounded sincerity Frieren brings to its own world, and it spends real time on an elf's perspective on lifespan and on the ruins of forgotten ancient kingdoms, which lands squarely in Frieren territory. The tone is far more comedic and food-obsessed than Frieren's gentle melancholy, so it sits lower, but the deep, sincere fantasy worldbuilding is a strong match. One note: it streams on Netflix, so availability depends on your region.

11. Haibane Renmei

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: In a quiet, walled-off town, children are born from cocoons as Haibane, gentle beings with small gray wings and glowing halos who live simple, rustic lives and never remember where they came from. Rakka, a new arrival, slowly settles into this peaceful community while sensing that something deeper and sadder lies beneath it. As she grows close to the others, she begins to understand what the Haibane truly are. It is a hushed, haunting, quietly spiritual series.

Haibane Renmei is a profound, melancholic meditation on grief, self-forgiveness, and the gentle passage of time, which puts it right alongside Frieren in spirit. Set in a quiet, isolated town where winged youths live peaceful, rustic lives, it shares Frieren's patient pacing, its soft focus on small daily moments, and its poignant, inevitable farewells. The fantasy is understated and the mysteries unfold slowly, exactly the register Frieren works in. There is no wandering journey or immortal lead, so it sits at the loosest end of this list, but on pure contemplative, bittersweet mood, few shows reach the same quiet depths.

A few close matches that watch a little differently

A few shows sit right next to Frieren but reach for its feeling from a slightly different angle, and they are worth knowing about.

A Place Further Than the Universe nails Frieren's emotional engine, a meaningful journey that becomes a way to process grief, memory, and the people who shaped us, but it is a modern, real-world story about four girls traveling to Antarctica, with no fantasy to it, so the pull is the feeling rather than the setting. Violet Evergarden reaches for the same ache about memory and connection, though it streams on Netflix, where availability swings hard by region. And Mushoku Tensei: Jobless Reincarnation shares Frieren's love of a deep, lived-in fantasy world and the sense of growing alongside a character across a lifetime, but it is a louder, more fan-service-heavy isekai, so the real overlap is the worldbuilding, not the gentle, reflective tone.

Sit with the feeling a little longer

Frieren is a hard act to follow, because so much of its magic lives in the silence between moments. Start at the top with Mushishi or To Your Eternity for the purest version of that feeling, sit with the heartbreaking asymmetry of time in Maquia, or let the gentle melancholy of Girls' Last Tour and Natsume's Book of Friends carry you, and you will have a quiet, lovely queue to hold onto now that Season 2 has landed. FAQs

Is there an anime exactly like Frieren?

Not really, and that is part of why it stands out. The closest in feeling is Mushishi, which shares the quiet, episodic, wandering structure, while To Your Eternity comes closest on premise, with an immortal lead processing the same loss and passage of time that Frieren does.

What to watch after Frieren

If you want the smoothest landing, start at the top of this list: Mushishi, To Your Eternity, Maquia, Somali and the Forest Spirit, or Spice and Wolf. Each one carries over the slow pace, the atmospheric fantasy, or the ache about time and memory that made Frieren so special.

When does Frieren Season 2 come out?

Season 2 premiered on January 16, 2026 on Crunchyroll, with Studio Madhouse returning. It continues Frieren, Fern, and Stark's journey and leans further into the show's themes of time, memory, and connection.

What anime has the same feeling as Frieren?

For the quiet, bittersweet mood specifically, Mushishi, Girls' Last Tour, and Natsume's Book of Friends are the closest. If it is the immortal-grief side you are after, Maquia and To Your Eternity capture the asymmetry of time between a long-lived heart and a mortal one beautifully.

Where can you watch anime like Frieren?

Most of these stream on Crunchyroll, including Frieren: Beyond Journey's End itself, while Delicious in Dungeon sits on Netflix and Kino's Journey is on Apple TV. Streaming rights shift by region and over time, so double-check what is available where you are before you settle in.
