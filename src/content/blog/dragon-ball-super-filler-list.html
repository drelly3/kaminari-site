Dragon Ball Super has 14 filler episodes out of 131. At 11% filler, it's the cleanest Dragon Ball anime ever made. You can skip everything non-essential and lose barely five hours of content. The number isn't the problem.

The problem is that DBS also has 15 anime-original episodes that aren't in the manga and aren't filler. They're anime canon, and they matter. Some of them directly set up the Tournament of Power. Skip them because a color-coded list told you to, and you'll walk into the show's best arc missing context you can't get back.

Below is the complete Dragon Ball Super filler list with verdicts for every episode, plus the honest calls on which "filler" is actually worth your time. Consider it a Dragon Ball Super filler guide from someone who's watched every episode and has opinions about all of them.

Secretly Peak: DBS Filler Worth Watching

Three filler episodes in Dragon Ball Super that aren't just "good for filler." They're good episodes, full stop.

1. Episode 69: Goku vs. Arale

Before Dragon Ball, Toriyama created Dr. Slump, a gag manga about a hyperactive robot girl named Arale who could punch the planet in half. Episode 69 brings her into the DBS power structure, and the results are beautiful chaos. Arale fights evenly with Super Saiyan Blue Goku. She one-shots Vegeta. She cracks the Earth in half and treats it like a joke, because in her world, it is one. Vegeta breaks the fourth wall to complain about "gag characters," which might be the single funniest moment in the series. A love letter from Toriyama to his own creative roots that works whether you know Dr. Slump or not.

2. Episode 70: Baseball

Champa challenges Universe 7 to a baseball game. Goku doesn't understand the rules. Vegeta takes it too seriously. Yamcha, borderline irrelevant since the Saiyan Saga, finally gets his moment in Dragon Ball Super. And then the moment kills him (sort of): he makes the game-winning play and ends up collapsed in the dirt in his iconic death pose from Z, a callback that became one of the most shared memes in the show's run. Beerus and Champa close it out with a full god-of-destruction fistfight over the score.

3. Episodes 75-76: Krillin's Fighting Spirit

Krillin hasn't fought seriously in years. He's a cop now. Then Goku asks him to train, and the show does something it almost never does: it lets a side character sit with real emotion. The training generates illusions of past enemies. Krillin faces Tambourine, who killed him in the original Dragon Ball. Frieza, who killed him on Namek. Cell. Nappa. The Ginyu Force. Every villain who ever ended his life is standing in front of him again. The payoff isn't a power-up. It's Krillin deciding to keep fighting anyway. For anyone who's been watching since the original series, these episodes are a character payoff decades in the making.

Dragon Ball Super's Best Writing Is in Its Filler

Hot take: the standalone episodes in Dragon Ball Super have better character writing than most of its canon arcs.

Think about it. The main arcs belong to Goku and Vegeta. Everyone else holds the line until the Saiyans show up. But the filler? The filler belongs to Krillin, Gohan, Yamcha, and the rest of the roster that gets sidelined the second a real villain appears. Episodes 75-76 give Krillin more genuine character development than the entire Tournament of Power does. The baseball episode gives Yamcha more personality in 22 minutes than he gets across the rest of the series. The Arale crossover lets Vegeta be funny in a way no serious arc ever allows.

DBZ taught fans to dread the word "filler." The Garlic Jr. arc earned that reputation. But DBS was never stalling for the manga to catch up. Its standalone episodes were written on purpose, as breathers between arcs, and Toei used them to give screen time to characters who'd otherwise get nothing. Skip them if you want. Any Dragon Ball Super filler list, including this one, exists so you can make that call. But the best episodes on the skip list are better than some of the canon surrounding them, and that's not something you can say about Goku learning to drive.

FAQ

How many filler episodes are in Dragon Ball Super?

Fourteen out of 131 episodes, putting DBS at an 11% filler rate. That number jumps to 22 if you count anime-canon episodes (original to the anime but important to the story), which this guide treats separately.

Can I skip Dragon Ball Super episodes 1 through 27?

If you've seen the Battle of Gods and Resurrection F movies, yes. Episodes 1-27 retell both films. Skip to episode 28 (Universe 6 Saga) without losing new plot. If you haven't seen the movies, watch the episodes instead.

Is Dragon Ball Super worth watching without skipping filler?

Yes. Five hours of extra content at 11% filler is nothing for a 131-episode series, and several of those filler episodes (69, 70, 75, 76) are among the most entertaining in the show.

What's the difference between filler and anime canon in Dragon Ball Super?

Filler is standalone content that doesn't affect the plot. Anime canon isn't in the manga either, but it was written for the anime and feeds into the main story. The Hit assassination arc (71-72) and the Universe Survival recruitment (78-81) are anime canon, and skipping them means losing setup for the Tournament of Power.

Does Dragon Ball Super have less filler than Dragon Ball Z?

Yes. DBS is at 11% (14/131) versus DBZ's 13% (38/291). The bigger difference is why the filler exists: DBZ needed it because the anime caught up to the manga. DBS chose it as intentional breathers between arcs.
