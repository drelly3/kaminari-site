The best black anime characters do not ask for the spotlight, they just take it. Afro, Yoruichi, Michiko, and Killer Bee are usually the first names people shout, and the bench behind them runs deep. Some anchor entire series on charisma alone. Others show up for a single arc and casually walk off with the whole thing.

We are talking grizzled swordsmen, a sword-swinging rapper, a jaded music kid scraping by on Mars, and a whole crew of professional troublemakers. A few are icons you already have a poster of. A couple are so new the ink is barely dry, and they are already climbing our list of favorites.

Every one of these anime characters that are black earns the spot on pure personality, so expect strong opinions and at least one pick guaranteed to start an argument in the replies. Fair warning: we play favorites, and we are not sorry about it.

Top 15 black anime characters

These fifteen black anime characters span decades of anime, mixing certified legends with a couple of faces from just last season. The most popular black anime characters share space with a few deeper cuts, and there are more than enough black anime dudes and women here to carry a show on charisma alone. It runs past the usual top 10 black anime characters roundups, so here is our list of black anime characters, in no particular order.

Yoruichi Shihoin

Anime: Bleach

Yoruichi could walk into a room full of soul reaper captains and still look like she is the one being inconvenienced. Former head of Bleach's Second Division, fast enough to earn the Flash Goddess nickname, and casual enough to spend chunks of the series as a house cat purely to mess with people. She mentors Ichigo, trades blows with the strongest fighters in Soul Society, and never once breaks a sweat about it. The blueprint for effortless cool, basically.

Afro

Anime: Afro Samurai

Afro barely says ten words a season and still comes across louder than everyone around him. He wears the Number Two headband and hunts the man holding Number One, the one who cut down his father in front of him when he was a kid. Samuel L. Jackson voices both Afro and his motor-mouthed hype-man Ninja Ninja, which is either a great joke or a stroke of genius, probably both. Pure revenge-movie energy poured into anime form.

Michiko Malandro

Anime: Michiko & Hatchin

Michiko breaks out of prison in the first episode and never really slows down after that. Motorcycle, short fuse, incredible fashion sense, and a stubborn streak a mile wide, she blasts across a sun-baked, Latin-inspired world chasing a man from her past with a reluctant kid in tow. She is not always likable, which is the whole point, and she is impossible to ignore. Nobody in anime carries themselves quite the way she does.

Killer Bee

Anime: Naruto Shippuden

Bee fights with seven swords at once, mentors Naruto through the toughest training in the series, and insists on rapping through most of it whether anyone asked or not. His bars are famously terrible, and we love him more for it. Under the goofball routine sits one of the strongest and most stable characters in the whole franchise, a guy who made peace with the monster sealed inside him and came out the other side laughing. Bee rules, no notes.

Canary

Anime: Hunter x Hunter

Canary gets maybe fifteen minutes early in Hunter x Hunter and makes every second land. A teenage apprentice butler for a family of professional killers, she plants herself at the Zoldyck gate and beats back a whole mob solo, all to do a job most people would have walked away from. Her loyalty to Killua quietly wrecks you once you understand what that household is actually like. Give her a spin-off, cowards.

Yasuke

Anime: Yasuke

Yasuke is the rare anime hero ripped straight out of a history book. The real Yasuke was an African man who served the warlord Oda Nobunaga in the 1500s, and the Netflix series reimagines him as a burnt-out ronin dragged back into a war he thought he had left behind. As an African anime character built on an actual historical figure, he has no real competition, and a Flying Lotus soundtrack gives the whole thing a hazy, melancholy pulse. Worth it for the mood alone.

Carole Stanley

Anime: Carole & Tuesday

Carole is scraping by on a colonized Mars, busking on keys and sax for spare change until she runs into Tuesday and the two decide to actually chase music. She is warm and quietly exhausted in a way that feels real, the kind of lead who makes a low-stakes story hit harder than it has any right to. If you have ever worked a dead-end shift while refusing to give up on the one thing you care about, she gets it.

Dutch

Anime: Black Lagoon

Dutch runs the Lagoon Company, piloting a boat full of gunrunners through the nastiest port in Southeast Asia without ever raising his voice. He is one of the coolest black anime characters around for a simple reason: nothing gets to him. Bullets, betrayal, a crew of certified lunatics, he handles all of it like he is deciding what to order for lunch. Every friend group needs a Dutch, and most of them are not lucky enough to have one.

Ogun Montgomery

Anime: Fire Force

Ogun throws fire and forges weapons, and his reunion with Shinra is one of the warmest stretches in Fire Force. Series creator Atsushi Ohkubo has said he built characters like Ogun because he wanted more Black heroes in his work, and it shows in how much life the guy gets to have. A genuinely good dude who also happens to be a monster in a fight. He deserved more screen time, and we will say that to anyone who listens.

Kilik Rung

Anime: Soul Eater

Kilik is Ohkubo doing the same thing a few years earlier over in Soul Eater. He fights by channeling his partners, the Pot of Fire and Pot of Thunder, basically turning his own body into a weapon, and he looks fantastic pulling it off. He is one of those cool black anime characters who never needs to be the main event to steal a scene. Underrated then, underrated now.

Mohammed Avdol

Anime: JoJo's Bizarre Adventure

Avdol is the steady hand of the Stardust Crusaders, the Egyptian fortune-teller whose Stand, Magician's Red, is basically a flamethrower with an attitude. He plays the wise older-brother type who always seems to have the plan, right up until the series decides to break your heart with him. Calm, loyal, and criminally dependable in a cast full of hotheads. Respect the man.

Onyankopon

Anime: Attack on Titan

Onyankopon shows up in Attack on Titan's back half and immediately becomes the guy keeping everyone alive, the engineer and pilot who gets the Survey Corps airborne right when the story needs a miracle. He is named after an Akan sky god, a small touch that tells you somebody cared. Level-headed and decent in a show that is allergic to both, he is a small mercy every time he walks on.

Agil

Anime: Sword Art Online

Agil is the axe-swinging shopkeeper who has Kirito's back inside the death game and runs a bar once everyone makes it out. He is the dependable big-brother figure every long series needs, proof that you do not have to be the chosen one to be the most-liked person in the room. Reliable to a fault and endlessly patient with a much younger crowd. The kind of friend you actually want in your corner.

Musa Kamara

Anime: Run with the Wind

Musa is the odd one out on this list, and we mean that as a compliment. No powers, no Stands, just a kind-hearted international student and long-distance runner in a grounded drama about a college relay team. His arc is all about finding where he belongs, and it creeps up on you until you are somehow shouting at a screen during a track race. Sports anime works, and Musa is a big reason this one lands so hard.

Semiu Grier

Anime: Gachiakuta

Semiu is the newest face here and already one we are attached to. She works as a Cleaner in Gachiakuta, the 2025 breakout from Studio Bones, and she gets one of the show's sharpest introductions. Fun bit of trivia: creator Kei Urana came up as an assistant to Atsushi Ohkubo, the same guy who made Kilik and Ogun, so the whole tradition of great characters runs straight through her. Confident, quick, and instantly magnetic. Keep an eye on this one.

Why the roster keeps getting deeper

The old guard used to carry all the weight, a short list of legends you could rattle off in one breath. Now Yoruichi and Afro share the screen with new favorites turning up every year, and Gachiakuta alone dropped a few more onto the pile. The top black anime characters are not a fixed hall of fame anymore, they are a growing crew, and some of the best black anime characters are probably still a season or two out.

FAQs

Who is the most popular black anime character?

Yoruichi Shihouin from Bleach tends to top fan polls, with Afro Samurai and Killer Bee right behind her. It shifts by generation, though, and Gachiakuta's Semiu is climbing fast with newer fans.

Are there black anime main characters?

Plenty of them. Afro, Michiko, Yasuke, and Carole all headline their own shows, and series like Cannon Busters and Michiko & Hatchin are built around them rather than tucking them into the background.

Is Yoruichi black?

Yoruichi is a dark-skinned character the community has claimed and celebrated for years. Bleach never spells out real-world nationality, but her design and her huge following make her a lock for any list like this one.

Which anime has the most black characters?

Cannon Busters and Michiko & Hatchin lead the way with diverse casts, and Gachiakuta joined them in 2025 with Semiu, Jabber, and Corvus. Fire Force and Soul Eater are stacked too, thanks to creator Atsushi Ohkubo.

Who was the first black anime character?

The honest answer is that it is hard to pin down, since it depends on how you define both anime and the character, with candidates going back decades. Rather than crown a single first, it is fair to say the early ones were rare and scattered, which is part of why the current run of new faces feels so good.
