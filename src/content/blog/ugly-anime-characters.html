Anime spends most of its budget making people beautiful. Sparkling eyes, perfect hair, jawlines you could cut bread with. So when a design goes the other way on purpose, it stands out hard, and the best ugly anime characters end up sticking in your memory longer than half the pretty ones ever will.

Ugly here almost never means lazy art. It means a deliberate choice, a face built to make you feel something specific: disgust, dread, pity, or a laugh you didn't expect. The Witch of the Waste is supposed to repulse you. Big Mom's proportions are supposed to throw you off balance. The design is doing a job, and doing it well.

Below is the full roster, grouped by the kind of ugly each one is going for, so the grotesque villains, the comic goblins, and the body-horror nightmares each get their own corner.

The ugliest anime characters of all time

Ranking ugly anime characters against each other gets subjective fast, so instead of one flat list, here is the roster of the ugliest anime characters sorted by what each design is actually trying to pull off. Picking a single most ugly anime character is almost beside the point once you see how differently these faces fail on purpose, and every one of them shares the same trait: a face you do not forget.

Grotesque by design (the villains)

The Witch of the Waste earns the crown because her ugliness is the whole point of her arc. Ghibli shows you the glamour first, then strips it away to reveal the greedy, grasping thing that was underneath all along. Big Mom works the opposite way around. Charlotte Linlin was drawn ugly from day one, an Emperor of the Sea whose jumbled features and permanent leer tell you she is a threat before she says a single word. One Piece exaggerates everyone, but Oda saves his most cartoonish cruelty for the characters he wants you to fear, and Big Mom gets a double helping.

Betelgeuse is a different flavor of hideous. Re:Zero builds him as a man barely held together, hunched and jittering, chewing his own fingers, eyes rolling back whenever his devotion tips into ecstasy. Gecko Moria rounds out the villain tier with his sickly pallor, stretched grin, and horns of hair, a Thriller Bark ghoul who looks exactly as washed-up and dangerous as he turns out to be.

Comic-relief goblins

Not every ugly design wants to scare you. Some just want the laugh, and anime has a long love affair with the little gremlin who is fully convinced he is gorgeous. Ichiya from Fairy Tail is the patron saint of that club: a tiny torso topped with an enormous head, sniffing his way toward every woman in range while insisting he positively reeks of charm. He is proof that confidence plus a strong silhouette can carry a character the animators clearly had a blast drawing.

Kouji Kouda from My Hero Academia looks like someone carved a nervous face into a boulder and enrolled it in hero school, which somehow makes his shy, animal-loving heart even more lovable. Pesci over in JoJo's Golden Wind takes the other road, a grown assassin with barely any chin, a permanent look of panic, and none of the menace his job description demands. His design does half the character work for the writers, quietly telling you he is the weak link of the squad before he ever opens his mouth.

Body horror and nightmare fuel

Then there is the ugly that is built to haunt you. The Smiling Titan from Attack on Titan barely has a design at all, and that is exactly the horror of it: a lopsided, permanently grinning face with dead eyes, wearing an expression of pure joy while committing the most traumatic act in the whole series. Its blankness lands harder than any set of fangs could.

Bonolenov from Hunter x Hunter spends most of his screen time wrapped head to toe, and the reveal underneath is all gaunt angles and hollow sockets, a frame that reads like a walking instrument of pain (which, given how he fights, it basically is). Carnage Kabuto from One Punch Man goes full kaiju-insect, a roach the size of a building pumped so full of muscle that his resting form already looks wrong, well before he transforms into something worse. And Mr. Me-Mania from Perfect Blue, mentioned up top, lives in this tier too. No claws, no fangs, just a sweating human face rendered with more genuine menace than most literal demons manage.

Ugly but beloved (the designs fans adore anyway)

Here is the part most rankings gloss right over. Ugly does not mean unloved. Some of the most cherished ugly anime characters are the ones fans would take a bullet for, precisely because a memorable face paired with a good heart hits harder than any polished pretty-boy design ever could.

Gordon Agrippa

Black Clover lands Gordon on every ugliest list, and every single time, his fans show up to defend him. Pale, long-faced, and forever muttering under his breath, he looks like a horror-movie extra and behaves like the most loyal, gentle friend in the entire squad. The gap between how he reads at a glance and who he actually is became the whole joke first, and then the whole reason people love him.

Biwamaru

Dororo's blind wandering swordsman has a face that has clearly been through several wars and lost most of them. Weathered, scarred, grinning like he knows a secret you do not, Biwamaru could easily have read as a monster. Instead he is one of the show's warmest presences, a mentor figure whose gnarled features make his kindness land twice as hard when it shows up.

Melody

Hunter x Hunter gave Melody a plain, heavy-featured face and a look fans have compared to just about every burrowing animal you can name. Then the series turned her into one of its most quietly compassionate characters, a musician whose calm makes her exactly the kind of bodyguard you would want standing behind you. She is a clean little test the writers set for the audience, a design daring you to judge it a beat before the character proves you wrong.

Beauty was never the point

Run back through this whole roster and one pattern jumps out: the ugly anime characters that last are never ugly by accident. The Witch of the Waste, Big Mom, the Smiling Titan, Gordon Agrippa, every one of them was drawn with intent, whether to disgust you, unsettle you, or slip past your guard so a lovable character could ambush you with actual warmth. A gorgeous face is easy to forget because a thousand others look just like it. A great ugly design is one of a kind, and that is precisely why these are the faces you can still picture years after the credits rolled. Beauty gets the posters. Ugly gets remembered.

Frequently asked questions

Who is the ugliest anime character?

By fan consensus, the Witch of the Waste from Howl's Moving Castle takes it, especially in her drained, sagging form after her magic gets stripped away. Big Mom, Betelgeuse, and Mr. Me-Mania are the usual runners-up, and any of them would headline a weaker list.

Why are some anime characters designed to be ugly?

Almost always on purpose. An ugly design is visual shorthand. It can mark a villain as a threat, code a character as comic relief, or push a creature into full body-horror territory. A face that breaks the usual anime beauty rules grabs your attention and lodges in your memory, which is exactly what a good character designer is going for.

Which ugly anime characters do fans actually argue about?

The real fights are rarely about the obvious grotesque designs. They flare up around debated heroines and generic-looking protagonists, where half the fandom calls a design plain or forgettable and the other half pushes back hard. The honestly ugly-on-purpose characters, like Big Mom or Betelgeuse, barely spark any argument at all. Everyone agrees the design is doing its job.

Are ugly anime characters usually villains?

Often, but far from always. Grotesque design is a quick way to signal danger, so plenty of ugly faces do belong to antagonists. Just as many beloved heroes and side characters (Gordon Agrippa, Biwamaru, Melody) prove an unconventional face and a good heart go together perfectly well.

Can an ugly anime character still be popular?

Absolutely, and some of the most popular characters around are proof. Memorable beats pretty every time. A distinctive ugly design gives a character instant recognition, and when it is paired with a great personality, fans latch on even harder than they would for one more interchangeable handsome face.
