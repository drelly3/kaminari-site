Most villains want power, or revenge, or money. Goku Black wants something far more unsettling: a better world, scrubbed clean of the people he has decided are ruining it. He is not raving. He is calm, articulate, and completely certain he is the hero of his own story, which is exactly what makes him one of the most chilling antagonists Dragon Ball has ever produced.

It helps to know who is actually talking. Goku Black is Zamasu, a former apprentice god who grew to despise mortals, then used the Dragon Balls to steal Goku's body and kill him with it. So every one of these Goku Black quotes is a god's contempt for humanity, delivered through the stolen face of humanity's greatest protector. Here they are, sorted by the belief sitting underneath each one.

Goku Black quotes about justice and playing god

His idea of justice is not heroism. It is a verdict only he is qualified to hand down, and a power only he is pure enough to wield. Listen closely and you can hear the exact moment mercy got removed from the definition.

"I am justice. I am the world!"

Not that he serves justice, or fights for it. He is it. Once a person genuinely believes they personally embody justice itself, there is no higher authority left to answer to, no line they can cross, because they have quietly appointed themselves the one who draws the lines. It is the most dangerous sentence a villain can mean sincerely, and he means it.

"This must be what beauty is. This is the color of justice."

He says this while admiring his own Super Saiyan Rosé form, mid-slaughter, finding his violence genuinely gorgeous. The horror is not rage. It is serenity. He has convinced himself that the pink glow of his killing power is what righteousness is supposed to look like, and there is not a flicker of doubt anywhere in his voice.

"Evil is the great obstacle to good, and justice the sword by which good can overcome this obstacle and flourish."

Said back when he was still Zamasu, to the master who was trying to teach him better. His master argued that justice should be a scale, keeping good and evil in balance so people can learn and change. Zamasu heard "sword." That single word, sword instead of scale, is the entire villain in miniature.

Goku Black quotes about mortals and the Zero Mortals Plan

At the center of everything is what he calls the Zero Mortals Plan: the total extermination of every mortal in existence, because he decided that mortal life itself is the flaw in creation. The lines about mortals are where his god complex stops being abstract and turns into a body count.

"Evil does not inform good. It sullies it."

His flat rejection of the idea that people learn from their mistakes and grow. His master believed struggle and failure are how mortals become better. Zamasu believes they only contaminate. Strip out the possibility of growth, and every mortal becomes a permanent stain rather than a work in progress, which is precisely the belief you need to justify wiping them all out.

"That arrogant Goku sullied this sacred ground by swinging his fists at a god."

Trace his hatred back far enough and you find wounded pride, not principle. A mortal dared to fight him, dared to be strong, and his divine ego never recovered. Everything grand and cosmic he says afterward is that bruised vanity wearing the costume of a holy mission.

The plan itself curdles into one of the great ironies in the franchise, and fans never tire of pointing it out. He hates mortals so completely that he stole the body of one to carry it out, preaching about the filth of humanity from behind humanity's most beloved face, and still calling the whole thing the justice of the gods right up until the moment he was cut down.

The ideal world he thinks he's building

He does not see himself as a destroyer. He frames the genocide as construction, the necessary clearing of ground before a perfect world can be built. That reframe is how a person talks themselves all the way into the unthinkable.

"I needed someone who truly understands my will. One who carries the same justice. One who feels the same foolishness of mortals. And one who harbors the ideal world in his heart."

He says this about recruiting his own alternate-timeline self, and the truly disturbing part is that he found him. Two versions of the same god, from two different realities, arriving at the exact same conclusion and feeding each other's certainty until no doubt could survive between them. Fanaticism is always more comfortable with company.

That other self, Future Zamasu, is so drunk on his own righteousness that he cheerfully admits it does not matter to him whether anyone is even listening. Goku Black's reply is the smug thesis of the entire partnership.

"One of the many benefits of being a god."

No audience required, no approval needed, no second opinion worth hearing. Once you have crowned yourself divine, other people stop being voices to consider and become subjects to manage, or obstacles to clear. It is arrogance distilled to its purest, quietest form.

Why Goku's face makes it worse

The genius of the character is the face. Strip Goku's features away and he is a fairly standard god-complex villain you have seen before. Put them back, and every serene line about purging humanity comes out of the mouth that belongs to humanity's kindest defender, a god who loathes mortals so thoroughly that he stole the body of the mortal who best embodies everything good about them. He never once notices the contradiction. He keeps calling it the justice of the gods to his final breath.

That is the real menace in these lines, more than the power and more than the threats. It is the calm, airtight certainty of someone who has carved every last trace of compassion out of his idea of justice, and fully believes he made it better.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most iconic Goku Black quote?

"I am justice. I am the world!" is the one most fans reach for, because it captures his entire god complex in a single breath. It is not a threat or a boast so much as a genuine belief, and that sincerity is what makes it stick.

What is the Zero Mortals Plan?

It is Goku Black and Zamasu's goal to exterminate every mortal across all timelines and universes. They believe mortals are inherently flawed and violent, that the gods erred by allowing them to exist, and that erasing them is the only way to create a pure, ideal world. Everything Goku Black does traces back to this plan.

Is Goku Black the same as Zamasu?

Essentially, yes. Goku Black is Zamasu after he used the Super Dragon Balls to swap bodies with Goku. He later teams up with the Zamasu of an alternate future timeline, and the two eventually fuse. Same soul, same beliefs, different bodies.

What are the best Goku Black quotes about mortals?

His sharpest ones are the flat, philosophical lines: "evil does not inform good, it sullies it," and his scorn for the "arrogant" mortal who dared to fight a god. They matter because they show his hatred is a fixed conclusion, not an emotion he might talk himself out of, which is what makes him so relentless.

What does "I am justice, I am the world" mean?

It means he no longer sees himself as someone who fights for justice but as the literal embodiment of it. If he is justice, then whatever he decides is automatically right, and any mortal who opposes him is by definition evil. It is the ultimate self-granted permission slip for anything he wants to do.
