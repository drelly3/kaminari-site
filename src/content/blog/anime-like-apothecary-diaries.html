The anime most like The Apothecary Diaries, ranked

Here are the anime like The Apothecary Diaries that genuinely share its build, ranked from the closest match to the loosest. The Similarity Rating is our read on how near each one gets to Maomao's story specifically, not how good the show is overall, and we kept the stars to whole numbers. The build we are measuring against is simple: a clever heroine solving episodic mysteries through expertise, usually with a court or a slow-burn in the mix.

1. Raven of the Inner Palace

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Jusetsu is the Raven Consort, a woman who lives deep in the inner palace but never serves the emperor, keeping to herself and her strange arts. People come to her with problems the court cannot explain, curses, hauntings, deaths with no clear cause, and she untangles them one by one. The young emperor Koushun takes an interest in her, and a careful bond forms between them. The setting is a richly drawn, Chinese-inspired court full of secrets.

Of everything on this list, Raven of the Inner Palace mirrors The Apothecary Diaries most directly, almost beat for beat. It is the imperial-setting twin: a secluded consort living deep within an ancient, Chinese-coded rear palace solves court mysteries, poisonings, and concealed deaths brought to her by a handsome young emperor, all while the dark, claustrophobic reality of palace women hums underneath. The puzzles lean supernatural rather than pharmacological, so the expertise is spiritual instead of medical, but the rhythm of the cases, the slow-burn bond, and the mood of the inner court feel exactly like home.

2. Ameku M.D.: Doctor Detective

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Takao Ameku is a doctor at a university hospital with an almost unsettling gift for reading a body and catching the one detail everyone else missed. Patients arrive with baffling symptoms, and she treats each case like a puzzle, following the evidence to a diagnosis nobody saw coming. The series runs as a string of self-contained medical mysteries. It trades palace robes for a white coat and a modern hospital.

Ameku Takao is the direct modern reincarnation of Maomao: an eccentric, socially abrasive, hyper-analytical medical genius with an obsession for diagnostic anomalies. Where Maomao reads a poisoning, Ameku debunks seemingly impossible crimes and “supernatural” phenomena by identifying the rare disease, chemical interaction, or biological toxin hiding underneath, which is Maomao's exact method in a white coat. The court and the slow-burn are gone, but the single most distinctive thing about The Apothecary Diaries, a brilliant mind solving the unsolvable through medicine, sits front and center.

3. Gosick

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Victorique is a tiny, doll-like genius who rarely leaves a library tower in a small 1920s European country, solving crimes she never witnesses firsthand. The earnest transfer student Kazuya brings her the puzzles and does the legwork, and the two form an odd, close partnership. The mysteries start small and grow darker and more personal as a war-shadowed setting closes in. It is dripping with period atmosphere.

Gosick runs on the same detective-and-patron dynamic that powers Maomao's best arcs. Victorique de Blois is an eccentric, sharp-tongued girl confined to a grand conservatory, reading through the chaos of intricate murder cases while an earnest boy acts as her hands and feet, which mirrors Maomao's deductive brilliance and her prickly friction with the ruling nobility. Add a steady stream of case-of-the-week mysteries, a lavish period setting, and a slow-burn built on a loyal partnership, and it hits the same core beats. Swap the 1920s European backdrop for an imperial court and you are most of the way to Maomao's world.

4. The Story of Saiunkoku

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Prime Video

Synopsis: Shurei Kou is a brilliant, impoverished noblewoman who dreams of serving as a government official in a world that bars women from the role. When she is hired to enter the inner palace and turn a lazy, disinterested young emperor into a capable ruler, she seizes the chance to work her way into the court. What follows is a sweeping drama of imperial politics, reform, and quiet ambition. It is a lush, character-rich period epic.

The Story of Saiunkoku is a blueprint for the ancient imperial-palace politics that give The Apothecary Diaries its backbone. Shurei enters the court to straighten out a seemingly frivolous emperor who, exactly like Jinshi, uses a carefree facade to hide sharp political maneuvering, and she takes on corrupt ministries, court poisonings, and the grueling administrative examinations that decide a person's fate. The focus is on court statecraft rather than case-of-the-week deduction, so it is lighter on the mystery engine, but on palace intrigue and a clever woman clawing her way up a rigid court, it is a rich match.

5. Ooku: The Inner Chambers

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Netflix

Synopsis: In an alternate Edo-period Japan, a mysterious plague called the Redface Pox kills off most of the male population, leaving women to run the country and reversing the roles of an entire society. Inside the shogun's inner chambers, now a male harem, power, love, and survival become tangled and deadly. The series unfolds as a sweeping, generation-spanning historical drama. It is bold, tragic, and unlike anything else.

Ooku is the definitive exploration of gender politics and medical stakes inside a cloistered palace. A deadly epidemic reshapes the demographics of an entire era and turns the shogun's inner chambers into a dense web of concubine intrigue, inheritance struggles, and medical desperation, including a real push to develop a vaccine against the pox. That blend of claustrophobic inner-palace politics and life-or-death medicine runs straight through The Apothecary Diaries too. It is a serious, long-form historical drama rather than an episodic mystery, which keeps it mid-list, but the palace-and-plague build is a strong match. One note: it streams on Netflix, so availability depends on your region.

6. Snow White with the Red Hair

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Shirayuki is a skilled herbalist who refuses to become a prince's possession, so she flees her home and builds a new life in a neighboring kingdom. There she earns a position as a court pharmacist on the strength of her knowledge alone, winning respect one remedy at a time. Along the way she grows close to Prince Zen, and the two come to support each other as equals. It is a warm, gentle royal romance.

Snow White with the Red Hair shares Maomao's exact profession and royal partnership. Shirayuki is a knowledgeable herbalist and pharmacist who enters the royal court and earns her place as a court herbalist purely through botanical expertise, winning respect one remedy at a time while a bond with Prince Zen slowly deepens. The herbalism, the palace setting, and the capable-woman-at-court fantasy all rhyme directly. It sits mid-tier because it trades The Apothecary Diaries' cynical palace intrigue and poison plots for earnest, sweet romance, so the pull here is the competence and the warmth rather than the deduction.

7. Ascendance of a Bookworm

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Myne is reborn into a rigid, class-bound world with almost no books, so she sets out to make her own, and the simple goal spirals into a methodical climb through a society never built for someone like her. She problem-solves her way forward with knowledge from a past life, inventing and negotiating at every turn. A deep, well-drawn cast fills out the world around her. It is cozy, clever, and quietly ambitious.

Ascendance of a Bookworm is the obsessive-tradecraft sibling. Myne matches Maomao's single-minded mania almost perfectly: she cares nothing for power, status, or high-society politics, driven purely by her fixation on making books, exactly the way Maomao cares only for poisons and medicine. To chase that obsession she leans on practical chemistry, plant fibers, and forgotten historical recipes, accidentally upending the local economy and catching the eye of a calculating head priest along the way. It swaps court poisonings for papermaking, so there is no mystery engine, but the brilliant-outsider-driven-by-obsession heroine is a close match.

8. Undead Murder Farce

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: In a late-nineteenth-century world where humans live alongside the supernatural, the brilliant Aya Rindo exists as nothing but a living, disembodied head, carried around by her half-oni bodyguard Tsugaru. Together they travel Europe solving impossible cases that baffle everyone else. Aya cracks locked-room murders and monstrous mysteries through pure logic. It is stylish, gothic, and razor-sharp.

Undead Murder Farce captures The Apothecary Diaries' sharp, biting banter and its surgical approach to mystery-solving. A disembodied detective head and her half-demon companion work through intricate locked-room puzzles and murders, methodically exposing how physical alibis, poisons, and plain human malice have been dressed up to look like the occult, which is Maomao's whole talent for cutting through superstition to the real cause. The gothic, supernatural trappings and the European setting are a big departure from the inner court, which keeps it lower, but the deduction-through-expertise build and the witty back-and-forth land.

9. Ron Kamonohashi's Forbidden Deductions

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Ron is a brilliant detective forbidden from practicing after a past case went catastrophically wrong, so he works vicariously through Totomaru, an earnest and hapless young officer. Together they take on a run of bizarre murders and locked-room puzzles. Ron's deductions are dazzling and more than a little unhinged. The tone is loud, fast, and frequently comedic.

Ron Kamonohashi runs on the eccentric, socially broken detective pairing that Maomao fans will recognize. A disgraced genius investigator who simply cannot help but solve complex murders teams up with a naive police detective, and the two work through eccentric clues, forensic footprints, and hidden contraptions one case at a time. That image of a mind several steps ahead, piecing together the single clue that cracks everything open, maps cleanly onto Maomao's investigations. It plays far louder and sillier than The Apothecary Diaries, with no court or medicine to it, but for the pure deduction rush it delivers with a grin.

10. Hyouka

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Oreki would rather conserve energy than lift a finger, but his relentlessly curious classmate Chitanda keeps pulling him into small mysteries hiding in everyday school life. A misread note, an old grievance, a decades-buried secret, each one is less a crime than a human puzzle. Oreki solves them almost reluctantly, with quiet brilliance. It is a gorgeously animated slice of deduction.

Hyouka is the gold standard for the quieter side of The Apothecary Diaries, the cases that turn on a misunderstanding or a hidden kindness rather than a murder. Houtarou Oreki even shares Maomao's instinct to conserve energy and avoid troublesome attention, only to be dragged again and again into untangling intricate logical puzzles and long-buried institutional secrets through sheer deductive reasoning. The stakes are low and the setting is a modern high school rather than a palace, which is why it sits mid-list, but the pleasure of watching a sharp mind reason the truth out of tiny clues is identical.

11. Moriarty the Patriot

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: William James Moriarty is a brilliant young man who looks at a rotten, rigid aristocracy and decides the only cure is to tear it down from the shadows. Alongside his brothers, he engineers elaborate schemes against the cruel and the powerful in Victorian England. The series plays out as a run of intricate, morally tangled plots. It eventually draws a certain famous detective into its orbit.

Moriarty the Patriot delivers Victorian-era tactical deduction and forensic crime with the same chess-under-the-surface feeling as the politics of Maomao's court. William uses chemistry, ballistics, and careful social engineering to orchestrate and solve murders aimed at corrupt nobility, trading Maomao's herbal medicine for outright class revolution. The difference is the engine, since Moriarty builds crimes rather than solving them, and the tone runs darker and more tragic than The Apothecary Diaries. It sits lower for that reason, but for elaborate period scheming led by a brilliant mind, few do it better.

A few anime similar to The Apothecary Diaries that watch differently

Yona of the Dawn and My Happy Marriage both give you the lush historical court and a heroine worth rooting for, yet neither runs on case-of-the-week deduction, so the pull is the atmosphere and the romance rather than the mystery. Parallel World Pharmacy shares Maomao's love of real pharmacology, with a reborn pharmacist curing one impossible illness at a time, but it is a gentle wish-fulfillment fantasy with no court intrigue or puzzle to crack. Each one is a great watch. Just go in knowing they scratch the setting or the medicine, not the specific mystery-solving rhythm that makes Maomao tick.

FAQs

Is there an anime exactly like The Apothecary Diaries?

Not a perfect twin, but Raven of the Inner Palace comes closest: a knowing woman solving case-of-the-week mysteries inside a Chinese-inspired inner palace, with a slow-burn romance under it all. For the medical-deduction side specifically, Ameku M.D.: Doctor Detective is the sharpest match, and Gosick nails the deadpan-genius-heroine-solving-period-cases shape.

What should fans watch after The Apothecary Diaries Season 2?

If you want the easiest transition, begin at the top of this list: Raven of the Inner Palace, Ameku M.D., Gosick, The Story of Saiunkoku, or Snow White with the Red Hair. Each one carries over the clever heroine, the episodic mystery, or the lush court that made Season 2 so moreish.

When does The Apothecary Diaries Season 3 come out?

Season 3 premieres on October 2, 2026 on Crunchyroll, in a split-cour format with the first half airing in fall 2026 and the second half in spring 2027. The series' first theatrical film, featuring an original story by author Natsu Hyuga, opens in Japan on December 11, 2026.

What is the closest mystery anime to The Apothecary Diaries?

For pure deduction, Gosick is the strongest structural match, with a tiny genius solving period mysteries beside a loyal partner. Undead Murder Farce, Ron Kamonohashi's Forbidden Deductions, and Hyouka are close behind, ranging from gothic locked-room cases to loud comedy to quiet, elegant everyday mysteries.

Where can you watch anime like The Apothecary Diaries?

Most of these stream on Crunchyroll, including The Apothecary Diaries itself, while The Story of Saiunkoku sits on Prime Video and Ooku: The Inner Chambers on Netflix. Streaming rights shift by region and over time, so double-check what is available where you are before you dive in.
