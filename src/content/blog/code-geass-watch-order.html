The two timelines, explained (this is the whole trick)

Here is the one thing that makes the Code Geass watch order click: there are two separate continuities.

The original TV timeline is Lelouch of the Rebellion plus R2. Fifty episodes, one continuous story, a complete and genuinely iconic ending. Nothing else is required to enjoy it.

The movie timeline is a different beast. In 2017 and 2018, Sunrise released three films (Initiation, Transgression, and Glorification) that compress the TV run into a trilogy. They look like simple recaps, but they change several key events and adjust the ending, which spins the story off into an alternate timeline. The 2019 sequel film, Lelouch of the Re;surrection, then builds directly on that altered ending, and 2024's Rozé of the Recapture continues from there. So the movies are not remasters. They are revisions with more story attached.

The split is why people get whiplash: watch the TV finale, jump straight to Re;surrection, and the film references an outcome you never actually saw on screen. Once you know the two timelines exist, picking your path is easy.

Where to start (and do you need the recap movies?)

For a newcomer, my recommendation is clean: watch Lelouch of the Rebellion, then R2, and let that be your Code Geass. It is a complete story with one of the best endings in the medium, and you lose nothing by stopping there. If you just want the classic, those two seasons are the honest answer to what order to watch Code Geass.

Caught the bug and want more? You have two ways to continue:

  • The easy path (most people): go straight from the TV series to Re;surrection, then Rozé of the Recapture. You will hit a small continuity bump because the sequel assumes the movies' altered ending, but the gap is minor and easy to roll with.
  • The completionist path: watch the three recap films first as the official bridge, then Re;surrection, then Rozé. It gets you the exact setup the sequel was built on, at the cost of re-watching a condensed version of the story you just finished.

Neither is wrong. If you value your time, take the easy path. If you want every beat to line up perfectly, the trilogy is your bridge. So the answer to what order should I watch Code Geass really depends on how much of a completionist you are, and both routes end in the same place.

Where do Akito, the recap specials, and Rozé fit?

A few odds and ends round out the franchise, and here is the honest placement for each.

Akito the Exiled is a five-part OVA film series set in Europe during the gap between the two TV seasons, following a new cast rather than Lelouch. It is a fun, visually gorgeous side chapter that enriches the world without being essential. Chronologically it slots between the seasons, but for a first-timer it plays best saved until after you have finished the main story, so you are already invested before taking a detour.

Rozé of the Recapture brings in two new leads, the mercenary brothers Rozé and Ash, in a story set years after Re;surrection within the movie continuity. It can work as a standalone, though it lands better once you have the earlier films in your head. Reactions have been mixed, so go in curious rather than expecting a second Zero Requiem.

The recap specials (Black Rebellion and Zero Requiem) are straight summaries of the TV seasons and safe to skip unless you want a quick refresher. The Nunnally in Wonderland OVA is a non-canon comedy bonus with no real home in the timeline, so tuck it in at the very end if you watch it at all.

Is there a Code Geass chronological order?

If you want the in-universe sequence, the original timeline runs Lelouch of the Rebellion, then Akito the Exiled (set between the seasons), then R2. The movie timeline is its own separate branch: the recap trilogy, then Re;surrection, then Rozé of the Recapture. The key rule is not to weave the two together, because they resolve the same events differently and mixing them just creates contradictions. Stay inside one continuity and you keep every reveal intact, which is the smoothest way to take in Code Geass in order.

Where to watch Code Geass in order

Streaming is a little scattered, so here is the lay of the land. In most regions the two TV seasons stream on Crunchyroll, which is the main home for the core story. The four films (the recap trilogy plus Re;surrection) tend to be on Crunchyroll in the US and on Amazon Prime elsewhere, sometimes as a rental or purchase. Rozé of the Recapture is the odd one out, streaming on Disney-operated platforms worldwide and on Hulu in the US. The older OVAs, including Akito, are the hardest to find and often need a physical copy. Availability shifts by region and over time, so confirm what is live where you are before you plan a marathon.

FAQ

What order should I watch Code Geass in?

Start with Lelouch of the Rebellion, then R2, which is the complete original story. If you want to continue, watch Re;surrection and then Rozé of the Recapture, optionally watching the recap movie trilogy first as a bridge into the sequel's timeline.

Do I need to watch the recap movies before Re;surrection?

Not strictly. The recap trilogy sets up the altered ending that Re;surrection follows, so watching it first makes the continuity line up perfectly. Most viewers skip it, go straight from the TV series to Re;surrection, and simply accept a small gap, which works fine.

Where does Akito the Exiled fit in the watch order?

Akito is an optional side story set in Europe between the two TV seasons, with a separate cast. Chronologically it falls between Season 1 and R2, but it plays best saved until after you have finished the main story.

Is Rozé of the Recapture canon, and where does it go?

Rozé of the Recapture (2024) continues the movie continuity, set years after Re;surrection with new lead characters. Watch it after Re;surrection if you are following that timeline. It can stand alone, but it is richer with the earlier films behind you.

Where can I watch Code Geass?

The two TV seasons are usually on Crunchyroll, the four films are on Crunchyroll in the US and Amazon Prime elsewhere, and Rozé of the Recapture streams on Disney-operated platforms and Hulu. The older OVAs are harder to find. Availability varies by region, so check before you start.
