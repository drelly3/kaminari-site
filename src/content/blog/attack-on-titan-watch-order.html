Decoding Season 4 and the Final Chapters (the only confusing part)

The Final Season is where the labeling goes sideways, so here is the plain version. Season 4, produced by MAPPA after WIT Studio handled the first three, was released in chunks: Part 1 (16 episodes), then Part 2 (12 episodes), then the ending arrived as two feature-length specials called the Final Chapters. You will see those specials labeled all sorts of ways (Part 3 and Part 4, Special 1 and Special 2, The Final Chapters), but they are simply the last two installments that close the story.

So the whole of Season 4 reads: Part 1, then Part 2, then Final Chapters Special 1, then Final Chapters Special 2. Nothing more to it. Once you know the specials are just the finale under a fancier name, the confusion disappears.

The Last Attack or the Final Chapters specials?

In late 2024, the two Final Chapters specials were re-cut into a single theatrical film, Attack on Titan: The Last Attack, running about two and a half hours with upgraded sound and visuals. It does not change the ending or add new story, it just presents the same finale in one polished cinematic sitting.

For a first-time viewer, either option closes the series just fine. The two specials and the movie tell the same story with only tiny differences. If you can watch The Last Attack, it is the better-looking, better-sounding way to end your run, so I would point a newcomer there. If it is not available in your region, the two Final Chapters specials lose you nothing important.

Which OVAs are worth watching?

Attack on Titan has a handful of OVAs, and they are optional side stories rather than required viewing. Two stand out. No Regrets is the clear pick, a two-part look at Levi's past and how he ended up in the Survey Corps, and it plays great after Season 1. Ilse's Notebook is the other worth-your-time entry, a short, eerie piece of canon Titan lore that rewards watching early.

The rest (Distress, The Sudden Visitor, and the Lost Girls episodes focused on Annie and Mikasa) are pleasant character extras you can take or leave. None of them are essential to the plot, so slot them in after their matching season or skip them without worry.

What you can safely skip

A few entries sound important but are not part of the core experience. The recap movies (Crimson Bow and Arrow, Wings of Freedom, The Roar of Awakening, and Chronicle) compress the TV seasons into film form and add no new story, so skip them unless you want a quick refresher before a rewatch. Attack on Titan: Junior High is a non-canon chibi parody that is genuinely funny, but only after you have finished the real thing, never before. The live-action films sit completely apart from the anime and are easy to leave for the curious.

Chronological order vs release order

You may see a chronological Attack on Titan watch order floating around, and it only reshuffles a couple of OVAs to match the in-universe timeline. For a first watch, skip it and stick to release order, which is the cleanest way to watch Attack on Titan in order. The series is built on foreshadowing and slow reveals, and moving side stories earlier risks spoiling moments the main show wants to land on its own terms. Release order keeps every gut-punch intact, which is exactly how the story earns its reputation.

FAQ

In what order should I watch Attack on Titan?

Watch it in release order: Season 1, Season 2, Season 3 (Parts 1 and 2), then Season 4, which runs Part 1, Part 2, and the two Final Chapters specials. It covers the entire story from beginning to end.

How many episodes are in Attack on Titan?

The main anime has 89 numbered episodes across four seasons, with the two Final Chapters specials serving as the finale. The optional OVAs and recap films sit on top of that if you want the complete set.

Should I watch The Last Attack or the Final Chapters specials?

Both cover the same ending. The Last Attack is a 2024 theatrical re-cut of the two specials with better visuals and sound, so it is the nicer way to finish if you can access it. The specials work perfectly well otherwise.

Do I need to watch the Attack on Titan OVAs?

No, they are optional. No Regrets (Levi's backstory) and Ilse's Notebook are the two most worth your time, while the others are light character extras you can skip without missing anything from the main plot.

Where can I watch Attack on Titan?

Crunchyroll carries the full series, the Final Chapters specials, and the OVAs in most regions, with the show also on Netflix in some countries. Availability varies by region, so check what is live where you are before you start.
