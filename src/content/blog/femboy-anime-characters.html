Femboy anime characters are the boys who wear the dress better than anyone and dare you to say something about it. Astolfo, Felix, and Haku are the names fans think of first, and the roster of confident, feminine-presenting boys behind them is deeper than you would expect. Some crossdress for fun. Others just happen to look softer than the girls around them, and every one of them owns it completely.

The appeal has never really been about the outfits. These feminine male anime characters win fans over with pure confidence and personality, the charm of a character who knows exactly who he is and refuses to apologize for it. A few are comic relief. Others are some of the most beloved characters in their entire shows.

The anime femboy has gone from niche gag to genuine fan favorite, and this list celebrates the best of them across decades of shows. Expect icons, deep cuts, and at least one pick who could out-dress you without trying. Fair warning: we play favorites, and Astolfo was always going to be here.

Top 15 femboy anime characters

Our anime femboy list spans classic icons and modern scene-stealers, all canonically male, all effortlessly feminine. You will find the most popular femboys sitting next to a few deeper cuts, since the best anime femboys are not always the loudest ones. It goes past the usual top 10 femboys rundowns into a fuller roster of popular femboy anime characters. These are the famous anime femboys fans keep coming back to, in no particular order.

Astolfo

Anime: Fate/Apocrypha

Astolfo is the undisputed face of the whole category, the cheerful pink-haired Rider whose signature look has launched a thousand cosplays. He is brave, kind, a little airheaded, and completely unbothered by anyone's opinion of how he dresses, which is exactly why fans adore him. He charges into battle with the same energy he brings to everything: pure delight. If there is a mascot for anime femboys, it is unquestionably him.

Felix Argyle

Anime: Re:Zero

Felix, also called Ferris, is a knight and one of the most gifted healers in Re:Zero, and Subaru absolutely mistakes him for a girl at first. He wears his cat-themed outfits with total confidence and teases everyone in a sing-song voice, but underneath is a fiercely loyal fighter devoted to the people he serves. That blend of playful and deadly is his whole charm. Plenty of fans call him the single most popular pick in the scene.

Haku

Anime: Naruto

Haku is the original for a lot of longtime fans, the gentle, elegant boy Naruto flat-out assumes is a girl the first time they meet. His kindness and his tragic loyalty to Zabuza make his short arc one of the most memorable stretches in early Naruto. He fights with a sad grace that stuck with an entire generation. Few characters have made this much impact in so little screen time.

Ruka Urushibara

Anime: Steins;Gate

Ruka is the soft-spoken, impossibly graceful shrine kid of Steins;Gate, so delicate that the cast openly jokes he is more feminine than any of the actual girls. His gentle personality and quiet devotion to his friends make him genuinely lovable, and the show treats his story with real tenderness. He is kindness in human form. Ruka proves the softest character in the room is often the sweetest.

Chihiro Fujisaki

Anime: Danganronpa

Chihiro is the shy, brilliant programmer of Danganronpa, and one of the most quietly heartbreaking characters in the whole series. Behind the timid smile is a seriously gifted mind and a story about strength that hits far harder than you expect. Chihiro's kindness toward everyone, even inside a killing game, is unforgettable. This is a character who stays with you long after the credits roll.

Saika Totsuka

Anime: My Teen Romantic Comedy SNAFU

Totsuka is the tennis club sweetheart of Oregairu, so effortlessly cute that even the show's famously cynical narrator loses his composure around him. He is earnest, warm, and completely unaware of the effect he has on everyone, which only makes him more endearing. Every scene he wanders into gets a little brighter. Pure, uncomplicated sweetness in a cast full of overthinkers.

Nagisa Shiota

Anime: Assassination Classroom

Nagisa is the small, blue-haired student with an androgynous look and a startlingly sharp instinct for assassination. Gentle and unassuming on the surface, he turns out to be one of the most naturally talented killers in his class, which is a delicious contrast. His famous undercover moment leans all the way into the look. Soft-spoken and secretly lethal is a combination fans never get tired of.

Gasper Vladi

Anime: High School DxD

Gasper is the anxious half-vampire who is more comfortable hiding in a cardboard box than holding a conversation, and he crossdresses simply because he finds it comforting. His shyness and constant second-guessing make him the show's designated cinnamon roll, the one everyone wants to protect. Under the nerves is a genuinely frightening power he is terrified of using. Awkward and endearing goes a long way.

Hideri Kanzaki

Anime: Blend S

Hideri works at the same café as the rest of the Blend S cast while chasing a dream of becoming a glamorous idol, and he commits to the bit with everything he has. He is vain, dramatic, and hilariously confident about his own cuteness, which the show plays for pure comedy. His farm-kid backstory only makes the idol fantasy funnier. He is a scene-stealer who absolutely knows it.

Kuranosuke Koibuchi

Anime: Princess Jellyfish

Kuranosuke is the fashion-obsessed son of a politician who dresses femininely partly to rebel and partly because he simply looks incredible doing it. In Princess Jellyfish he becomes the confident spark that pushes a group of shy women out of their shells. His styling eye and total fearlessness make him magnetic. He treats self-expression like the superpower it clearly is.

Ritsu Sohma

Anime: Fruits Basket

Ritsu is the endlessly apologetic member of the Sohma family who finds real calm in wearing kimono and feminine clothes. His arc in Fruits Basket, about crippling anxiety and slowly learning to accept himself, is handled with the same warmth the whole show is known for. He is gentle, sincere, and always trying his best. Watching him grow into a little confidence is genuinely moving.

Gowther

Anime: The Seven Deadly Sins

Gowther is the androgynous doll turned Sin of The Seven Deadly Sins, built with delicate features and a flat, curious way of speaking. His blank exterior hides a character slowly learning what emotions even are, which gives him a strange and compelling arc. He is powerful, odd, and quietly heartfelt. Few characters manage to be this unsettling and this sympathetic at the same time.

Hideyoshi Kinoshita

Anime: Baka and Test

Hideyoshi is so convincingly feminine that Baka and Test turns it into a running joke, with the cast insisting he belongs to a whole “third gender” of his own. He is level-headed, kind, and endlessly patient with a school full of complete idiots. His twin sister is basically identical, which the show milks for everything it is worth. He is the sensible center of a very silly comedy.

Titus Alexius

Anime: Magi

Titus is the refined, feminine-featured magician of Magi, introduced with an elegance that sets him apart from the rougher cast around him. His arc about what it truly means to be alive gives him surprising emotional weight for such a delicate-looking character. He is graceful, clever, and braver than his soft appearance suggests. A gentle character who grows into real heroism.

Rui Ninomiya

Anime: Gatchaman Crowds

Rui is the brilliant young activist of Gatchaman Crowds who presents femininely and quietly runs a world-changing app from behind the scenes. Idealistic and sharp, he genuinely believes technology can make people kinder, and the show takes his vision seriously. His confidence in his own style is just part of the package. A thoughtful character wrapped in a striking look.

Honorable mentions

Fifteen femboy anime characters barely scratch a category this deep. Envy from Fullmetal Alchemist blurs every gender line on purpose as a shapeshifting homunculus. Souta Takanashi of Working!! never hides his love of cute things, and Kazuki brought serious style to an older generation over in GetBackers. Add the growing wave of pretty, long-lashed mages filling modern isekai, and this list could easily double.

Why these boys keep winning fans over

The through-line across every pick is confidence. The best femboy anime characters are memorable not because of what they wear but because of how completely they own it, the charm, the self-assurance, the refusal to fit anyone else's box. Astolfo and Felix opened the door, and the category only keeps growing as anime gets more comfortable letting boys be soft. Self-expression, it turns out, is extremely likable.

FAQs

Who is the most famous femboy in anime?

Astolfo from the Fate series is the near-unanimous answer, the character most fans picture the second the topic comes up. Felix Argyle from Re:Zero is the other name that constantly turns up right beside him.

Who was the first femboy in anime?

It is hard to pin down a definitive first, since androgynous and crossdressing characters go back decades in manga and anime. Haku from Naruto is the example many younger fans point to as their introduction, but the archetype is much older than any single character.

Is there an anime with a femboy main character?

A few, yes. If you want an anime with femboy main character billing, Princess Jellyfish gives Kuranosuke a huge role and Blue Period features Yuka, while plenty of isekai leads lean androgynous. Full lead roles are rarer than scene-stealers, but the number keeps growing.

What is a femboy in anime?

It usually refers to a canonically male character with a feminine appearance or style, whether by design, fashion choice, or crossdressing. Fans use the term affectionately to celebrate characters who break the usual gender mold.

Why are femboy anime characters so popular?

Mostly confidence. Femboy anime characters tend to be written with an unapologetic sense of self that fans find genuinely magnetic, and the contrast between a soft look and a strong personality makes for memorable characters. The playful challenge to gender norms is a big part of the appeal too.
