Inuyasha runs 193 episodes, with 36 of them being filler, roughly 20% of the total run. The filler includes a notorious 13-episode marathon, several multi-episode arcs, and a handful of scattered standalones that pause the Naraku hunt for side stories.

But here's what makes the Inuyasha filler list different from most: the show's episodic travel structure means filler often feels less jarring than you'd expect. The group is always wandering feudal Japan, encountering one-off demons and troubled villages. A filler episode about a random demon encounter doesn't break the format the way a disconnected side arc would in a more plot-driven series. Some of these detours are genuinely fun, and a few are fan favorites.

With Rumiko Takahashi's MAO anime debuting in April 2026 (directed by Teruo Sato, the same director behind Yashahime), fans are circling back to her most iconic work. Whether you're starting fresh or returning after dropping the show mid-run, here's the complete guide covering the original 167 episodes and the 26-episode Final Act in one place.

The Complete Inuyasha Filler List

Inuyasha's filler falls into three types: standalone one-off episodes scattered throughout the run, multi-episode filler arcs that stall the Naraku hunt, and mixed episodes that blend anime-original scenes with manga content. The tables below list every filler episode with a verdict, organized by the stretch of the series where they appear. If an episode isn't in the tables, it's canon and safe to watch.

The Foundation (Episodes 1-58)

Zero filler in this stretch. The first 58 episodes are canon or mixed canon, covering Kagome's arrival in feudal Japan, the Shikon Jewel shattering, the full group assembling (Shippo, Miroku, Sango), Naraku's emergence, Sesshomaru's arc, and Kikyo's return. Watch all of them.

Secretly Peak: Inuyasha Filler Worth Protecting

Not all Inuyasha filler deserves to be skipped. The tables above give you the clean route, but these three picks are worth circling back to.

The Modern-Day Episodes

Inuyasha's best filler happens when the group visits Kagome's modern-day world. The culture clash between feudal-era characters and contemporary Japan is consistently funny, and these episodes give the cast room to breathe outside the Naraku hunt. The school festival episodes in particular are fan favorites. Scattered across the filler blocks, they're worth cherry-picking even if you skip everything else around them.

The Band of Seven Arc (Episodes 103-126)

Not filler at all, but it deserves the spotlight because it's the stretch that proves Inuyasha can deliver when the plot tightens. Seven distinct villains with their own fighting styles, a genuine threat to the main cast, and a pace that never lets up. Fans who dropped the show during the Episodes 89-101 marathon never saw this arc, and that's the real cost of that 13-episode wall.

The Final Act (Episodes 168-193)

26 episodes, zero filler, pure payoff. Every unresolved thread gets its conclusion. The pacing is tight (sometimes too tight), but the emotional weight of the finale earns every beat. Fans who gave up on Inuyasha during the original run's rougher stretches owe themselves these 26 episodes.

Hot Take: The 13-Episode Marathon Ruined Inuyasha's Reputation

Any Inuyasha filler list shows a 20% rate, which is modest by long-running shonen standards.

The real issue isn't volume. It's placement. Scattering standalone filler episodes between canon arcs is fine, and Inuyasha's wandering structure handles it better than most. But stacking 13 filler episodes at Episodes 89-101 creates a wall that kills momentum right when the story should be escalating. Most fans who dropped Inuyasha quit during that stretch, not because the episodes were terrible, but because thirteen in a row is too many for any series to survive.

Strip out that one block and Inuyasha's filler barely registers. The show's reputation took the hit for one scheduling decision.

Through the Well and Out the Other Side

The Inuyasha filler list runs about 36 episodes across 193, and most of it concentrates in a few skippable blocks. Skip 89-101 without hesitation. Pass on 75-79, 127-130, and 133-140. The scattered standalones barely register.

And when you clear the filler, what's left is a genuinely rewarding journey: a strong first half, the Band of Seven as one of the best villain arcs in long-running shonen, and a 26-episode Final Act that delivers the ending the original series couldn't. Use the verdicts above, skip the blocks that don't earn your time, and come back for the Final Act.

FAQ

How much filler does Inuyasha have?

About 36 episodes out of 193 total, or roughly 20%. Most of the filler is concentrated in the original 167-episode run. The 26-episode Final Act has zero filler.

Does Inuyasha: The Final Act have filler?

No. All 26 episodes of the Final Act are pure manga canon. The series returned in 2009 specifically to adapt the manga's conclusion, and every episode advances the main story without any padding or standalone detours.

Can I skip Inuyasha Episodes 89-101?

Yes. The 13-episode filler block at Episodes 89-101 is the longest in the series and the safest to skip entirely. None of these episodes affect the main story, and picking up at Episode 102 puts you right back into canon content without missing a beat.

What order should I watch the Inuyasha movies?

Movie 1 after Episode 54, Movie 2 after Episode 95, Movie 3 after Episode 136, and Movie 4 after Episode 167. All four are standalone stories that don't affect the main plot. Movie 3 is the strongest; Movie 4 is skippable.

Is Yashahime worth watching after Inuyasha?

Yashahime follows the daughters of Inuyasha and Sesshomaru across 48 episodes (2 seasons). It's divisive among fans: some enjoy seeing the next generation, others find it doesn't match the original's quality. It connects to the Inuyasha story but is entirely optional for the core experience.
