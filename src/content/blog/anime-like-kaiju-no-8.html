The anime most like Kaiju No. 8, ranked

Here are the anime like Kaiju No. 8 that genuinely share its build, ranked from the closest match to the loosest. The Similarity Rating is our read on how near each one gets to Kaiju No. 8 specifically, not how good the show is overall, and we kept the stars to whole numbers. The build we are measuring against is simple: a professional force battling monsters, led by an underdog who gains power, with spectacle and camaraderie.

1. Attack on Titan

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Humanity huddles behind enormous walls while towering, man-eating Titans roam the land outside, until the day the walls are breached and Eren Yeager swears to wipe the Titans out. He joins the military's elite Survey Corps, throwing himself into a desperate war, only to discover that the truth behind the Titans is far darker than anyone imagined. He also gains the power to transform into a Titan himself. It builds to one of the most talked-about stories in anime.

On premise, Attack on Titan is the nearest thing to Kaiju No. 8 here. An underdog signs up with a specialized military corps to wipe out the enormous, flesh-eating titans terrorizing humanity, only to learn he can turn into a rogue, sentient titan himself. From there he has to prove his humanity to fearful military tribunals while fighting intelligent enemy shifters, and that hero-who-is-also-the-monster bind is Kafka's exact situation turned grim. It is far darker and more tragic than Kaiju No. 8's hopeful, crowd-pleasing tone, so brace for heartbreak, but the monsters-versus-military build is unmistakable.

2. World Trigger

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: When gates to another world open and monsters called Neighbors pour through, a defense organization named Border holds the line using special weapons called Triggers. Osamu Mikumo is a low-ranked agent with weak stats who makes up for it with grit and strategy, fighting to climb an explicit ranking system alongside his team. The series runs on tactical team battles rather than raw power. It rewards patience with some of the sharpest fights in the genre.

World Trigger is the closest match here for the tactical-defense-organization side of Kaiju No. 8. Border is an urban military defense force run on the same procedural details Kafka lives with: siren alarms, evacuation bunkers, formal squad rank-wars, and specialized issued Triggers built to repel the interdimensional Neighbor monsters pouring into modern Japanese cities. Osamu's low-ranked grind up that explicit hierarchy rhymes with Kafka's underdog climb. It is calmer and more strategic than Kaiju No. 8's big spectacle, leaning on team tactics over raw power, but the professional-force-against-monster-invaders machinery is almost a one-to-one fit.

3. My Hero Academia

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Izuku Midoriya is born without a superpower in a world where almost everyone has one, until the greatest hero alive sees his heart and passes him a legacy. He enrolls at an elite hero academy, trains relentlessly, and throws himself into a professional system built to protect the public from threats. His class becomes a found family, and every battle pushes him to grow. It is bright, earnest, and endlessly rewatchable.

My Hero Academia mirrors the institutional entrance-exam and cadet structure that frames Kafka's story. Kafka is the late-blooming underdog who feels like he missed his shot, much like Deku, and he idolizes the top captain Mina Ashiro the way Deku worships All Might, then has to earn his place in rigorous practical combat trials beside young prodigies like Kikoru Shinomiya, who plays a Bakugo-style gifted rival. The elite-academy-into-professional-force pipeline, the found-family class, and the crowd-pleasing spectacle all echo Kaiju No. 8's shape. It is brighter and more traditionally shonen, but the underdog-earns-his-place core is a strong fit. Our My Hero Academia filler list flags the episodes you can skip.

4. Tokyo Ghoul

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Ken Kaneki is an ordinary college student until a date goes horribly wrong and leaves him half-turned into a ghoul, one of the flesh-eating creatures that secretly live among humans. Forced to hide what he has become, he scrapes together a double life while the ghoul-hunting agents of the CCG close in. The series is a dark, violent descent into identity and survival. Kaneki's slow unraveling is one of anime's most harrowing arcs.

Tokyo Ghoul lands close to Kafka's predicament for one big reason: an ordinary guy is involuntarily turned into a half-monster and has to hide the condition from everyone around him, exactly the secret Kafka carries into the Defense Force. The parallel runs deeper than the premise, too. The ghoul-hunting CCG harvests organs from the creatures it kills to forge personalized weapons called Quinque, which works almost identically to the way the Defense Force builds its Numbers armaments from the remains of identified kaiju. It is darker and more introspective than Kaiju No. 8, with no found-family squad, but the hide-your-monster-half tension is a direct hit. Our Tokyo Ghoul filler list sorts out which episodes to skip.

5. Chainsaw Man

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Denji is a broke teenager who hunts devils to pay off a dead man's debt, until a betrayal fuses him with his chainsaw-devil companion and turns him into something new. Recruited by a government devil-hunting agency, he throws himself into a brutal world while chasing the simplest dreams. The series lurches between crude comedy, shocking violence, and raw emotion. The animation is gorgeous and unhinged.

Chainsaw Man shares Kaiju No. 8's gritty, blue-collar view of monster work. Denji starts out as a debt-ridden kid disposing of devil corpses for pocket change before a betrayal fuses him with a devil and turns him into a living weapon, at which point he gets drafted into a public-safety devil-extermination bureau under strict squad superiors. That arc from grubby cleanup labor to a regimented professional force, with a hero who becomes the very thing he hunts, lines up with Kafka's leap into the Defense Force. It is far darker and more nihilistic than Kaiju No. 8's optimism, so the tones diverge, but the agency-plus-monster-within build is a clear fit.

6. God Eater

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: In a ruined future, monstrous beings called Aragami have devoured most of the world, and humanity's last defense is an elite force of God Eaters wielding living weapons that can harm them. Lenka joins their ranks, throwing himself into a desperate war to hold back extinction. The series is stylish, brutal, and loaded with high-stakes battles. Survival is never guaranteed.

God Eater is set in a wrecked, post-apocalyptic world where massive, morphing beasts called Aragami have torn civilization apart, and an elite corps holds the last defensive hub. Its soldiers wield biological weapons called God Arcs forged directly from Aragami cells, which is the same trick the Defense Force pulls when it builds gear from kaiju remains, and the story follows a rookie who carries a rare hybrid variant of that weapon. The giant-monster battles and the organization behind them land squarely for Kaiju No. 8 fans. It is grimmer and more straightforward, without the underdog charm or the secret-identity hook, which keeps it mid-list, but the specialized-unit-versus-giant-monsters core fits.

7. Kabaneri of the Iron Fortress

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Prime Video

Synopsis: In a steam-powered, isolationist Japan, humanity huddles inside fortified stations while the Kabane, armored undead monsters, overrun the land. Ikoma, a young engineer, survives a Kabane attack half-transformed and becomes a Kabaneri, strong enough to fight back without fully turning. He joins survivors fleeing aboard an armored train, battling to protect the evacuation routes. The action is fast, stylish, and desperate.

Kabaneri gives you a protagonist cut from similar cloth to Kafka. Ikoma is an ordinary steam mechanic who gets infected and becomes a hybrid human-monster, and he turns his close, hands-on knowledge of the creatures' anatomy and weak points into armor-piercing weapons that defend the fortified evacuation lines. That blend of an engineer's practical mind, a half-monster body, and a desperate defensive campaign maps neatly onto the Defense Force and Kafka's own transformation. It is grimmer and set in a retro-future rather than a modern city, but the infected-hero-who-fights-the-thing-he-became build is a strong match.

8. SSSS.Gridman

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Yuta wakes up with amnesia and starts hearing a voice from an old computer calling itself Gridman, right as a giant kaiju levels part of his city. Merging with Gridman, he transforms into a towering hero to battle the monsters that keep appearing, while a mystery about his town slowly unravels. The series is a loving, stylish homage to classic tokusatsu. The kaiju battles are a spectacle.

SSSS.Gridman is a loving throwback to classic Japanese tokusatsu and kaiju cinema, and that is exactly where its overlap with Kaiju No. 8 lives. Giant monsters abruptly materialize in suburban Tokyo, triggering public panic, collapsing buildings, and defensive strikes from a high-tech guardian hero. The city-leveling clashes and the monster-of-the-week rhythm scratch the same giant-monster-spectacle itch Kaiju No. 8 runs on. It is a solo tokusatsu hero rather than a defense force, and a strange mystery pulls the story somewhere weirder, which keeps it mid-list, but the kaiju spectacle is a direct hit.

9. Parasyte: The Maxim

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll, HIDIVE

Synopsis: Shinichi is a normal high schooler until an alien parasite tries to burrow into his brain and ends up stuck in his right hand instead. As more parasites quietly infest humanity, devouring people and wearing their faces, Shinichi and his strange new companion are caught in a war for survival. The series asks hard questions about what makes a monster, and what makes a human. It is tense, gory, and surprisingly thoughtful.

Parasyte digs into the tension of an alien creature taking over a human body, which puts its hero in a bind close to Kafka's. Shinichi ends up fused with a parasite and has to throw himself into high-speed, close-quarters brawls against the cannibalistic infiltrators hiding in plain sight, all while keeping his own transformation secret from his family and the police. The monsters-among-us threat and the body-horror change echo the kaiju-within hook at the heart of Kaiju No. 8. There is no defense force or big spectacle here, so it sits lower, but the human-and-monster-in-one-body pressure is a strong partial match.

10. Fire Force

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll, Prime Video

Synopsis: In a world plagued by spontaneous human combustion, special fire brigades fight Infernals, people who ignite into rampaging monsters. Shinra, a boy with flame-powered feet and a nervous grin, joins Company 8 to lay the Infernals to rest and uncover the truth behind the phenomenon. The squad becomes a tight-knit family, and the mythology runs deeper than it first appears. The action is flashy and stylish.

Fire Force runs on specialized municipal defense brigades, the Special Fire Force, who scramble to urban disaster zones the moment the alarms sound to put down Infernals, the people who spontaneously combust into rampaging demons. That alarm-to-deployment rhythm, the ranked companies, and the found-family squad all feel a lot like Kafka's unit. The difference is that Fire Force leans harder into shonen tournament beats and flashy fantasy fire powers, and its hero is not a monster himself. Still, the brigade-versus-urban-monsters build is a solid match.

11. Neon Genesis Evangelion

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Prime Video

Synopsis: In a post-apocalyptic future, a traumatized teenager named Shinji is pressed by his cold father to pilot a towering bio-mechanical giant, the Evangelion, and defend a fortress city from monstrous beings called Angels. As the battles escalate, so does the psychological toll and the sinister truth behind the organization he serves. It is as much a study of trauma and identity as a monster-war series. It is one of the most influential anime ever made.

So much of the modern kaiju-defense playbook traces back to Evangelion, now overseen by Studio Khara. The emergency siren alarms, the tactical command rooms coordinating a response, the artillery bombardments that bounce uselessly off giant invaders, and the biological war machines grown from the enemy's own DNA all show up here first, and Kaiju No. 8 clearly inherited that vocabulary. The difference is tone: Evangelion is a bleak psychological study rather than an underdog crowd-pleaser, so the heart is heavier and stranger. The genre-defining monster-defense machinery, though, is a clear ancestor. One note: it streams on Netflix, so availability depends on your region.

A couple that get recommended but watch differently

Search around for anime similar to Kaiju No. 8 and a couple of modern monster shows come up that share a piece of the premise but not the defense-force-crew-and-secret-identity core. They are worth your time. We just want the match to feel honest.

Solo Leveling has the modern world of gates, licensed hunters, and monsters spilling into cities, plus an underdog in Sung Jinwoo who claws his way up from the bottom, but it runs on a solo leveling power fantasy rather than Kaiju No. 8's tight-knit professional squad, and there is no hide-your-monster-identity angle to it. Godzilla Singular Point delivers plenty of kaiju, yet it is a slow, cerebral sci-fi puzzle about the science behind the monsters rather than a spectacle-driven shonen. Both are excellent in their own right. Just go in knowing they trade Kaiju No. 8's crew camaraderie and underdog heart for something more solitary or more heady.

FAQs

Is there an anime exactly like Kaiju No. 8?

Not a perfect twin, but two come very close. Attack on Titan matches the premise, a specialized military force battling giant monsters with a hero who can become one himself, while World Trigger matches the defense-force procedure almost beat for beat, from the ranked squads to the alarms and the gear built to repel invaders. Each nails a core piece of what makes Kaiju No. 8 work.

What is the best anime like Kaiju No. 8?

For the closest overall match, Attack on Titan leads on premise and World Trigger leads on the organization and tactics. If you want the hide-your-monster-identity hook specifically, Tokyo Ghoul is the one to beat, with Chainsaw Man close behind for a darker take on becoming the thing you hunt.

What anime has a defense force fighting monsters like Kaiju No. 8?

The strongest picks are World Trigger, with its Border defense agency, God Eater, with its elite God Eaters, Fire Force, with its ranked fire companies, and Attack on Titan, with the Survey Corps. Each centers a professional organization built to fight monstrous threats.

What anime has a protagonist who becomes a monster like Kaiju No. 8?

Several match Kafka's dilemma. Attack on Titan gives Eren the power to turn into a Titan, Tokyo Ghoul leaves Kaneki a half-ghoul, Chainsaw Man fuses Denji with a devil, and Parasyte binds Shinichi to an alien creature. All center a hero tied to the power of the thing they fight.

Where can you watch anime like Kaiju No. 8?

Most of these stream on Crunchyroll, including Kaiju No. 8 itself, while Kabaneri of the Iron Fortress sits on Prime Video and Neon Genesis Evangelion on Netflix. Streaming rights shift by region and over time, so double-check what is available where you are before you settle in.
