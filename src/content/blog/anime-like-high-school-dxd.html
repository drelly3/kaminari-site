The anime most like High School DxD, ranked

Here are the anime like High School DxD that genuinely share its build, ranked from the closest match to the loosest. The Similarity Rating is our read on how near each one gets to High School DxD specifically, not how good the show is overall, and we kept the stars to whole numbers. The build we are measuring against is simple: a supernatural battle harem with an underdog who gains power, faction mythology, a school setting, and a strong streak of fanservice comedy.

1. The Testament of Sister New Devil

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll, Prime Video

Synopsis: Basara Toujou's father remarries and brings home two new step-sisters, who turn out to be a young demon lord, Mio, and a succubus, Maria. A botched contract leaves Basara bound to protect Mio as demons and heroes alike come hunting her. What follows is a run of supernatural fights wrapped around a lot of harem comedy. It is easily the most fanservice-forward pick on this list, so go in knowing it earns its mature rating.

On build, this is the single closest match to High School DxD: a supernatural world of devils and heroes, a guy who stumbles into power and a group of girls who fight beside him, and battles stacked on top of ecchi comedy. The devil mythology and the protect-the-girl stakes line up especially neatly with Issei and Rias. It leans harder into the fanservice than DxD does, which is the one place it tips over for some viewers.

2. Trinity Seven

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: When a strange phenomenon destroys his town, Arata Kasuga enrolls at a secret magic academy to get it back, armed with a powerful grimoire. There he meets the Trinity Seven, the academy's strongest mage girls, and gets swept into a world of magic, demons, and escalating threats. The battles share space with a steady stream of harem comedy and fanservice. It is one of the titles fans name most often alongside DxD.

The build overlaps almost completely: a school setting, a supernatural power system, a guy who grows strong through a special gift, and a harem of capable girls fighting at his side, all laced with ecchi comedy. Arata's grimoire and growth mirror Issei's Sacred Gear journey, and the academy-plus-demons structure feels like DxD's cousin. The action is a step below DxD's best fights, but the formula is the same.

3. Strike the Blood

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Kojou Akatsuki is an ordinary high schooler who has become the fourth primogenitor, the most powerful vampire in existence, without quite meaning to. A young swordswoman, Yukina, is sent to watch him, and the two end up defending a city full of warring supernatural factions. The series is long-running, action-heavy, and dotted with harem dynamics and fanservice. It leans more on the fights than the comedy.

The connection is the underdog-turned-powerhouse surrounded by factions and allies: a regular guy suddenly holding world-class power, a mythology of vampires and demons and attack mages, and a growing group of girls who fight beside him. That is Issei's arc with the ecchi turned down a notch and the action turned up. For DxD fans who came mostly for the supernatural battles, this is a strong pick.

4. Rosario + Vampire

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Tsukune Aono accidentally enrolls at Youkai Academy, a school for monsters where being human is a death sentence if anyone finds out. He survives thanks to Moka, a sweet vampire girl with a far fiercer second personality, and slowly gathers a harem of monster girls. Each arc brings a new threat and a new fight. It is a classic of the supernatural-harem genre, with plenty of fanservice built in.

Rosario + Vampire is textbook DxD a few years early: a human boy dropped into a supernatural school, a mythology of monsters instead of devils, a harem of powerful girls, and monster-of-the-week battles. The school-for-the-supernatural setup and the protective bond with the lead girl map almost directly onto the Occult Research Club. It is older and rougher around the edges, but the DNA is unmistakable.

5. Sekirei

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Apple TV

Synopsis: Minato, a hapless young man, becomes an Ashikabi when a superpowered girl named Musubi crashes into his life, and he is pulled into a secret battle among beings called Sekirei. As more Sekirei bond to him, he gains a growing group of powerful partners and a target on his back. The series mixes tournament-style fights with harem comedy and heavy fanservice. The plot gets surprisingly dramatic as it goes.

The build match is the battle harem itself: a powerless everyman who becomes the anchor for a team of superpowered girls, all fighting through an organized supernatural contest that reads a lot like DxD's rating games. The bond mechanic, the escalating fights, and the fanservice comedy are squarely in DxD territory. It runs on its own mythology rather than devils and angels, which is the main point of difference.

6. Date A Live

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Shido Itsuka learns that the catastrophic spacequakes devastating the world are caused by Spirits, powerful girls from another dimension, and that the only way to neutralize them peacefully is to make them fall in love. So he sets out to win over each Spirit before the military can destroy them. The series blends romance, comedy, and genuinely strong supernatural action. The fanservice is present but lighter than most of this list.

The overlap is the supernatural harem with world-ending stakes: a fairly ordinary guy gathering a group of immensely powerful girls, a mythology of otherworldly beings, and action that ramps up season over season. Shido's save-them-by-connecting-with-them approach rhymes with how Issei pulls his peerage together. It trades heavy fanservice for more plot and character work, which is why it tends to rank higher on quality.

7. Freezing

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: In a world invaded by interdimensional beings called Nova, humanity fights back with Pandoras, girls enhanced for combat, each paired with a male partner called a Limiter. Kazuya becomes the Limiter to Satellizer, a fearsome and standoffish Pandora, and the two are thrown into brutal battles and academy politics. The series is violent and very heavy on fanservice. It pulls no punches on either front.

The build connection is the combat-partner harem: a guy bonded to powerful fighting girls, an academy that trains them, and a faction war against supernatural invaders. The partner mechanic and the escalating battles sit right next to DxD's structure, with the ecchi dialed up and the tone made darker. For viewers who want the battle-harem build with a harder edge, it fits.

8. Campione!

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll, Prime Video

Synopsis: Godou Kusanagi accidentally kills a god and becomes a Campione, a godslayer destined to fight rogue deities and other Campiones. As his legend grows, so does the group of powerful women drawn into his orbit, including a proud knight and a magic-wielding shrine maiden. The series mixes mythological battles with harem comedy and fanservice. The fights lean on gods and legends from around the world.

At its core is the underdog-gains-godlike-power framework wrapped in a harem: a regular guy who stumbles into world-class strength, a mythology-heavy supernatural world, and a growing cast of capable women at his side. Godou reluctantly becoming a feared power while the girls gather around him echoes Issei's rise. It is lighter on fanservice than the top picks and heavier on mythology, which shifts it down a tier on pure similarity.

9. To Love-Ru

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: HIDIVE, Prime Video

Synopsis: Rito Yuuki's life turns upside down when Lala, an alien princess, teleports into his home and decides to marry him, dragging in her family, her gadgets, and a parade of chaos. What follows is a long-running harem comedy packed with misunderstandings and fanservice. The later Darkness series adds more plot and more characters. There is very little action, and the focus is squarely on the comedy.

What it shares is the harem comedy and the fanservice rather than the battles: an ordinary, well-meaning guy surrounded by a huge cast of girls, with chaos around every corner. It matches DxD's sense of humor and its willingness to go all-in on fanservice, but it swaps the supernatural faction war for sci-fi romance hijinks. If you loved DxD's comedy more than its fights, this is the pick.

10. Heaven's Lost Property

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Tomoki just wants a peaceful life, until a winged girl named Ikaros, an Angeloid, falls from the sky and declares herself his servant. More Angeloids follow, and his quiet town becomes the stage for comedy, fanservice, and the occasional surprisingly dark or emotional turn. The series swings wildly between crude gags and genuine pathos. It is stranger and more ambitious than it first looks.

The connection is the supernatural harem comedy: an ordinary guy who ends up the center of a group of powerful, otherworldly girls, with heavenly mythology in the background and fanservice in the foreground. The angel framing even flips DxD's devil angle. It is more comedy than action, with only occasional fights, so it sits a tier down on the battle side of the build.

11. Is This a Zombie?

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Ayumu is murdered and brought back as a zombie by a necromancer, then accidentally steals the powers of a magical girl, forcing him to fight in her place, costume and all. Add a vampire ninja and a chainsaw-wielding warrior, and his afterlife becomes a crowded supernatural comedy. The series is fast, funny, and self-aware. The fanservice is present but takes a back seat to the jokes.

The build match is the supernatural harem with a powered-up everyman at its center: a guy thrust into a world of magic and monsters, surrounded by a group of strong, strange girls, mixing comedy with real fights. Ayumu reluctantly gaining powers and a crew of female allies is a looser version of Issei's setup. The tone is more parody than straight ecchi-action, which is why it lands mid-list.

12. Highschool of the Dead

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll, Apple TV

Synopsis: When a sudden plague turns people into the walking dead, a group of high schoolers fights to survive, led by the hot-headed Takashi and the kendo ace Saeko. The series is a fast, gory zombie-apocalypse thriller with a strong streak of fanservice running through the carnage. It never got a proper ending, which fans still lament. It is tense, bloody, and unmistakably mature.

What it shares is the fanservice-heavy action in a high-school frame rather than the harem or the mythology: a group of students fighting through a supernatural catastrophe, with the ecchi laid over the top. It matches DxD's willingness to pair bold fanservice with real stakes, but there is no devil mythology, harem romance, or power-progression spine, so the match is about tone more than structure.

A few picks that get recommended but watch differently

Search around for anime like High School DxD and a handful of shows come up that share the harem or the fanservice but not the supernatural-battle build. They are worth your time. We just want the match to feel honest.

The Quintessential Quintuplets is a beloved harem, but it is a grounded, heartfelt romance with no supernatural powers, factions, or fights, so the overlap is only the harem shape. Prison School delivers outrageous, raunchy comedy, yet it is a grounded school farce rather than a supernatural action series. Monster Musume nails the monster-girl harem and the fanservice, but it is a slice-of-life comedy about cohabitation rather than a battle series. Each is a good watch. Just go in knowing they scratch the harem or the comedy, not DxD's supernatural-action build.

FAQs

Is there an anime exactly like High School DxD?

Not a perfect twin, but two come very close. The Testament of Sister New Devil shares the devil mythology, the bonded battle harem, and the heavy fanservice, while Trinity Seven matches the magic-academy setting, the power gift, and the harem of capable girls. Both are the ones fans reach for first.

What to watch after High School DxD

If you want the smoothest transition, begin at the top of this list: The Testament of Sister New Devil, Trinity Seven, Strike the Blood, or Date A Live. Each one carries over the supernatural battles, the harem, or the fanservice comedy that made High School DxD so moreish.

Is High School DxD getting a Season 5?

As of now, no release date has been confirmed. The anime has been dormant since its fourth season, DxD Hero, in 2018, and while a fifth season is widely expected given the franchise's popularity and unadapted source material, nothing official has been scheduled.

What is the closest anime to High School DxD with less fanservice?

Date A Live and Strike the Blood are the picks for the supernatural-battle-harem build with the ecchi turned down, leaning more on plot, mythology, and action. Both are easier recommendations if the fanservice was never the main draw for you.

Where can you watch anime like High School DxD?

Most of these stream on Crunchyroll or HIDIVE, including High School DxD itself. Streaming rights for older titles shift by region and over time, so double-check what is available where you are before you settle in.
