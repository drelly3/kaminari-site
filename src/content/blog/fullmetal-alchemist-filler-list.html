Fullmetal Alchemist is one of the few anime franchises adapted twice, and both versions have almost no filler. FMA 2003 has 3 filler episodes out of 51 (6%). Brotherhood has 1 filler episode out of 64 (2%). Across 115 total episodes, you could count every skippable entry on one hand.

The list can get confusing because roughly half of FMA 2003 diverges from Hiromu Arakawa's manga entirely, and those episodes keep getting mislabeled as filler. They're not. They're the actual ending of a different story, told because the manga wasn't finished when the 2003 anime aired. Calling them filler is like calling an alternate ending a deleted scene.

With Arakawa's new anime Yomi no Tsugai premiering on Netflix in July 2026 (animated by Bones Film, the same studio family behind both FMA series), fans are circling back to her original masterpiece. Whether you're starting fresh or revisiting the Elric brothers, here's the only FMA filler list that covers both anime in one place with honest verdicts.

Fullmetal Alchemist filler barely exists because both adaptations had strong source material to draw from, and neither studio padded the runtime with throwaway content. The 2003 version ran ahead of the manga and wrote its own ending rather than inserting filler arcs. Brotherhood waited until the manga was nearly complete before starting, which is why it adapts all 27 volumes with almost nothing added. For context, Naruto sits at 41% filler and Bleach at 45%. FMA's combined rate across both series is under 4%.

One critical note: FMA 2003's anime-original episodes (roughly Episodes 29 onward) are labeled "Anime Canon" in the tables below, not filler. They tell a complete alternate storyline with its own ending. Treating them as filler misrepresents what the 2003 series actually is.

Secretly Peak: FMA Episodes Worth Defending

Any Fullmetal Alchemist filler list can give you the four episodes to skip. These three picks are the ones worth protecting.

FMA 2003's Tucker Arc (Episodes 6-7)

Brotherhood handles Tucker in a single devastating episode. FMA 2003 gives the story two, letting you spend more time with Nina and Alexander before the betrayal. The extra runtime makes the horror land harder because you've had the space to care. Fans who only know Brotherhood's version have experienced the shock but not the full weight of the grief that follows. The 2003 pacing is the reason this moment became one of anime's most infamous scenes.

Brotherhood Episode 19: "Death of the Undying"

The Lust confrontation. Mustang's flames, Hawkeye's breakdown, and one of the most visually stunning action sequences in the entire franchise. Often cited as the moment Brotherhood shifts from "great adaptation" to "all-time classic." The animation quality spikes, the stakes become genuinely lethal, and the series announces that it's done holding back.

FMA 2003's Homunculi

The 2003 version gives the Homunculi a completely different origin: each one is born from a failed human transmutation attempt. Sloth is the Elric brothers' mother. Wrath is Izumi's son. These connections make the Homunculi personal in a way Brotherhood's "creations of Father" structure doesn't attempt. Neither version is wrong. Brotherhood's Homunculi serve a grander plot. FMA 2003's Homunculi serve the characters. Both work, and experiencing both sharpens your appreciation for what each version prioritizes.

Hot Take: FMA 2003 Isn't the Wrong Version, It's the Other Answer

The community consensus has crowned Brotherhood as the definitive Fullmetal Alchemist, and that's fair. Brotherhood adapts the complete manga, delivers a more satisfying final arc, and earns its permanent seat near the top of every "best anime" ranking.

But framing FMA 2003 as the "lesser" version, or treating its anime-original content as glorified FMA filler, sells it short. The 2003 series handles the early arcs with more patience and emotional weight. Its Homunculi carry personal connections to the Elric brothers that Brotherhood never attempts. Its tone is darker and more willing to sit in grief rather than push toward resolution. And its ending, while divisive, asks a genuinely different philosophical question than Brotherhood's triumphant finale.

The real equivalent exchange isn't choosing one version over the other. It's watching both and letting each one illuminate what the other couldn't reach.

The Law of Equivalent Exchange (Applied to Your Watch List)

The Fullmetal Alchemist filler list is one of the shortest in anime: 3 episodes in FMA 2003, 1 in Brotherhood, and nothing else worth skipping. But the number was never the point of this guide.

The point was cutting through the confusion that surrounds this franchise. FMA 2003's anime-original content is not filler. Brotherhood's Episode 1 is not a waste of your time. And the question of which version to watch has never been "either/or" for anyone willing to invest in both. Use the verdicts above, skip the four episodes that genuinely don't matter, and give both Elric brothers' journeys the time they earned.

FAQ

Does Fullmetal Alchemist have filler?

Very little. FMA 2003 has 3 filler episodes (4, 10, and 37) for a 6% filler rate. Brotherhood has 1 filler episode (27) for a 2% rate. The combined filler across both series is under 4%, making FMA one of the leanest major anime franchises.

Which Fullmetal Alchemist filler episodes can I skip?

In FMA 2003: Episodes 4 ("A Forger's Love"), 10 ("The Phantom Thief"), and 37 ("The Flame Alchemist, The Bachelor Lieutenant & The Mystery of Warehouse 13"). In Brotherhood: Episode 27 ("Interlude Party"). All other episodes are canon or anime canon and contribute to their respective stories.

Should I watch FMA 2003 or Brotherhood first?

Brotherhood is the complete manga adaptation and works perfectly as a standalone watch. However, starting with FMA 2003's early episodes (1-28) gives you a stronger, more patient introduction to the characters and world. Many fans recommend FMA 2003 first for emotional grounding, then Brotherhood for the full story. Either order works.

Is FMA 2003's anime-original content filler?

No. Episodes 29-51 of FMA 2003 tell a complete alternate storyline with its own ending, created because the manga wasn't finished when the anime aired. They are anime canon, not filler. Conqueror of Shamballa (2005) serves as the conclusion to this storyline.

Is Sacred Star of Milos worth watching?

Only if you want a standalone FMA adventure. The film doesn't connect to Brotherhood's main plot and introduces characters who never appear in the series. It's fine as a side story but adds nothing essential.
