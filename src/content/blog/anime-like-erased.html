The anime most like Erased, ranked

Here are the anime like Erased that genuinely share its build, ranked from the closest match to the loosest. The Similarity Rating is our read on how near each one gets to Erased specifically, not how good the show is overall, and we kept the stars to whole numbers. The build we are measuring against is simple: a grounded psychological thriller driven by a time-travel or do-over mechanic, where the hero races to prevent a death and unmask a hidden threat, with high suspense and a bittersweet, protective heart.

1. Summer Time Rendering

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Disney+, Hulu

Synopsis: Shinpei returns to his small island hometown for the funeral of his childhood friend, who drowned saving a girl, only to learn her death may not have been an accident. As a deadly mystery unfolds, he gains the ability to loop back in time whenever he dies, using it to protect the people he loves. The island setting is cozy and sinister at once. It is a tense, twist-filled thriller that rarely lets up.

Summer Time Rendering is the undisputed closest match to Erased. It is a murder mystery set in an isolated seaside town, where the protagonist's time resets let him gather forensic clues one loop at a time, all while he races against an encroaching threat that keeps targeting the people he loves. The reset-to-investigate-and-save structure, the small-community setting, and the constant ticking-clock dread are Erased's exact shape. The threat is supernatural rather than a lone human killer, which is the one real difference, but nothing else lines up this precisely.

2. Higurashi: When They Cry

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: A boy moves to a sleepy rural village and settles into easy days with new friends, until a cycle of paranoia, murder, and madness reveals itself beneath the calm. The story loops through variations of the same cursed summer, each one peeling back more of the mystery. The sweetness curdles into horror without warning. It is a cult classic of the mystery-horror genre.

Higurashi matches Erased on the setting, the tight group of elementary and middle-school peers, and the dark psychological terror of slowly realizing a killer is operating among ordinary townspeople. The friends relive the same cursed stretch of time, using the resets to work out what they have to change to break the tragedy and save everyone, which is Satoru's investigation turned into a puzzle box. It is bloodier and more openly horror than Erased, which is the main difference in tone, but the rural-town dread and the loop-to-solve-and-save build are a remarkably close fit.

3. Tokyo Revengers

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Takemichi, a down-on-his-luck adult, learns that his middle-school girlfriend has been killed by a violent gang, then discovers he can leap twelve years into the past. He throws himself into changing the events that led to her death, infiltrating the gang as a kid to rewrite a brutal future. Each trip back raises the stakes. It blends delinquent drama with real time-travel tension.

Tokyo Revengers is the closest mechanical twin to Erased's time-leap. An adult failure leaps a decade back into his younger, school-age self to undo a tragic death, then jumps between past actions and the present to watch the timeline ripple, which is Satoru's Revival scaled up to years instead of minutes. Like Erased, it is an ordinary person throwing himself into his own past to save people he could not protect. The setting is gang violence rather than a child-killer mystery, but the time-leap-to-prevent-a-death mechanic and the bittersweet stakes line up closely.

4. Steins;Gate

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: A self-styled mad scientist named Okabe discovers that his makeshift microwave can send text messages into the past, and his tinkering spirals into something far bigger than he bargained for. When a close friend's death becomes the price of his meddling, he is forced to leap through time again and again to undo it. The first half is a slow burn, the second is relentless. It is widely considered one of the greatest anime ever made.

Steins;Gate matches Erased's sheer psychological agony and its desperate, repeated timeline alterations to prevent a loved one's murder. Okabe, like Satoru, is forced to reach into the past again and again, each attempt costing him more, in a race to reach the one outcome where the person he loves survives. It trades the snowy rural school for an urban tech-thriller lab, so the texture is different, but the grounded time-travel and the frantic save-them-this-time core are pure Erased.

5. Orange

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Naho receives a letter from her twenty-six-year-old self, begging her to change the past and save a new transfer student, Kakeru, from a future tragedy. As she pieces together what is coming, she and her friends try to reach a boy quietly drowning in grief. The story is gentle, aching, and deeply kind. It is a quiet gut-punch about regret and the people we wish we had saved.

Orange captures the quiet, melancholic emotional core of Erased. Using foreknowledge of the future, Naho reaches out to save a socially isolated peer from trauma and a looming death, and the whole story runs on the same ache of hindsight and the desperate wish to protect someone vulnerable before it is too late. It is a grounded drama rather than a thriller, so the suspense is gentler, but the reach-back-to-save-a-lonely-kid build is a direct match for Erased's heart.

6. The Promised Neverland

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll, Hulu

Synopsis: Three gifted children at a seemingly idyllic orphanage discover the horrifying truth about their home and the caretaker they trusted, and begin a tense game of wits to escape before they are next. Every plan is a gamble, every glance from the adults a threat. The first season is a masterclass in suspense. It is a nerve-shredding thriller about children outwitting a predator.

The Promised Neverland puts children in a psychological chess match, trying to outwit an adult predator who holds all the institutional power and authority over them, which is the beating heart of Erased's main arc. Like Satoru shielding Kayo and his classmates from a trusted adult, the kids here have to read, plan, and outmaneuver a smiling threat to survive, with vulnerable young lives on the line and a clock running down. Its first season is the match, and there is no time-travel, which keeps it off the top, but the protect-the-children-from-a-predator thriller build is a strong fit.

7. Re:Zero

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Subaru is yanked into a fantasy world and quickly discovers his one power: when he dies, he returns to an earlier checkpoint, keeping his memories. He uses it, at enormous psychological cost, to claw his way toward outcomes where the people he loves survive. The deaths are brutal and the trauma is real. It is a punishing, gripping deconstruction of the wish-fulfillment isekai.

Re:Zero shares Erased's checkpoint-rewind mechanic and the psychological trauma of repeatedly failing to stop impending murders. Subaru's Return by Death drops him to an earlier save point every time he dies, forcing him to relive horror until he finds the path where the people he loves survive, which is Satoru's Revival turned merciless. It is set in high fantasy rather than a grounded town, which keeps it a notch lower, but the rewind-to-prevent-deaths engine and the toll it takes are a close match.

8. Monster

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Prime Video

Synopsis: A brilliant surgeon saves the life of a young boy, only to watch that boy grow into a charming, remorseless killer, and spends years hunting him across Europe to undo the evil he allowed to live. It is a slow, sprawling, deeply human thriller with one of anime's great villains. Every character feels real. It is a towering achievement in suspense.

Monster is the closest match if you strip the sci-fi away entirely: a dark, realistic, grounded detective chase hunting down a terrifying human killer across bleak European environments. Like Satoru, Dr. Tenma is an ordinary man consumed by the need to stop a murderer and protect the vulnerable, trading spectacle for dread, character, and suspense. There is no time-travel or do-over here, which is why it sits lower on an Erased list specifically, but for the realistic-thriller-and-hidden-killer half of the show, nothing does it better.

9. ID:Invaded

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: A disgraced detective dives into the shattered minds of serial killers, reconstructed as surreal dreamscapes, to piece together clues and catch them in the real world. Each case is a puzzle built from a murderer's psyche. The structure is inventive and the mysteries are tightly plotted. It is a cerebral sci-fi detective thriller.

ID:Invaded is a high-concept mystery thriller focused on entering the psychological minds of active serial killers to stop their murders before time runs out, which lines up with Erased's investigative, catch-the-killer side. The pressure of getting inside a criminal's logic to prevent the next death echoes Satoru's attempts to outthink the man hurting his classmates. There is no time-travel and the vibe is colder and more procedural, which keeps it lower, but the race-to-stop-a-murderer build is a solid fit.

10. Another

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: A transfer student joins a class haunted by a decades-old curse that brings death to those connected to it, and must uncover the mystery's rules before the body count climbs. A quiet girl with an eyepatch holds the key. The atmosphere is dread-soaked and the deaths are grisly. It is a stylish mystery-horror with a puzzle at its center.

Another is a dark school mystery about a cursed class and an isolated girl at the center of it, which rhymes with Erased's grim classroom and its focus on a vulnerable classmate. The tension comes from piecing together the hidden rules before the body count climbs. It leans much more into supernatural curse horror than grounded detective problem-solving, and there is no time-travel, which keeps it lower, but the solve-the-deadly-mystery build is a fair match.

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Two mysterious teenagers carry out a series of calculated attacks on Tokyo, taunting the authorities with riddles while a weary detective races to understand their motive. Beneath the thriller is a melancholy story about damaged kids the world left behind. The tone is cool, tense, and deeply sad. It is a grounded suspense drama with a haunting heart.

Terror in Resonance is a moody, somber cat-and-mouse thriller with real psychological weight, carrying the same grounded tension and tragic undercurrent as Erased. The difference is perspective: you follow two teenage bomb-planting instigators rather than a time-rewinding savior, so there is no do-over mechanic and no save-the-children stakes. It is further from the center than most of this list, but the grounded-thriller-with-a-sad-heart build still makes it a genuine match.

A couple that get recommended but watch differently

Search around for anime similar to Erased and two names come up a lot that share a piece of it but not the full grounded-thriller-with-a-do-over build. They are worth your time. We just want the match to feel honest.

Death Note is the go-to psychological thriller recommendation, and the suspense is unbeatable, but it is a cat-and-mouse game built around a brilliant villain chasing power, the inverse of Satoru's race to protect the vulnerable, with no time-travel in sight. If you want the tension without the save-the-innocent heart, it still delivers, and our Death Note filler list shows it is almost pure story. The Girl Who Leapt Through Time nails the time-travel and the bittersweet ache, but it is a gentle coming-of-age romance rather than a thriller, so the suspense and the stakes are much softer. Both are worth watching, just for different reasons than Erased.

FAQs

Is there an anime exactly like Erased?

Not a perfect twin, but two come very close. Summer Time Rendering is the undisputed closest, with a protagonist looping back to solve a seaside murder and save his loved ones, and Higurashi matches the small-town setting, the peer group, and the resets used to break a cycle of killings. Each captures the core of what makes Erased tick.

What is the best anime like Erased?

Summer Time Rendering is the best overall match, with its reset-to-solve-a-murder structure in an isolated town. Steins;Gate is right beside it for grounded time-travel agony, while Monster is the top pick if you want Erased's realistic hidden-killer thriller with no supernatural element at all.

What anime has time travel used to save someone, like Erased?

Summer Time Rendering, Steins;Gate, Tokyo Revengers, Orange, and Re:Zero all hand their heroes a time-leap or a death loop and send them racing to prevent the deaths of people they care about, which is the exact mechanic that drives Erased.

Is Erased getting a season 2?

No. The anime is a complete twelve-episode adaptation of Kei Sanbe's finished manga, and the story is fully self-contained, so a second season is not planned. A separate live-action film and a live-action series have adapted the same story, but there is no continuation of the anime.

Where can you watch anime like Erased?

Most of these stream on Crunchyroll, including Erased itself, with Summer Time Rendering on Disney+ and Hulu and Monster on Prime Video. Streaming rights shift by region and over time, so double-check what is available where you are before you settle in.
