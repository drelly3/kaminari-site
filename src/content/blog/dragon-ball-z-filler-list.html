Look, you could just watch Dragon Ball Z Kai, which condenses the whole story into 167 filler-free, HD-remastered episodes covering everything from Raditz crashing into Kame House to Kid Buu turning into space dust. But something about that 291-episode original keeps pulling people back, probably because the version of DBZ that taught an entire generation to sit through three episodes of a Spirit Bomb charging is the version that actually lives in your memory. If you're here, you already made your choice, and now you just want to know which episodes to cut so the rewatch doesn't drag.

Good news: there are only 38 pure filler episodes in this Dragon Ball Z filler list, putting the series at roughly 13% filler. Lower than Naruto Shippuden, lower than Bleach, lower than Fairy Tail. The number sounds clean until you remember how the show actually felt to watch, but we'll get to that. For now: every filler episode broken down by saga, real skip-or-watch verdicts, the Kai situation explained, and the reason one chunk of "skippable" filler might contain the most important character work in the franchise.

The accurate DBZ filler count is 38 pure filler episodes, though you'll occasionally see 29 from trackers that classify saga-padding episodes differently (particularly the Frieza fight extensions and Buu Saga stalling). We count 38 as the true number and keep the 8 mixed canon/filler episodes (11, 17-18, 20, 44, 204, 229, 251, 287) in their own lane since they contain real plot beats wrapped in original anime material. Don't skip those.

Worth noting: that 13% stat only captures standalone filler episodes, not the padding Toei baked into canon episodes by stretching two or three manga pages across an entire episode's runtime. The Dragon Ball Z filler list handles the 38 skippable episodes; the in-canon padding is the reason Kai was made.

Dragon Ball Z Skippable Episodes: The Quick-Skip List

Here's the full DBZ filler list in one clean block, every episode you can skip without losing a single plot thread:

Episodes 9-10, 12-16, 39-43, 45, 100, 102, 108-117, 124-125, 170-171, 174, 195-199, 202-203, 288

Before you go scorched-earth on all of those, three quick asterisks. Episode 125 (the one where Goku and Piccolo try to get driver's licenses) is widely considered the single best filler episode in the franchise and one of the funniest things DBZ ever produced. Episodes 195-199 (the Other World Tournament) are five episodes of genuinely well-animated fights that introduced a fan-favorite character the community still talks about. And episodes 9-10 plus 12-15 (Gohan's wilderness training) built the Gohan-Piccolo relationship that the rest of the entire series emotionally cashes in on. Those are worth keeping.

The 8 mixed canon/filler episodes are NOT included here. Watch them.

Secretly Peak: The Filler Worth Protecting

Some Dragon Ball Z fillers carry more weight than their classification would suggest, and these are the episodes fans will fight you about skipping.

Episode 125: Goku's Ordeal

If there's a single filler episode every Dragon Ball fan agrees on, it's this one. Chi-Chi decides Goku and Piccolo need to earn their driver's licenses because she's tired of walking to the grocery store, and what follows is one of the funniest episodes the franchise has ever produced.

Piccolo shows up to driving school wearing Goku's civilian clothes (tight jeans, yellow shirt, backwards baseball cap) looking completely bewildered. Goku panics behind the wheel of a car despite being able to fly faster than jets. The whole thing plays like a slice-of-life comedy starring two planet-busters who have been domesticated against their will, and it captures the original Dragon Ball's goofy humor better than almost anything else in Z.

Other World Tournament (Episodes 195-199)

Five episodes of afterlife tournament action that gave us Pikkon, an anime-original fighter who went blow-for-blow with Super Saiyan Goku and earned a permanent spot in the fan community's heart despite never appearing in a single panel of the manga.

The fights are well-animated, the pacing is tight (rare for this show), and Goku's non-canon Super Saiyan Kaioken remains one of the most visually striking moments in the series. As a breather between the emotional wreckage of Cell and the slow buildup to Buu, these five episodes do exactly what a palate cleanser should.

Gohan's Wilderness Training (Episodes 9-10, 12-15)

The filler that quietly built the most important relationship in the franchise. Where the manga covers Gohan's year under Piccolo in a handful of panels, the anime turns it into six episodes of a four-year-old surviving alone in the wilderness while Piccolo watches from the shadows, gradually cracking under his own reluctant protectiveness. More on why this matters in the hot take below.

The Fake Skip: Garlic Jr. Arc (Episodes 108-117)

Ten episodes, zero consequences, a villain who never comes back. The longest skippable block in DBZ and the safest cut on this list. The arc has been mildly reappraised for letting Gohan, Piccolo, and Krillin run the show without Goku, but the villain feels like a retread of his own movie, the Spice Boys are forgettable, and the whole thing wraps up the same way the Dead Zone did. Worth a look if you loved that film, easy to pass on otherwise.

The Real Hot Take: DBZ's Filler Did More for Gohan Than the Manga Did

Here's the thing about Gohan's arc that nobody talks about when they talk about filler: the manga handed him a destiny (defeat Cell, surpass Goku, become the strongest fighter alive) without building the emotional foundation to make any of it land. His training under Piccolo, the relationship that becomes the beating heart of the entire Dragon Ball franchise, gets a few manga panels. Piccolo takes Gohan, time passes, Gohan is ready, move on.

The anime's filler episodes built everything the manga left out. Six episodes of a kid surviving alone in the wild, earning the respect of the Namekian warrior who kidnapped him, while Piccolo watches from the shadows with his cold exterior breaking down one episode at a time. Those episodes are the reason Piccolo's death against Nappa doesn't just work as a plot moment but absolutely destroys you, and the franchise eventually proved how central that bond is when Dragon Ball Super: Super Hero turned their relationship into an entire movie, decades after filler episodes laid the groundwork.

The Spirit Bomb Was Always Worth the Wait

Dragon Ball Z has 38 filler episodes across 291 total, and most are easy skips. Fake Namek delays the best saga, Garlic Jr. goes nowhere, and the Buu Saga padding tests your patience. But woven between those are filler episodes that became iconic for good reason: a driving test, a dead man's tournament, and a child learning to survive alone. Kai removes all of it in one clean cut. This Dragon Ball Z filler list lets you pick what stays and what goes on your own terms.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many filler episodes does Dragon Ball Z have?

38 pure filler episodes out of 291 total, putting the filler rate at roughly 13%. An additional 8 mixed canon/filler episodes contain both original and manga-adapted content but shouldn't be skipped.

What is the difference between DBZ and DBZ Kai?

Kai removes all filler and trims the in-canon padding, condensing 291 episodes down to 167 with re-recorded voice work, updated visuals, and a different music score (the Bruce Faulconer soundtrack from the US version of DBZ is not in Kai). Same story, dramatically tighter pacing.

Is the Garlic Jr. arc worth watching?

For most viewers, no. Ten episodes of standalone filler with no lasting consequences and a villain who never returns. If you enjoyed the Dead Zone movie the arc expands on that storyline, but strictly for story purposes you can jump from 107 to 118 without missing anything.

Is Dragon Ball Daima filler?

No. Daima (20 episodes, Oct 2024 to Feb 2025) is fully canon, written by Akira Toriyama before his passing, and set between the end of Dragon Ball Z and the beginning of Dragon Ball Super.

What is the best filler episode in Dragon Ball Z?

Episode 125, "Goku's Ordeal," and it's not close. Goku and Piccolo attempt to earn their driver's licenses at Chi-Chi's insistence, and the result is the funniest 20 minutes the franchise has ever produced.
