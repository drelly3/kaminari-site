Fate looks impossible, but the essential story is only five entries, and everything else is optional. For newcomers, the cleanest path is Fate/stay night: Unlimited Blade Works, then the three Heaven's Feel films, then Fate/Zero last (which keeps the big reveals intact). If you would rather start with the most action-packed hook, the popular alternative is Fate/Zero first, then Unlimited Blade Works, then Heaven's Feel. Both are great. The rest of the franchise is separate and skippable.

At its heart, Fate is about the Holy Grail War, a secret battle in which mages summon legendary heroes from history and myth as their Servants and fight for a wish-granting Grail. Fate has a reputation as the single most confusing Fate watch order in anime, and honestly, it earns it: three parallel routes, a prequel, alternate timelines, a giant mobile game, and two decades of spin-offs. Here is the calm truth, though. Ignore about ninety percent of it.

The core Fuyuki storyline is a complete, self-contained saga of roughly 26 hours, and once you know how to watch Fate in order at that core, the rest is just bonus material you can explore or ignore. Below I lay out the essential run, settle the famous Fate/Zero debate, explain the three routes, and slot in the optional extras.

Fate/Zero first or Unlimited Blade Works first?

Here is the debate that has split the fandom for years, and the honest answer to it. Fate/Zero is a prequel set ten years before Fate/stay night, covering the Fourth Holy Grail War. Watching it first sounds logical, and plenty of people do exactly that, because Zero is gorgeous, action-packed, and an incredible hook.

The catch is that Fate/Zero was written for an audience that had already finished Fate/stay night, so it treats several major reveals from the later routes as ordinary background detail. Start with Zero and you learn a few of Heaven's Feel's biggest secrets before the main story has earned them. It helps to know what Zero even is, too: it follows Kiritsugu Emiya, the adoptive father the main hero Shirou barely remembers, through a bleaker, more adult war of clashing ideals, which is a big part of why it hooks people so hard.

So here is my recommendation: If you want every twist to land the way it was designed to, watch Unlimited Blade Works and Heaven's Feel first, then finish with Fate/Zero. If you care more about the strongest possible opening and do not mind mild spoilers, Zero first is a completely valid and popular choice. Neither camp is wrong, and both orders end with the same five entries, so pick the priority that matters more to you. Beyond that one call, there are no other real forks in the entire Fate watch order.

The three routes, explained

The thing to understand about Fate/stay night is that it began as a visual novel with three routes, each retelling the same Fifth Holy Grail War from a different angle with a different heroine and escalating reveals. Knowing this makes the whole Fate anime order click into place.

  • Fate (the Saber route) is the original path. Its only real anime adaptation is the 2006 Studio Deen series, widely considered the weakest of the bunch.
  • Unlimited Blade Works (the Rin and Archer route) digs into Shirou's ideals and his brutal rivalry with Archer. The 2014 ufotable series is the definitive version and your ideal entry point.
  • Heaven's Feel (the Sakura route) is the darkest path, and it upends much of what the earlier routes set up. The ufotable film trilogy is stunning.

The routes are meant to be experienced roughly in that progression, which is why the Fate/stay night watch order flows Unlimited Blade Works into Heaven's Feel so well. Two quick warnings: skip the 2010 Deen film version of Unlimited Blade Works, which crams a full route into 100 minutes and loses everything, and treat the 2006 series as an optional curiosity rather than a starting point. Unlimited Blade Works was built to be watched without it.

The optional spin-offs and alternate timelines

Once you finish the core, a huge amount of extra Fate awaits, and none of it is required. Most of these are set in entirely separate timelines with their own casts and Grail Wars.

  • Fate/Apocrypha (2017) is a separate Great Holy Grail War with a brand-new cast. Self-contained and easy to enjoy on its own.
  • Fate/strange Fake (2026) is the newest addition, an A-1 Pictures series adapting yet another separate Grail War. It is excellent, but it draws on franchise knowledge, so watch it after at least Fate/Zero and Unlimited Blade Works rather than as a starting point.
  • Fate/Grand Order adaptations (Babylonia, Camelot, Solomon, and more) expand the massive mobile game's story and assume you already know the basics, so save them for after the core routes and watch them in their own release order.
  • Lord El-Melloi II's Case Files (2019) is a mystery spin-off following a Fate/Zero character, best watched after Zero.
  • Fate/kaleid liner Prisma Illya is a magical-girl spin-off in its own lighthearted timeline, Today's Menu for the Emiya Family is a cozy cooking series best enjoyed after Heaven's Feel as emotional recovery, and Carnival Phantasm is a non-canon comedy crossover that only lands once you already know the whole cast.

You genuinely do not need any of these to feel like you have watched Fate. They are desserts, not the meal.

Where to watch Fate

Streaming is scattered, which adds to the confusion. Crunchyroll is the main home for the core, carrying Fate/Zero, Unlimited Blade Works, and many of the spin-offs. The Heaven's Feel films and some older titles tend to live on other services like Hulu depending on region, and Netflix carries Fate/Apocrypha and select Fate/Grand Order content in some countries. Availability shifts by region and over time, so confirm what is live where you are before you build your plan.

FAQ

What order should I watch Fate in?

For the core story, watch Fate/stay night: Unlimited Blade Works, then the three Heaven's Feel films, then Fate/Zero. If you prefer the most action-packed starting point and do not mind mild spoilers, watch Fate/Zero first instead. Both cover the same essential five entries.

Should I watch Fate/Zero first?

You can, but it is a trade-off. Fate/Zero is a prequel that spoils some later reveals, so watching it last preserves the mysteries. Many fans still start with it because it is the most accessible and exciting entry, so it comes down to whether unspoiled twists or the strongest hook matters more to you.

Do I need to watch the 2006 Fate/stay night?

No. The 2006 Studio Deen series adapts the original Fate route but is regarded as the weakest adaptation, and Unlimited Blade Works was made to be watched without it. Save it as an optional curiosity for completionists only.

What are the three Fate routes?

Fate/stay night is built on three routes that retell the same Holy Grail War: Fate (the Saber route), Unlimited Blade Works (the Rin and Archer route), and Heaven's Feel (the Sakura route). Each escalates the reveals, which is why they are meant to be watched in that progression.

Where can I watch Fate?

Crunchyroll carries the core, including Fate/Zero and Unlimited Blade Works, while the Heaven's Feel films and some titles appear on services like Hulu, and Netflix has Fate/Apocrypha and some Fate/Grand Order content. Availability varies by region, so check what is streaming near you before you start.
