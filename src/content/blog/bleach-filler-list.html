Nearly half of Bleach doesn't exist. Not in the manga, anyway. Out of 366 episodes in the original anime, 163 are filler. That's a 45% filler rate, which makes Bleach the worst offender in the entire Big Three. Naruto Shippuden clocks in around 41%. One Piece barely hits 10%. Bleach almost doubles One Piece's entire filler count *in percentage alone*.

And the placement makes it worse. Bleach doesn't dump its filler at the end of a saga where you can see it coming. It drops 46 straight episodes of spiritual vampires right after the Soul Society arc, the best stretch of the entire show. You ride the highest high Bleach ever gives you, and then you crash into the Bount Arc for four months. Studio Pierrot did this to buy Tite Kubo time to stay ahead in the manga, and it worked for production. It didn't work for fans.

This bleach filler list gives you the full breakdown. Every filler arc and standalone episode, sorted and judged honestly. A copy-paste skip list for the people who just want numbers. A section on the filler that's actually worth your time. And a look at the episodes the trackers get wrong, because blindly copying animefillerlist.com will steer you wrong in at least three places.

Bleach Filler Episodes Worth Watching

Most of Bleach's filler earns its skip label. But a handful of arcs and episodes are worth breaking your skip list for, and they're worth it because they do something the rest of the filler never attempts: they feel like they belong.

Zanpakuto Unknown Tales / Rebellion Arc (Episodes 230-265)

The single best filler arc in Bleach, and a genuine contender for best filler arc in shonen anime, period. A rogue zanpakuto spirit named Muramasa severs the bond between every Soul Reaper and their blade, giving the zanpakuto physical forms, unique personalities, and the will to fight back against their masters.

What makes it work is specificity. Senbonzakura manifests as a masked swordsman with his own pride and combat philosophy. Zabimaru splits into two bickering halves who can't agree on anything. Hyorinmaru walks the battlefield as an ice warrior who has forgotten his wielder entirely. Every zanpakuto design became iconic to the fanbase. People forget these characters originated in filler. That's how well they fit Kubo's world.

Muramasa himself carries the arc. His motivation isn't conquest; it's abandonment. He was sealed away by his master and the pain of that rejection drives everything he does. For a filler villain, that's a shocking amount of emotional depth. He has more internal life than some canon antagonists.

The fanbase has argued for years that the Rebellion deserves canon status, and TYBW may have quietly agreed. A Byakuya flashback in the modern anime appears to reference events from this arc as though they happened. More on that in the Hot Take below.

Gotei 13 Invasion Arc (Episodes 317-342)

The runner-up. Mod Soul duplicates of the Gotei 13 captains infiltrate Soul Society, and Ichigo can't rely on his powers. The imposter concept gives the arc more tension than the Bount or Amagai arcs ever managed. Kageroza Inaba, the villain, actually feels dangerous, which is more than you can say for most filler antagonists.

It runs long at 26 episodes and certain fights lose steam, but the overall experience is solid if you're open to more Bleach after the canon run.

Forest of Menos (Episodes 147-149)

Three episodes. That's it. And they accomplish more worldbuilding than arcs ten times their length. Ashido, the lost Soul Reaper surviving alone in the Menos Forest, was a character Kubo originally planned for the manga but cut for pacing. His story fills in how the Hollow ecosystem actually works, and the setting builds atmosphere that the main arcs rarely slow down long enough to create. If you watch one short filler block, this is the one.

Episode 132: "Hitsugaya, Karin, and Soccer Ball"

Ichigo's younger sister Karin bullies Captain Hitsugaya into joining her neighborhood soccer match. On paper, it sounds like throwaway fluff. In practice, it's one of the only times in 366 episodes that Karin gets meaningful screen time and genuine character work. The Hitsugaya/Karin dynamic plays naturally (prodigy captain humbled by a stubborn kid who doesn't care about rank), and the episode avoids the forced melodrama that plagues most filler one-offs.

Episode 133: "Ikkaku: The Hot-Blooded Kendo Tale"

Ikkaku teaches a bullied kid how to fight. Pure character piece. Zero plot relevance. But it captures who Ikkaku actually is (aggressive, blunt, secretly kind) in ways that some of his canon fights don't bother with. Good comfort-food anime.

Hot Take: The Zanpakuto Rebellion Deserves to Be Canon

The Rebellion arc (episodes 230-265) explored a question Kubo's manga never touched: what happens when a zanpakuto gains full autonomy and turns on its wielder?

And the wild part is how naturally the concept fits. Zanpakuto aren't just weapons in Bleach. They have names. They have inner worlds. They communicate with their wielders, argue with them, test them. The idea that they could rebel isn't a stretch of the lore; it's a logical extension of it. Kubo built the foundation. The filler team built the house.

The designs stuck, too. Senbonzakura as a masked warrior. Kazeshini as a violent, chaotic spirit who resents Hisagi's self-control. Sogyo no Kotowari as twin children. These aren't footnotes in the fandom's memory. They're load-bearing images that people associate with the characters permanently. Most fans can picture Senbonzakura's human form faster than they can picture half the canon Arrancar.

Then TYBW dropped a reference that nobody expected. During a Byakuya flashback in the modern anime, the show briefly depicts events resembling the Rebellion timeline, specifically the confrontation between Byakuya and Koga Kuchiki. Kubo is deeply involved in TYBW's production. He hasn't confirmed or denied the nod. The "make it canon" crowd has never been louder.

Would the full arc slot cleanly into continuity? Not perfectly. Some power levels don't align, and a few character positions are off relative to where the manga was at that point. But the thematic core, zanpakuto as autonomous beings capable of rejecting their wielders, fits Kubo's world like a sheath fits its blade. If any filler arc in shonen history earned a promotion to canon, it's this one.

Cut the Filler, Keep the Bankai

Bleach is better without 45% of its runtime. That's not an insult to the show. It's just what happens when a weekly anime outruns its source material for eight straight years. The canon arcs, from Soul Society's legendary twist through the Arrancar saga's emotional crescendo, hit harder when they aren't buried under dozens of episodes about vampires and ball games.

But "skip everything" is a blunt instrument for a series where the Zanpakuto Rebellion exists, where the Forest of Menos quietly fills a real lore gap, and where a soccer episode gives Karin her only meaningful moment in the entire anime. Use the skip list as your foundation. Use the "Worth Watching" picks as your exceptions. And check whether a "filler" episode is actually anime-original before you hit skip, because the trackers aren't always right.

FAQ

How many episodes of Bleach are filler?

163 out of 366 episodes in the original series are filler, roughly 45%. If you also count the 37 mixed canon episodes (which blend manga scenes with anime-original content), the non-pure-canon number climbs higher, but mixed episodes are generally worth keeping.

Can I skip all Bleach filler and still understand the story?

Yes. Every filler arc and standalone can be cut without losing plot-critical information. The manga storyline flows cleanly across the 203 canon and mixed canon episodes. Filler characters sometimes appear in the background of later canon episodes, but they're never relevant.

Does Bleach: Thousand-Year Blood War have filler?

No. TYBW has zero filler across all of its cours. Studio Pierrot adopted a seasonal schedule, and every episode pulls from the manga. Some scenes are anime-original expansions that Kubo supervised to address gaps in the manga's ending, but those are creator-approved additions to the canon, not filler.

Is the Zanpakuto Rebellion arc worth watching?

It's the most widely recommended filler arc in Bleach, and it earns that reputation. The premise (zanpakuto spirits gaining physical forms and rebelling against their masters) fits the show's lore naturally, the villain carries real emotional weight, and the character designs became iconic. If you're watching one filler arc, make it this one.

Why do different Bleach filler lists show different episode counts?

It comes down to how each tracker handles "mixed canon" episodes and whether manga omake adaptations (like episodes 213-214) count as filler. Different sites use different definitions, which produces counts anywhere from 160 to 165. The safest approach: skip only episodes that are entirely anime-original and keep anything with partial manga content.
