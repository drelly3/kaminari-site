Does Death Note have filler? No. Zero filler episodes. Across all 37 entries, every single one is adapted from Tsugumi Ohba and Takeshi Obata's manga. For a franchise that consistently ranks among the most popular anime ever made, the 0% filler rate is almost comically clean. Compare that to Naruto at 41% or Bleach at 45%, and Death Note looks like it showed up to the exam already knowing every answer.

Death Note's infamous shift after Episode 25 is one of the most debated quality drops in anime history. Near and Mello replace L, the pacing changes, and the community has been arguing about whether the final twelve episodes earn their place since 2007. Fans searching for a filler guide are often looking for someone to tell them whether they can stop at Episode 25 with a clear conscience.

With Death Note: The Musical getting a full revival at London's Barbican Theatre this summer (July 30 through September 12, 2026) and the Duffer Brothers' Netflix live-action adaptation still sitting in development limbo, the franchise stays in the conversation even without new anime on the horizon. Here's the honest, phase-by-phase verdict guide that you've got to know.

Death Note: The Complete Skip or Watch List

Since there are no Death Note filler episodes to flag, a traditional Death Note filler list would be one line long. The verdicts below do the job that actually matters: they cover the show's three structural phases, flag the moments fans genuinely debate, and give you honest viewing guidance for all 37 episodes.

The L Era (Episodes 1-25)

EpisodesPhaseTypeVerdict
1-15The Cat-and-Mouse GameCanonWatch. The stretch that made Death Note legendary. Light finds the notebook, L enters the frame, and the psychological chess match between them is as tight as anime gets. Episode 1 ("Rebirth") has minor anime-original additions (Light hesitates before picking up the notebook, a killing montage absent from the manga), but they strengthen the setup rather than pad it. Episode 7 ("Overcast") has a reputation as the weakest in this run, with the Naomi Misora resolution feeling a bit too convenient, but it's still essential to the plot. No skips.
16-24The Yotsuba Group ArcCanonWatch. The pacing shifts here. Light surrenders the Death Note and loses his memories, the Yotsuba corporate conspiracy takes over, and the show becomes more of an ensemble thriller than a two-person duel. Some fans find this stretch slower without the direct Light-vs-L tension, and the criticism is fair. But the arc does crucial work: it tests whether Kira's ideology can survive without Light consciously driving it, and the payoff when his memories come flooding back is one of the most iconic moments in the series. Worth every minute of the slower build.
25"Silence"Canon (Mixed)Watch. The episode that changed everything. L's death plays out slightly differently than in the manga (no final inner monologue, added symbolic imagery), but the emotional devastation lands either way. The anime's version arguably hits harder visually. If you're reading this guide because you've heard "Death Note falls off after L dies," this is the hinge. Keep going.

The Transition (Episode 26)

EpisodePhaseTypeVerdict
26"Renewal"Canon (Mixed)Watch. The closest thing Death Note has to filler. The first half recaps the events of Episodes 1-25, making it the only recap content in the entire series. But the second half introduces Near and Mello, jumps five years forward, and sets up the final act. You cannot skip this episode. The recap is the price of admission for the new characters and time jump waiting on the other side.

The Near/Mello Era (Episodes 27-37)

EpisodesPhaseTypeVerdict
27-36The Final GameCanonWatch. Here's where the community fractures. Near and Mello step into L's role, the psychological warfare shifts from personal to institutional, and the pacing runs hotter with time jumps and parallel storylines. The common criticism: Near reads like a copy of L without the magnetism, and Mello's mafia subplot tests your patience. The counterargument: these episodes show Light at the peak of his corruption, manipulating entire systems instead of outdueling one detective, and his slow unraveling is compelling if you're watching for character rather than just the chess match. The quality does dip from the first 25 episodes. Pretending otherwise would be dishonest. But it doesn't dip enough to justify abandoning the story twelve episodes from its conclusion.
37"New World"Canon (Mixed)Watch. The finale diverges from the manga more than any other episode. Light's death plays out differently (shot and cornered in the anime, killed by Ryuk writing his name in the manga), and Misa's fate is changed entirely. The anime's ending is more cinematic. The manga's is more thematically clean. Either way, you need to see how the notebook closes.

Secretly Peak: Death Note Episodes Worth Defending

Death Note doesn't have filler to rehabilitate, but it does have episodes and stretches that get unfairly dismissed or rushed past. These three picks are the ones worth slowing down for, whether you're watching for the first time or coming back to a series you think you already figured out.

Episode 2: "Confrontation"

The real start of the show. Where Episode 1 sets up the premise, "Confrontation" is the moment Death Note becomes Death Note. L's broadcast trap, Light's furious reaction, and the instant escalation from "teenager with a notebook" to "global psychological warfare" all land in 23 minutes. If the first episode is the cover of the notebook, Episode 2 is the first name written in ink.

The Yotsuba Stretch (Episodes 16-24)

The arc that tests people's patience is also the one doing the heaviest thematic lifting. A memory-wiped Light working alongside L to catch a Kira he doesn't remember being is the most interesting version of the character the show ever produces. He's genuinely likable here, which makes the moment his memories return and the "good Light" vanishes forever one of the coldest beats in anime. Fans who rush through this section to get back to the duel miss the point entirely: the duel was already decided before Light remembered he was playing.

Episode 25: "Silence"

Most fans already know why this episode matters, but its craft deserves closer attention. The anime's choice to strip L's final inner monologue and replace it with silence, rain, and a foot-washing scene is a genuine artistic decision that divides even manga loyalists. The stillness of L's death, set against Light's internal victory, is the kind of storytelling restraint that Death Note rarely gets credit for. Regardless of where you land on the Near/Mello era, Episode 25 is the emotional peak, and it earns that in every frame.

Hot Take: The Post-L Episodes Aren't Filler, They're the Bill Coming Due

The most popular take in the Death Note fandom is that the show peaks at Episode 25 and everything after is a lesser version of itself. The quality does dip. Near lacks L's magnetism. Mello's arc strains credulity. The pacing trades intimacy for scope. All true.

But the post-L stretch serves a purpose the first 25 episodes couldn't: it shows what happens when Light actually wins. The L era is the chess match. The Near/Mello era is the aftermath, where Light has to maintain a god complex without the only person who ever made him feel mortal. His arrogance compounds, his mistakes multiply, and his downfall becomes inevitable because he stopped being challenged at his level.

Skipping Episodes 26-37 doesn't preserve a perfect show. It leaves a story without its closing argument. Light's death is the thesis statement the whole series builds toward. Without it, Death Note is just a villain who got away with it, and that's a worse story than the one Ohba actually wrote.

The Last Name on the Page

The Death Note filler list is the easiest one in anime: zero filler episodes across the entire run. But the number alone is not why you searched for this guide, and pretending otherwise would be a waste of your time.

Every episode is canon. Not every episode hits the same heights. Use the verdicts above to know what you're walking into, watch the Yotsuba stretch with the patience it deserves, and whatever you do, don't stop at Episode 25. Death Note's ending is divisive for a reason, and that reason is worth experiencing for yourself.

FAQ

Does Death Note have filler?

No. Death Note has a 0% filler rate across all 37 episodes. Every episode is adapted from the manga. Four episodes (1, 25, 26, and 37) contain minor anime-original additions or changes from the source material, but none are filler by any standard definition.

Which Death Note episodes are mixed canon and filler?

Episodes 1, 25, 26, and 37. Episode 1 adds a hesitation beat and a killing montage. Episode 25 changes small details of L's death scene. Episode 26 includes recap material alongside the Near/Mello introduction. Episode 37 alters how Light and Misa's stories end. All four are still essential viewing.

Can I stop watching Death Note after Episode 25?

You can, but the story isn't finished. Episode 25 ends L's arc, not Light's. The final twelve episodes show Light's corruption reaching its peak and his eventual downfall. The quality does shift, but the ending gives the series its thematic weight. Stopping at 25 leaves you with half a story.

Is Death Note: Relight worth watching?

Only for completionists. The two Relight films are recap compilations that condense the series with a handful of new scenes. They don't replace the full anime and don't add essential story content.

How many episodes of Death Note are there?

37 episodes in the main series, aired from October 2006 to June 2007. There are also two Relight compilation films, but the core experience is the 37-episode run.
