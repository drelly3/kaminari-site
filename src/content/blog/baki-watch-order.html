The Netflix-only route (the easy way in)

For nearly everyone, the smartest path is the Netflix run: Baki (2018), then its second season, then both seasons of Baki Hanma, then Baki-Dou. It is consistently animated, it is all in one place, and it is written to stand on its own, so you will not feel lost jumping straight in. For a newcomer, that route is basically all how to watch Baki in order requires.

The one thing to know is that Netflix's Baki (2018) is technically a continuation of the older 2001 series rather than a remake, picking the manga back up at a later arc. Even so, it reintroduces what matters, so most fans recommend starting here and never looking back. If you only ever watch the Netflix entries, you still get a complete, satisfying version of Baki's saga.

Do you need the 2001 Baki the Grappler?

Short answer: no, but it is a fun bonus. The 2001 Baki the Grappler ran for two 24-episode seasons and covers Baki's earlier days, the Underground Arena and the Maximum Tournament, before the Netflix run picks up. It is genuinely enjoyable and gives the modern seasons more weight, so completionists should watch it first.

There are two catches. It is older, so the animation shows its age next to the Netflix entries, and it is not on Netflix, so you will have to track it down on other services or physical releases depending on your region. The 1994 OVA that predates it is a short, skippable curiosity you can safely ignore unless you want every scrap of history. So the answer to what order to watch Baki depends on your appetite: Netflix-only for speed, or the 2001 series first for the complete picture.

The Netflix naming, decoded

The labeling is where people get lost, so here is the plain version. On Netflix, the modern story is split across two named series, each with two seasons. Baki (2018) is the first, covering the Most Evil Death Row Convicts arc in its first season and the Great Raitai Tournament in its second (you may see that second season listed under alternate titles like Dai Raitaisai-hen). Baki Hanma is the second series, again running two seasons, adapting the Son of Ogre arc that builds to the long-awaited fight with Yujiro.

People often call Baki Hanma "Baki Season 3," but Netflix does not label it that way, so searching for a third season inside the Baki listing leads nowhere. Match each subtitle to its place in the run and the confusion clears up fast.

Is the Kengan Ashura crossover canon? Where does Baki-Dou fit?

Two recent entries round things out. Baki Hanma vs Kengan Ashura (2024) is a crossover film that pits Baki against fighters from the Kengan Ashura series. It is a fun one-off, but it is officially non-canon and sits outside the main timeline, so watch it after Baki Hanma purely for the spectacle, or skip it with no loss.

Baki-Dou: The Invincible Samurai (2026) is the newest canon chapter, released on Netflix in two parts across the year, and it continues the story with the revived-swordsman Musashi Miyamoto arc. It is the current endpoint of the anime, so slot it in last, after everything else.

Where to watch Baki

Everything from Baki (2018) onward, including Baki Hanma, the Kengan Ashura crossover, and Baki-Dou, streams exclusively on Netflix, which makes the modern run easy to marathon in one place. The older 1994 OVA and the 2001 Baki the Grappler are the exception, living on other services or physical media that vary by region, so that is the part to hunt down if you want the full history. One heads-up before you start: Baki is an adult series known for extremely graphic fight violence and dark themes, so it is firmly not for younger viewers.

Step into the arena in the right order

For all its tangled titles, the Baki watch order comes down to a simple choice: take the Netflix run from Baki (2018) through Baki Hanma to Baki-Dou for the fast, self-contained path, or start back with the 2001 Baki the Grappler if you want every round from the opening bell. Add the Kengan Ashura crossover for fun, skip the old OVA unless you are a completionist, and you are set to watch one of anime's most gloriously over-the-top fighting sagas without second-guessing where each season goes.

FAQ

What order do I watch Baki in?

For the modern story, watch Baki (2018), then its second season (the Great Raitai Tournament), then both seasons of Baki Hanma, then Baki-Dou. If you want the full history, start with the 2001 Baki the Grappler first, though it is not on Netflix.

Do I need to watch Baki the Grappler (2001) first?

No. Netflix's Baki (2018) is written to stand on its own, so you can start there without feeling lost. The 2001 series is an optional, older look at Baki's earlier fights, and most newcomers skip straight to the Netflix run.

Is Baki Hanma the same as Baki Season 3?

Not officially. Fans casually call Baki Hanma the "third season," but Netflix lists it as its own two-season series rather than a continuation of the Baki (2018) listing, so search for Baki Hanma by name.

Is the Kengan Ashura crossover canon?

No. Baki Hanma vs Kengan Ashura (2024) is a non-canon crossover film outside the main timeline. It is a fun bonus you can watch after Baki Hanma, but it changes nothing in the core story.

Where can I watch Baki?

Everything from Baki (2018) onward is on Netflix, including Baki Hanma, the Kengan crossover, and Baki-Dou. The 1994 OVA and the 2001 Baki the Grappler are not on Netflix and live on other services or physical media depending on region. Availability varies, so check what is live where you are.
