Still haunted by Kaneki's unraveling from shy college kid to something that feeds in the dark? Plenty of anime like Tokyo Ghoul deliver the same dread: an ordinary person forced into a monstrous new existence, a hidden predatory species living among us, stomach-churning body horror, and the quiet agony of hiding what you have become. Nothing copies it outright, but the closest ones press on the same raw nerve.

The anime most like Tokyo Ghoul, ranked

Here are the anime like Tokyo Ghoul that genuinely share its build, ranked from the closest match to the loosest. The Similarity Rating is our read on how near each one gets to Tokyo Ghoul specifically, not how good the show is overall, and we kept the stars to whole numbers. The build we are measuring against is simple: an ordinary person forced into a hidden monstrous existence, caught between humans and a predatory species, heavy on body horror and tragedy.

1. Parasyte: The Maxim

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Shinichi is a normal high schooler until an alien parasite tries to burrow into his brain and ends up stuck in his right hand instead. As more parasites quietly infest humanity, devouring people and wearing their faces, Shinichi and his strange new companion are caught in a war for survival. The series asks hard questions about what makes a monster and what makes a human. It is tense, gory, and surprisingly thoughtful.

For Tokyo Ghoul's exact nerve, this is the undisputed number-one peer. Shinichi Izumi is an ordinary student whose right hand is hijacked by an alien parasite, and like Kaneki he is thrust into a secret predatory war where human-eating organisms hide in plain sight among Tokyo's civilian population. The series forces him to gradually lose his fragile human emotions and evolve into a lethal hybrid combatant, mirroring Kaneki's own grim hardening, and it wrestles with the same question of whether someone who feeds like a monster is still a person at all. The body horror, the moral weight, and the monsters-among-us dread line up perfectly.

2. Ajin: Demi-Human

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Netflix

Synopsis: Kei Nagai is a cold, studious high schooler until he dies in a traffic accident and immediately revives, exposing himself as an Ajin, an immortal being that society treats as less than human. Hunted by a government determined to capture and experiment on his kind, he goes on the run and is pulled into a war between Ajin who want revenge and the humans who fear them. His power to summon a ghostly black figure makes him a weapon. The series is a taut, brutal cat-and-mouse thriller.

Ajin is the closest modern-thriller match on the list. Kei Nagai discovers he is an Ajin, an immortal sub-species hunted by the Japanese government for grotesque biological experimentation, which maps almost exactly onto Kaneki's existence as hunted prey under the CCG. It is built from the same parts: underground factions, terrorist rebellions led by radical immortals like Satou, and the brutal reality of being hunted like an animal in modern society. The moral murk is identical too, with neither the humans nor the Ajin coming out clean. One note: it streams on Netflix, so availability depends on your region.

3. Devilman Crybaby

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Netflix

Synopsis: Gentle, tearful Akira merges with a powerful demon and becomes Devilman, keeping his human heart inside a monster's body. As demons reveal themselves and humanity spirals into paranoid violence, Akira fights to protect the people he loves while the line between human and monster collapses around him. The series is hyper-stylized, explicit, and emotionally devastating. It builds to one of the bleakest finales in anime.

Devilman Crybaby is the psychological and tragic counterpart to Tokyo Ghoul. Akira Fudo fuses with the demon Amon to become Devilman, keeping his weeping human heart while straining to control ravenous, carnal urges, the same tug-of-war between humanity and appetite that defines Kaneki. Just as sharply, it captures a society's rapid descent into hysteria, paranoia, and government-sanctioned witch hunts against anyone suspected of being a monster, which is Tokyo Ghoul's human cruelty pushed to its breaking point. It is more surreal and even more extreme, but the heart of it is pure Tokyo Ghoul. One note: it streams on Netflix, so availability depends on your region.

4. Claymore

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Prime Video

Synopsis: In a world preyed on by shape-shifting man-eaters called Yoma, an organization fields half-Yoma women, the Claymores, who wield massive swords to hunt the monsters down. Clare is one of them, driven by a vengeance rooted in a childhood the Yoma destroyed. The deeper she goes, the more she risks losing herself to the monster in her own blood. It is grim, bloody, and quietly tragic.

Claymore is the dark-fantasy blueprint for the CCG itself. An organization creates half-human, half-monster female warriors by grafting the flesh of human-eating Yoma into their bodies, then sends them to hunt the monsters hiding undercover among ordinary people. They are ranked by combat power and fight a constant inner battle against Awakening into the very beasts they hunt, which is Kaneki's tightrope between human and ghoul handed a greatsword. The organization that controls them is as cold and exploitative as the CCG, too. It is medieval rather than urban, but the half-monster tragedy and the monster-hunting hierarchy are a strong match.

5. Shiki

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll, Prime Video

Synopsis: Life is quiet in the isolated mountain village of Sotoba until a string of inexplicable deaths begins the summer a strange family moves into an old mansion on the hill. The town doctor, Toshio Ozaki, slowly realizes the dead are returning as Shiki, undead creatures that must feed on the living to survive. As the body count climbs, the village splits into the hunted and the hunters. It is a slow burn that becomes genuinely harrowing.

Shiki is the definitive deconstruction of the human-versus-monster food chain, which is the exact moral knot at the center of Tokyo Ghoul. When an undead predatory clan moves into an isolated town, the story spirals into a horrific ideological war over whether creatures that kill only out of biological necessity deserve total eradication, the same question Kaneki and the ghouls are trapped inside. It refuses to hand anyone the moral high ground, and it builds to some genuinely stomach-churning human cruelty that rivals anything the monsters do. It is rural and slow rather than urban and kinetic, but on theme it may be the purest match here. One note: Shiki's streaming home is unsettled right now, so check what is available in your region.

6. Chainsaw Man

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Denji is a broke teenager who hunts devils to pay off a dead man's debt, until a betrayal fuses him with his chainsaw-devil companion and turns him into something new. Recruited by a government devil-hunting agency, he throws himself into a brutal world while chasing the simplest dreams. The series lurches between crude comedy, shocking violence, and raw emotion. The animation is gorgeous and unhinged.

The overlap is public-safety devil-extermination agents teaming up with weaponized human-devil hybrids to put down threats born from negative human dread, which rhymes closely with the CCG and its half-ghoul complications. Denji's part-devil body, the grim bureau he answers to, the high workplace casualty rate, the urban back-alley brawls, and the visceral dismemberment all sit right next to Tokyo Ghoul. It is crasser and more chaotic than Tokyo Ghoul's bleak restraint, but the becoming-a-monster-to-fight-monsters core connects. Our Chainsaw Man filler list sorts out what to skip.

7. Deadman Wonderland

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Ganta is an ordinary middle schooler wrongly convicted of slaughtering his entire class and sent to a privatized prison that doubles as a twisted theme park. To survive, he discovers he can weaponize his own blood, joining the ranks of inmates with similar powers forced to fight for the crowd's entertainment. The series is savage, bleak, and soaked in cruelty. It pulls no punches with its despair.

Deadman Wonderland hits a very specific Tokyo Ghoul note. Ganta Igarashi is framed for the slaughter of his entire class and thrown into a brutal private prison, where inmates wield the Branches of Sin, weaponized blood that erupts from their flesh into blades and projectiles. That power functions almost identically to Kaneki's Kagune, right down to the body-horror imagery of turning your own blood into a weapon. Add the ordinary-kid-dragged-into-a-nightmare setup and the relentless cruelty, and the match is strong. There is no hidden-species-among-us angle, and it leans into prison-game shock, so it sits mid-list, but the Kagune-like powers and the dark descent fit.

8. Gleipnir

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Shuichi is an unremarkable high schooler hiding a bizarre secret: he can transform into a hulking, monstrous mascot-like creature with a hidden zipper down its back. When he saves a girl named Clair, she discovers his power and drags him into a deadly, sexually charged hunt for mysterious coins that grant wishes. The series mixes body horror, violence, and unsettling intimacy. It gets stranger and darker as it goes.

The build link is the teenager who involuntarily transforms into an empty, monstrous suit and gets dragged into a lethal battle against other grotesque mutants. Shuichi's transformation mirrors Kaneki's self-loathing, his body dysphoria, and the slow psychological decay that comes from leaning on violent instincts he never asked for. The queasy body horror of the change itself taps the same vein. It is smaller, pulpier, and more fixated on its central pair than on any larger moral war, which keeps it mid-list, but the ordinary-teen-becoming-a-monster premise is a solid partial match.

9. Attack on Titan

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Humanity huddles behind enormous walls while towering, man-eating Titans roam the land outside, until the day the walls are breached and Eren Yeager swears to wipe the Titans out. He joins the military's elite Survey Corps, throwing himself into a desperate war, only to discover that the truth behind the Titans is far darker than anyone imagined. He also gains the power to transform into a Titan himself. It builds to one of the most talked-about stories in anime.

Attack on Titan shares Tokyo Ghoul's darkest reveal: that the monsters are not simply evil, but people trapped by biology, propaganda, and inherited curses they never chose. The terror of cannibalistic humanoids, the horror of human experimentation, and the slow realization that the real cruelty is human all echo Tokyo Ghoul's worldview, and Eren carrying the power of the very thing he hunts rhymes with Kaneki's half-ghoul existence. It plays out on an epic military and civilizational scale rather than an intimate urban horror, which lands it lower on the build, but the monsters-are-us tragedy is a strong thematic match. 10. Psycho-Pass

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: In a future Tokyo, a vast system called Sibyl measures every citizen's mental state and flags those likely to commit crimes before they act. Rookie inspector Akane Tsunemori joins a unit that hunts these latent criminals, working alongside the enforcers who do the dirty, dangerous work. When a brilliant criminal the system cannot read emerges, her faith in the whole apparatus begins to crack. It is a sleek, cerebral sci-fi thriller.

Psycho-Pass captures the cold, clinical side of Tokyo Ghoul: the sleek, rain-slicked metropolitan Tokyo and the systematic, state-run machinery that tracks and eliminates designated threats, which is the CCG's whole approach to hunting ghouls. The investigative procedure, the moral rot inside the enforcement system, and the question of who the real monsters are all rhyme with Tokyo Ghoul's institutional side. There are no ghouls, body horror, or transformations here, so it sits lower on the build, but the grim-urban-enforcement atmosphere and the rogue-threats-hunted-by-the-state structure connect.

11. Kabaneri of the Iron Fortress

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll, Prime Video

Synopsis: In a steam-powered, isolationist Japan, humanity huddles inside fortified stations while the Kabane, armored undead monsters, overrun the land. Ikoma, a young engineer, survives a Kabane attack half-transformed and becomes a Kabaneri, strong enough to fight back without fully turning. He joins survivors fleeing aboard an armored train, battling the horde. The action is fast, stylish, and desperate.

Kabaneri puts its hero in a bind close to Kaneki's. A young technician is infected by cannibalistic undead creatures and becomes a half-human hybrid, a Kabaneri, then uses his monster abilities and his bio-mechanical tech to fight alongside the human survivors who still fear him. That straddling of the human and the monstrous, plus the dread of fully turning, is the Tokyo Ghoul hook in a different setting. It focuses more on steampunk zombie defense than on psychological tragedy, and it swaps the hidden-among-us element for open warfare, which keeps it at the bottom, but the half-monster struggle connects.

A couple that get recommended but watch differently

Search around for anime similar to Tokyo Ghoul and a couple of heavy hitters come up that share a piece of the mood but not the hidden-monster-among-humans horror. They are worth your time. We just want the match to feel honest.

Death Note nails the cold, calculating cat-and-mouse between a morally compromised anti-hero and the organization chasing him, which rhymes with the CCG-versus-ghoul war, but it is a bloodless psychological thriller with no monsters, body horror, or transformation. Jujutsu Kaisen shares the hook of a decent kid carrying a hungry monster sealed inside him, yet it runs on bright, action-forward shonen spectacle rather than Tokyo Ghoul's bleak, intimate horror. Both are essential viewing. Just go in knowing they trade Tokyo Ghoul's quiet, personal dread for something more cerebral or more explosive. FAQs

Is there an anime exactly like Tokyo Ghoul?

Not a perfect twin, but two come very close. Parasyte: The Maxim matches the body horror and the dread of an ordinary teen fused with a flesh-eater hiding among humans, while Ajin matches the premise almost beat for beat, an unremarkable person exposed as a hidden, hunted non-human and chased by a government that wants to dissect his kind. Each nails a core piece of what makes Tokyo Ghoul work.

What is the best anime like Tokyo Ghoul?

For the closest overall experience, Parasyte: The Maxim and Ajin lead the pack, one for the body-horror transformation and one for the hunted-species premise. If you want the human-heart-trapped-in-a-monster tragedy specifically, Devilman Crybaby is the one to beat, with Chainsaw Man close behind for a grimier, part-devil take.

What anime has a half-human half-monster hero like Tokyo Ghoul?

Several match Kaneki's split existence. Parasyte fuses Shinichi with an alien creature, Ajin reveals Kei as an immortal demi-human, Devilman Crybaby gives Akira a demon's body and a human heart, Chainsaw Man turns Denji into a devil hybrid, and Claymore fields half-Yoma warriors. All center a hero caught between human and monster.

Is there an anime as dark and violent as Tokyo Ghoul?

Yes. Devilman Crybaby, Shiki, Deadman Wonderland, and Claymore all match or exceed Tokyo Ghoul's violence and bleakness, whether through explicit body horror, slow-burn dread, grimy brutality, or unrelenting despair. Go in prepared.

Where can you watch anime like Tokyo Ghoul?

Most of these stream on Crunchyroll, including Tokyo Ghoul itself along with Attack on Titan, Psycho-Pass and Deadman Wonderland, while Kabaneri of the Iron Fortress sits on Prime Video, and Ajin and Devilman Crybaby on Netflix.
