Where the Scarlet Bond movie fits (and is it canon?)

Scarlet Bond, the franchise's first film, lands right after the events of Season 2, so that is where to watch it. It follows Rimuru and company to the Raja Kingdom for a self-contained adventure with an action-packed plot and some strong character moments.

Its canon status is a little fuzzy: it was supervised by original creator Fuse, but it sits slightly outside the strict light-novel timeline, so purists debate it. Here is the practical call, though. A character introduced in the film shows up again in later content, and the story is genuinely good, so I would watch it rather than skip it. Slot it in after Season 2 and before Season 3 for the smoothest ride.

Do you need to watch The Slime Diaries?

The Slime Diaries is a twelve-episode spin-off, and it is pure optional comfort viewing. Instead of big battles and plot progression, it is a cozy slice-of-life series about the daily lives and comedic antics of Rimuru and the residents of Tempest, loosely bridging the gap between Seasons 1 and 2.

Skip it with zero worry if you only want the main story, since it has no bearing on later events. If you fall in love with this cast, though, it is a delightful hangout, and it works just as well as a relaxed watch after Season 1 or as a palate cleanser once you have caught up. Take it or leave it based on how much extra time in Tempest you want.

What about the OVAs and side stories?

A few smaller pieces round out the franchise, and all of them are optional. Visions of Coleus (also called Coleus' Dream) is a three-episode side story set around the gap between Seasons 1 and 2, though it released much later, so for a first watch it is cleanest to treat it as a standalone extra rather than pausing the main run for it. The OVAs and OADs bundled with the manga are short comedy pieces, fun but inessential. The various recap specials, like Veldora's Journal, just compress episodes you have already seen, so skip those unless you want a quick refresher before a new season.

Is Season 3 (and Season 4) worth it?

Worth flagging honestly: Season 3 shifts the show toward politics and diplomacy, with Rimuru's Jura-Tempest Federation growing into a recognized world power rather than trading blows every episode. If you love the worldbuilding and the sprawling side cast, it is a rewarding stretch. If you mainly show up for Rimuru flattening enemies, it is a slower, talkier ride, so go in knowing the tone has shifted.

The good news is the story is far from over. A fourth season began airing in 2026 as a multi-cour run, picking up right where Season 3 left off, and a second film (Tears of the Azure Sea) arrived alongside it. All told, the main run through Season 3 comes to roughly 30 to 36 hours depending on how many extras you fold in, so the full That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime watch order is a real commitment, but one spread across a story that keeps moving somewhere. Anyone catching up now has a clear runway straight into the newest episodes.
