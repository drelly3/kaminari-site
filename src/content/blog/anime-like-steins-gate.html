The anime most like Steins;Gate, ranked

Here are the anime like Steins;Gate that genuinely share its build, ranked from the closest match to the loosest. The Similarity Rating is our read on how near each one gets to Steins;Gate specifically, not how good the show is overall, and we kept the stars to whole numbers. The build we are measuring against is simple: a grounded sci-fi psychological thriller driven by a time-travel or time-leap mechanic, where a brilliant protagonist races to prevent deaths by altering the timeline again and again, with real psychological weight and a bittersweet emotional core.

1. Summer Time Rendering

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Disney+, Hulu

Synopsis: Shinpei returns to his small island hometown for a funeral, only to learn his childhood friend's death may not have been an accident. As a deadly mystery unfolds, he gains the ability to loop back in time whenever he dies, and uses it to protect the people he loves. It is a tense, twist-filled thriller that rarely lets up. It is a hidden gem.

Of everything here, this is the closest structural match. Summer Time Rendering is an isolated town mystery driven by a localized time-loop, where every reset pushes the checkpoint forward, forcing the protagonist to treat information and forensic clues as his only persistent inventory in a race against an encroaching paranormal conspiracy. That is Okabe's problem in a different coat: use what you learned last time, change one variable, and run it again. The dread, the twist-heavy plotting, and the cost of every reset line up exactly. The threat is supernatural rather than grounded sci-fi, but the structure is Steins;Gate to the core.

2. Re:Zero

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Subaru is yanked into a fantasy world and discovers his one power: when he dies, he returns to an earlier checkpoint, keeping his memories. He uses it, at enormous psychological cost, to claw toward outcomes where the people he loves survive. The deaths are brutal and the trauma is real. It is a punishing, gripping deconstruction of the wish-fulfillment isekai.

Re:Zero is the emotional twin to Okabe Rintaro. Subaru is the sole bearer of memories across brutal, failed timelines, enduring psychological trauma, panic attacks, and isolation before turning trial-and-error knowledge into tactical, multi-stage plans to beat fate, which is Okabe's exact arc from helpless victim to desperate strategist. The agony of being the only one who remembers the deaths, and having to go back and do it again, is the beating heart of both shows. The one real difference is the fantasy setting, but the loop-and-suffer-to-save-everyone build is a near-perfect match.

3. Link Click

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Two men run a small studio with a secret: one can dive into any photograph to relive the past through its subject's eyes, while the other guides him from the present. They take on clients' requests to revisit lost moments, but changing the past comes with devastating consequences. The animation is slick and the plotting is tight. It is a standout modern donghua.

Link Click shares the exact terror of the butterfly effect that defines Steins;Gate. Diving into past moments to fulfill requests, dealing with fixed points in time, and watching well-intentioned adjustments cascade into tragedy and active police manhunts is Okabe's nightmare in a new form. Both treat time as a fragile system where the smallest change ripples outward catastrophically, and both wring real emotion and dread from that fragility. It is a Chinese donghua rather than a Japanese anime, and a touch shorter, but the butterfly-effect-time-thriller build is a superb match.

Erased shares the desperate urge to undo an impending murder. An ordinary protagonist uses an uncontrollable rewinding phenomenon to prevent a classmate's death, working through the ripple effects between past actions and present realities, which is Steins;Gate's engine with the sci-fi swapped for a quieter conceit. The mounting dread, the emotional weight, and the bittersweet stakes of reaching into the past to save someone all echo Okabe's story directly. It is a grounded human thriller rather than a lab-science one, but the race-across-time-to-save-a-life build is a strong match.

FAQs

Is there an anime exactly like Steins;Gate?

Not a perfect twin, but two come very close. Summer Time Rendering is the closest structural match, a localized time-loop mystery where every reset pushes the checkpoint forward and information is your only inventory, and Re:Zero is the emotional twin to Okabe, the sole bearer of memories across brutal, failed timelines. Each captures the core of what makes Steins;Gate tick.

What is the best anime like Steins;Gate?

For the closest match, Summer Time Rendering and Re:Zero lead the pack, both built on looping back to save someone at a brutal personal cost. If you want the hard-sci-fi, alter-history angle specifically, Vivy: Fluorite Eye's Song is the best anime like Steins;Gate to jump into next, built on changing the future at precise convergence points.

What anime has time travel like Steins;Gate?

Plenty do. Summer Time Rendering, Re:Zero, Link Click, Erased, Puella Magi Madoka Magica, Orange, and Tokyo Revengers all hand their heroes a time-leap, a loop, or a message across time and send them racing to change a doomed outcome, which is the exact mechanic that drives Steins;Gate.

Is Steins;Gate finished, or is there more to watch?

Steins;Gate is complete. The original 2011 series tells a full story, Steins;Gate 0 is a sequel exploring an alternate timeline, and there is a film, with no new anime currently announced. The wider Science Adventure visual-novel series continues to add installments if you want more of the universe.

Where can you watch anime like Steins;Gate?

Most of these stream on Crunchyroll, including Steins;Gate itself, with Summer Time Rendering on Disney+ and Hulu. Streaming rights shift by region and over time, so double-check what is available where you are before you settle in.
