Does JoJo's Bizarre Adventure have filler? No. Across 190 episodes and six completed Parts, every filler database puts the count at zero. David Production adapted Hirohiko Araki's manga faithfully from Phantom Blood through Stone Ocean without adding a single filler episode.

But a JoJo filler list that stops at "0%" is useless, because 190 episodes across six different protagonists, six different settings, and six different power systems is a genuine commitment. And buried inside that run is Stardust Crusaders, a 48-episode Part where roughly half the episodes are standalone "monster of the week" Stand battles that fans have debated skipping for over a decade. Everything is canon. Not everything feels essential.

With Steel Ball Run now airing on Netflix (Episode 1 dropped March 19, 2026; weekly episodes start September 25), new fans are trying to figure out how much JoJo they actually need. The JoJo filler list is empty. The catch-up guide below is what you're really here for.

Three Routes Through the Joestar Bloodline

A JoJo filler list can't trim episodes the way a Naruto or Bleach guide can, because there's nothing flagged as filler. What it can do is help you plan a route through 190 episodes that fits the time you have. JoJo's Part structure actually makes this easier than most long-running anime, since each Part works as a semi-standalone story with its own beginning and end.

Here are three paths. Pick the one that matches your situation.

The Highlight Reel (~10 Hours)

For the person who wants to understand JoJo well enough to follow conversations, get the memes, and know why the Joestar bloodline matters. These are the episodes people reference when they talk about JoJo at all. You'll skip the slower stretches and most of the monster-of-the-week encounters, but you'll meet every JoJo, see every major villain fall, and understand why Part 7 is generating the hype it is.

The Full Bizarre Adventure (~75 Hours)

All 190 episodes. Every Part. No skips, no trims.

At 75 hours, JoJo is a genuine long-haul commitment. But unlike most anime at this length, the Part structure gives you natural stopping points. You can finish Part 2 on a Monday, take a week off, and start Stardust Crusaders fresh without losing the plot. Each Part is its own story with its own resolution. Think of it as six connected short series rather than one continuous marathon.

Skip the recap specials. Skip the older 1993–2002 Stardust Crusaders OVAs (they tell the same story the TV anime tells better). The 2007 Phantom Blood movie is lost and unavailable anyway. The Thus Spoke Kishibe Rohan OVAs are a fun spin-off set during Part 4, but they're optional viewing, not required.

If you have the time, the full run is our recommendation. Part 3's monster-of-the-week stretch tests your patience, but the standalone episodes people love to skip include some of JoJo's most creative battles. You lose nothing by watching everything, and you gain the full weight of a story that's been running since 1987.

Steel Ball Run note: Part 7 is set in an alternate universe. It shares thematic DNA with the original six Parts (Joestars, Zeppelis, the concept of fate) but not a continuous plot. You can start Steel Ball Run without watching Parts 1–6. If you've seen at least Parts 1 and 2, you'll catch the parallels that make Steel Ball Run richer. But it's not required.

The Part 3 Problem: When Canon Feels Like Filler

Stardust Crusaders has 48 episodes and zero filler. Every fight is manga canon. And fans have been skipping half of them for years.

The issue is structural. Parts 1 and 2 told tight, continuous stories. Part 3 introduced Stands and, with them, a formula: the crew encounters a new enemy Stand user, figures out the gimmick, defeats them, and moves on. Some of those encounters are all-time classics (D'Arby the Gambler, the Oingo Boingo brothers, Vanilla Ice). Others are standalone puzzles that don't advance the plot, develop the characters, or raise the stakes.

One popular fan breakdown cut the 48 episodes down to 27 essentials. Others go further, arguing you can watch the first two episodes and the final four and miss nothing plot-critical. The community is genuinely split: some fans consider Part 3 the most iconic JoJo experience, while others call it the weakest stretch in the franchise because of its repetitive structure.

Our take: Part 3 isn't filler, but it is the one Part where the distance between "essential" and "skippable" is widest. If you're watching JoJo in full, lean into the monster-of-the-week format and enjoy the creative problem-solving. If you're on a shorter path, trim confidently. The plot-critical episodes are easy to identify, and skipping a few self-contained Stand fights won't break your understanding of the DIO confrontation.

The Stands you skip might include one of your future favorites. But you can always go back.

190 Episodes, Zero Filler, One Bizarre Legacy

The JoJo filler list is the shortest page on this site, and it belongs to one of the longest-running anime adaptations in modern history. Across Parts 1 through 6, there's nothing to cut, nothing flagged as non-canon, and nothing that contradicts the manga. David Production gave Araki's work a faithful adaptation from start to finish.

Whether you speed through the Highlight Reel or settle in for the full 75-hour Bizarre Adventure, the takeaway is the same: JoJo earned its zero-filler record by building a story where each Part stands on its own, and the generational thread connecting them never breaks. Even after Steel Ball Run wraps, these 190 episodes are the reason the Joestar name carries the weight it does.

FAQ

Does JoJo's Bizarre Adventure have filler?

No. JoJo's Bizarre Adventure has zero filler episodes across its entire 190-episode run. David Production adapted Hirohiko Araki's manga faithfully across all six completed Parts. The only content sometimes considered "optional" is the Stardust Crusaders OVAs from the 1990s and the Thus Spoke Kishibe Rohan spin-off, neither of which is part of the main TV anime.

Is Stardust Crusaders filler?

No, but it's the one Part where fans most commonly skip episodes. All 48 Stardust Crusaders episodes are manga canon, but the monster-of-the-week format means roughly half are standalone Stand battles that don't advance the main plot. The DIO confrontation at the end is essential. Many of the encounters along the way are great but individually skippable if you're short on time.

Can I skip Parts 1 and 2?

You can, but you'll lose more than you think. Phantom Blood (9 episodes) introduces the Joestar-Dio rivalry, the Stone Mask, and Hamon. Battle Tendency (17 episodes) introduces the Pillar Men and Joseph Joestar, who returns as a major character in Part 3. Skipping them means starting at Stardust Crusaders without understanding why DIO matters, who Joseph is, or what the Joestar bloodline represents. At 26 episodes combined, Parts 1 and 2 are shorter than many single-cour anime. Watch them.

Do I need to watch Parts 1–6 before Steel Ball Run?

No. Steel Ball Run takes place in an alternate universe with a separate continuity. You can start watching it on Netflix right now with zero prior JoJo knowledge. The story follows Johnny Joestar and Gyro Zeppeli in a cross-country horse race across 19th-century America, and it stands completely on its own. Fans who've seen Parts 1 and 2 will catch thematic parallels and name callbacks, but they aren't required to follow the plot.

What are the JoJo filler episodes?

There are none. Every episode in the David Production anime (Parts 1–6, Episodes 1–190) is adapted from the manga. The JoJo's Bizarre Adventure filler count cited by every major database is 0%, making it the longest zero-filler streak of any major shonen anime currently running.
