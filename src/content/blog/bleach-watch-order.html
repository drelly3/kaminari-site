Bleach is one of the friendlier long shonen to get into, as long as you know about the filler. The Bleach watch order is simple at its core: watch the original anime from episode 1 to 366, then continue with Thousand-Year Blood War, the sequel that finally adapts the ending. The only real complication is that the original run is loaded with filler, so a chunk of those 366 episodes is safe to skip. Sort that out and the path is clean.

The reason the filler matters is scale. Roughly 160 of the original 366 episodes are anime-only, padded in while the manga caught up, and four big stretches are pure filler you can pass on without losing the plot. The movies add four more non-canon adventures on the side. None of it is required, so the trick is knowing what to keep.

Thousand-Year Blood War: the finale

Thousand-Year Blood War is the reason Bleach feels complete now. The original anime ended in 2012 at episode 366 without adapting the manga's final arc, leaving the story hanging for a decade. The sequel arrived in 2022 and covers that ending across four parts: The Blood Warfare (2022), The Separation (2023), The Conflict (2024), and The Calamity, which premiered in 2026 as the send-off fans waited years for. It carries zero filler, and many fans call it the best Bleach has ever looked. Do not jump ahead to it, though. It assumes you have watched through the original series, so it lands hardest after the full run.

Where to watch Bleach

The two halves of Bleach live in different homes. The original series streams on Crunchyroll and Hulu in most regions, in both sub and dub. Thousand-Year Blood War sits on Hulu in the US and Disney+ internationally, so you may need to switch platforms to continue into the finale. Availability shifts by region, so check what is live where you are before you start the marathon.

FAQ

What is the Bleach watch order?

The Bleach watch order is the original anime from episode 1 to 366, then Thousand-Year Blood War for the final arc. The original run has heavy filler, so many viewers skip the four big filler arcs, while the sequel has none. The four movies are optional and non-canon.

Can I skip the filler in Bleach?

Yes. Around 160 of Bleach's 366 original episodes are filler, and the four major filler arcs (Bount, Amagai, Zanpakuto: Unknown Tales, and Invading Army) cut no canon content. Skipping them keeps the main story intact and saves you a lot of time. A filler guide makes the exact episodes easy to spot.

Where does Thousand-Year Blood War start?

Thousand-Year Blood War begins right after episode 366, at overall episode 367. It is a direct continuation of the original series, not a reboot, so watch the full original run first. It adapts the manga's final arc, which the 2004 anime never reached.

Are the Bleach movies canon?

No. All four Bleach movies are non-canon side stories that do not affect the main plot, so they are optional. They are fun extras you can watch at the rough point in the timeline they fit, or skip without missing anything essential.

Where can I watch Bleach?

The original Bleach series streams on Crunchyroll and Hulu, while Thousand-Year Blood War is on Hulu in the US and Disney+ internationally. Both sub and dub are available. Availability changes by region, so check what is streaming near you before you begin.
