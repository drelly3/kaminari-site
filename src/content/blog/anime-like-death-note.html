Still chasing the high of Light and L's deadly game of chess? Plenty of anime like Death Note deliver the same thrill: a brilliant, morally-grey mind locked in a high-stakes battle of wits, where every move is a gambit and one mistake means everything. The best of them trade action for tension, letting strategy, deduction, and cold nerve carry the drama. No show is a perfect copy, but the closest ones get your pulse going the exact same way.

There is no new season to wait on here, because Death Note wrapped as a complete story back in 2007 and never needed more. Nearly two decades later it is still the benchmark every psychological thriller gets measured against, which is exactly why fans keep hunting for the next one that comes close.

We ranked these from closest match to loosest, judged on one thing: how much each shares Death Note's actual build, a cerebral battle of wits led by a brilliant, morally-grey protagonist, often wielding a dangerous edge, driven by strategy and tension rather than action. The anime most like Death Note, ranked

Here are the anime like Death Note that genuinely share its build, ranked from the closest match to the loosest. The Similarity Rating is our read on how near each one gets to Death Note specifically, not how good the show is overall, and we kept the stars to whole numbers. The build we are measuring against is simple: a cerebral battle of wits led by a brilliant, morally-grey mind, carried by strategy and tension more than action.

1. Code Geass

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Lelouch vi Britannia is an exiled prince living in a world ruled by a brutal empire, until a mysterious girl grants him the Geass, a power that lets him command anyone to obey a single absolute order. He takes up a masked identity, Zero, and launches a rebellion to tear down the empire and build a better world for his sister. What follows is a sweeping war fought as much with strategy and manipulation as with mecha. He is brilliant, charismatic, and willing to cross almost any line.

Of everything here, this is the closest to Death Note's build. Lelouch is Light with an army: a brilliant, morally-grey mastermind handed a supernatural power, chasing a world-changing vision through elaborate schemes, bluffs, and traps, all while locked in a war of wits against opponents nearly as sharp as he is. The gap between his noble goals and his ruthless methods is the exact moral tension Death Note runs on. If you loved watching Light scheme, start here.

2. Monster

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Prime Video

Synopsis: Dr. Kenzo Tenma is a gifted neurosurgeon in Germany who defies his hospital's politics to save a young boy's life, a choice that ruins his career and, years later, unleashes something far worse. The boy, Johan, grows into a brilliant, charming, utterly remorseless killer, and Tenma sets out across Europe to stop the monster he saved. It is a slow-burning, grounded suspense story with no powers and no action, only dread. Johan is widely considered one of the greatest villains in anime.

For Death Note's pure psychological-thriller DNA, nothing comes closer. It is a relentless cat-and-mouse between a principled man and a brilliant, magnetic antagonist, built entirely on tension, deduction, and the slow unraveling of a mind you cannot predict. There is no supernatural note here, just a human monster, which makes the dread even more grounded than Light and L's duel. If the mind games and the moral weight were what hooked you, this is essential.

3. Psycho-Pass

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll, Apple TV

Synopsis: In a future Japan, a system called Sibyl reads people's mental states and flags anyone likely to commit a crime, long before they act. Rookie inspector Akane Tsunemori joins a unit that hunts these latent criminals, until she crosses paths with Shogo Makishima, a brilliant mastermind the system cannot even detect. Their clash forces her to question whether the system is justice or just control. It is a sleek, violent, idea-heavy cyberpunk thriller.

The Death Note overlap is the brilliant criminal mastermind and the questions of justice he forces open: Makishima is a Light-caliber antagonist who manipulates society from the shadows and treats the whole system as a game to break. The cat-and-mouse between him and the enforcers, plus the heavy debate about law, order, and who gets to decide, sits right next to Death Note's core. It adds action and cyberpunk trappings, but the battle of ideas is the same.

4. The Promised Neverland

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Emma, Norman, and Ray are the brightest children at a seemingly idyllic orphanage, until they discover the horrifying truth about what happens to the kids who get adopted. To survive, they have to outwit Isabella, the brilliant caretaker who controls every detail of their lives, and plan an escape under her constant watch. The first season is a tight, nerve-shredding battle of plans and counter-plans. It comes from the same magazine that ran Death Note.

Often called Death Note's spiritual successor, it runs on the exact same engine: a battle of wits between brilliant minds where every move is a gambit and one slip means death. The planning, the bluffing, the constant recalculating as the opponent adapts all echo Light and L, just with children against an adult jailer. The first season especially is as tense and cerebral as anything in the genre, which is why it lands so high for Death Note fans.

5. Terror in Resonance

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll, Apple TV

Synopsis: Two mysterious teenagers calling themselves Sphinx set off a bomb in Tokyo and begin taunting the authorities with cryptic riddles, daring them to keep up. A disgraced detective is the only one who starts to see the pattern behind their attacks. As the game escalates, the tragic reason behind it slowly surfaces. It is a stylish, melancholy thriller with a haunting score.

The build match is the cat-and-mouse itself: brilliant young masterminds running rings around the authorities with elaborate, carefully planned moves, pursued by one sharp investigator who can actually challenge them. The riddle-by-riddle duel and the morally complicated motives rhyme with Death Note's L-versus-Light chess match. It is shorter and more somber, trading Death Note's scope for a tight, emotional story, but the tension is cut from the same cloth.

6. Classroom of the Elite

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: At an elite school where classes compete for privileges in a ruthless points-based system, Kiyotaka Ayanokoji hides his true abilities and coasts in the bottom-ranked class. Behind the bland exterior is a mind that quietly orchestrates events from the shadows, several steps ahead of everyone. As the school's games grow more cutthroat, his real talents slowly surface. It is a cold, calculating drama of social strategy.

The connection is the hidden-genius manipulator: a protagonist who looks unremarkable while secretly pulling strings, outthinking rivals through layered plans and psychological reads, much like Light operating behind his honor-student mask. The school's high-stakes mind games and Ayanokoji's unreadable, morally-grey nature give it a strong Death Note flavor. There is no supernatural power and the stakes are social rather than life-and-death, which keeps it a step below the top.

7. Moriarty the Patriot

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: William James Moriarty is a brilliant young man who looks at a rotten, rigid aristocracy and decides the only cure is to burn it down from the shadows. Alongside his brothers, he engineers elaborate crimes against the cruel and powerful of Victorian England, styling himself the villain the world needs. Eventually a certain famous detective is drawn into his orbit. The series plays out as a run of intricate, morally tangled schemes.

The Death Note parallel is strong: a charismatic mastermind who appoints himself judge of a corrupt society and executes elaborate, meticulously planned crimes to reshape it, convinced his ends justify his means. Moriarty is essentially Light in Victorian dress, and his eventual duel with Sherlock is a pure battle of equal intellects. The period setting and the inverted detective story give it its own identity, but the genius-antihero build is unmistakable.

8. Kaiji

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Kaiji is a broke, aimless young man who gets roped into a high-stakes gambling gauntlet to clear a debt, where losing can cost far more than money. Forced to survive rigged games designed to crush the desperate, he discovers a gift for reading people and bending the rules in his favor. Each game is a brutal psychological war. The tension is relentless and the stakes are life-altering.

The build link is the pure battle of wits under pressure: elaborate games where victory comes down to out-thinking, out-reading, and out-bluffing the opponent, with everything on the line. Kaiji sweating through a deathtrap of a gamble delivers the same white-knuckle, who-is-ten-steps-ahead tension that makes Death Note's confrontations so gripping. It swaps notebooks and detectives for gambling dens, but the psychological chess is just as sharp.

9. Future Diary

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Loner Yukiteru is given a diary that predicts the future, then thrown into a deadly survival game against eleven other diary-holders, each with their own version of the power. The last one standing becomes a god. His only real ally is Yuno, a classmate whose devotion to him is as terrifying as it is useful. It is a chaotic, bloody thriller built on paranoia and mind games.

The overlap is the deadly game of foresight and counter-strategy: competitors using special powers and careful planning to predict and outmaneuver one another, with godhood as the prize and death as the penalty. The constant guessing of who knows what, and who is setting whom up, echoes Death Note's information warfare. It is far more violent and unhinged than Death Note's cold precision, so go in expecting chaos, but the predict-and-counter mind games are the shared core.

10. Steins;Gate

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Self-proclaimed mad scientist Rintaro Okabe stumbles onto a way to send messages into the past, and his goofy experiments quickly spiral into something far more dangerous. As a shadowy organization closes in, he is forced to use time itself to undo disaster after disaster, at a mounting personal cost. The early comedy gives way to one of anime's most harrowing thrillers. It is a slow burn that pays off enormously.

The connection is the genius protagonist locked in a high-stakes mental battle against forces he can barely see, forced to out-think an impossible situation with careful planning and nerve. Okabe's desperate, calculated attempts to stay one move ahead of catastrophe carry the same cerebral tension that powers Death Note, even without a direct rival. It is science-fiction rather than supernatural crime, and the duel is against fate more than a person, which places it mid-list, but the brains-over-brawn thrills are there.

11. Death Parade

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: In a mysterious bar between life and death, a stone-faced bartender named Decim receives pairs of the recently deceased and puts them through games with their souls on the line. The games are designed to reveal who they truly were and decide where they go next. What starts as an eerie puzzle becomes a meditation on guilt, memory, and human nature. It is dark, stylish, and quietly devastating.

The Death Note link is thematic rather than structural: both sit with hard questions about judgment, justice, and what people deserve, watching a cold arbiter weigh lives with chilling detachment. Decim passing sentence on souls echoes Light appointing himself judge of the world, and the series shares Death Note's talent for making you squirm over your own moral instincts. There is no cat-and-mouse here, so it ranks lower, but the dark exploration of judgment is a strong match.

A couple that get recommended but watch differently

Search around for anime similar to Death Note and a few titles come up that share a connection but not the build. They are worth your time. We just want the match to feel honest.

Bakuman comes from the exact same creators as Death Note, so it is a natural recommendation, but it is a warm, grounded drama about two friends chasing a manga career, with none of the thriller tension or moral darkness. Re:Zero is often called psychologically intense, and it is, yet it is a fantasy time-loop about trauma and endurance rather than a calculated battle of wits. Both are excellent. Just go in knowing they scratch a different itch than Light and L's chess match does.

Outthink the wait for the next masterpiece

Nearly two decades on, Death Note is still the gold standard, and nothing has fully replicated its blend of mind games and moral dread. The good news is that plenty of shows capture a piece of it beautifully. Start at the top with Code Geass and Monster for the closest battles of wits, bring the philosophy with Psycho-Pass, or sweat through the pure tension of The Promised Neverland. The whole point of a good list of anime like Death Note is to keep that cerebral, edge-of-your-seat feeling going long after Light and L take their final bow.

FAQs

Is there an anime exactly like Death Note?

Not a perfect twin, but two come very close. Code Geass matches the brilliant, morally-grey lead who gains a supernatural power and wages a war of wits, and Monster matches the grounded, relentless cat-and-mouse with a genius villain. Each nails a core piece of what made Death Note so gripping.

What is the best anime like Death Note?

For the closest battle of wits, Code Geass and Monster lead the pack. If you want the philosophy and the justice debate that ran under Death Note, Psycho-Pass is the one to beat, with The Promised Neverland close behind for pure cerebral tension.

What anime has mind games like Death Note?

The Promised Neverland, Code Geass, Classroom of the Elite, and Kaiji are the strongest picks for sheer battle-of-wits tension, each built on characters outthinking and outmaneuvering one another with every move carrying real stakes.

Is there an anime like Death Note with a supernatural power?

Yes. Code Geass pairs a dangerous mind-control power with elaborate strategy, and Future Diary hands its cast future-predicting diaries in a deadly game, so both match Death Note's blend of a special ability and cold, calculated planning.

Where can you watch anime like Death Note?

Most of these stream on Crunchyroll, including Death Note itself, while Monster currently sits on Prime Video. Streaming rights shift by region and over time, so double-check what is available where you are before you settle in.
