The anime most like Blue Lock, ranked

Here are the anime like Blue Lock that genuinely share its build, ranked from the closest match to the loosest. The Similarity Rating is our read on how near each one gets to Blue Lock specifically, not how good the show is overall, and we kept the stars to whole numbers. The build we are measuring against is simple: hyper-intense competition, a ruthless winner-takes-all structure, ego and ambition, and a unique weapon or edge.

1. Kuroko's Basketball

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Teiko Middle School's basketball team was so dominant it produced the “Generation of Miracles,” five prodigies who went their separate ways to rival high schools. Now Seirin High has two secret weapons: Kagami, a powerhouse returnee from America, and Kuroko, an almost invisible “phantom sixth man” who was the Miracles' hidden playmaker. Together they vow to topple each of the prodigies and reach the top. The games are pure, electric spectacle.

Kuroko's Basketball is the undisputed sports match, the pioneer of the modern “sports anime as superpower shonen” formula that Blue Lock later ran with. Its players wield signature eye techniques, with the Emperor Eye playing almost exactly like Blue Lock's Metavision, slip into heightened flow states called the Zone, and deploy individual specialty weapons that dismantle conventional team tactics. Every match is a battle of these unique abilities, driven by the hunger to defeat a set of monstrous prodigies and prove you are the best. Swap the court for the pitch and the match is nearly exact. If you came to Blue Lock for stylized, over-the-top sports showdowns, start here.

2. Aoashi

Similarity Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Ashito Aoi is a raw, hot-headed talent from the countryside who earns a shot at the youth academy of a professional soccer club in Tokyo. There he discovers that natural ability is not enough: he has to learn to read the whole pitch, rebuild his game, and survive a selection system that cuts players without mercy. His growth from instinctive striker to thinking player is the heart of it. It is a grounded, intelligent soccer drama.

Aoashi is the tactical football sibling, grounded in realistic youth-academy development but built on the exact weapon that defines Blue Lock's hero. Ashito Aoi possesses Yoichi Isagi's signature ability, a bird's-eye spatial awareness that is essentially Metavision by another name, and the series leans hard into pitch geometry, scanning blind spots, and orchestrating plays two moves ahead. On top of that, it drops its gifted outsider into a ruthless selection system that cuts players without mercy, which is Blue Lock's elimination program rendered realistically. It trades the flash and the survival-game staging for grounded drama, but on the same sport and the same core weapon, nothing is closer.

3. Kaiji: Ultimate Survivor

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Down on his luck and drowning in debt, the aimless Kaiji Ito is lured onto a gambling ship with the promise that one night can erase everything he owes. Instead he is thrown into a nightmare of psychological, high-stakes games where losing means financial and physical ruin. To survive, he has to outthink desperate, cutthroat opponents who will betray anyone to win. It is a tense, sweaty, brilliant thriller.

Kaiji is the structural blueprint for Jinpachi Ego's facility. Desperate contenders are locked inside an isolated, claustrophobic complex and forced into sadistic elimination trials where the stakes are everything, and where relying on soft sentimentality gets you destroyed instantly. That is Blue Lock's program with the soccer stripped out: the same pressure-cooker setting, the same ruthless logic that only the selfish and the clever survive, and the same intoxicating tension of watching people gamble their entire futures. It is a gambling thriller rather than a sports anime, but on the survival-game bones that hold Blue Lock up, it may be the purest match here.

4. Tomodachi Game

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Yuichi Katagiri treasures his friends above all else, so when he and his closest companions are abducted and forced into a series of psychological games to pay off a massive debt, he vows to protect them all. But the games are designed to turn friends against each other by weaponizing their secrets and their greed. As trust erodes, Yuichi reveals a chillingly calculating side. It is a nasty, gripping betrayal thriller.

Tomodachi Game directly captures the cutthroat social betrayal of Blue Lock's selection trials. Contestants are sealed in a closed facility and pushed through psychological games engineered to exploit their selfish greed, exactly the way Blue Lock's program is built to reward the players willing to put their own ego first. Best of all, it is led by a protagonist who looks harmless but slowly reveals a terrifying, cold-blooded predatory intelligence, which is the same awakening-of-a-ruthless-ego arc that Blue Lock puts its strikers through. There is no sport here, but the manipulation, the facility, and the worship of ego are a strong fit.

5. Kakegurui

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Netflix

Synopsis: At Hyakkaou Private Academy, the children of the elite settle their entire social hierarchy through high-stakes gambling, and those who lose are reduced to the status of “pets.” Into this twisted world walks Yumeko Jabami, a beautiful transfer student who turns out to be a compulsive gambler addicted to the pure thrill of risk. She upends the academy's power structure one insane bet at a time. It is stylish, manic, and gleefully unhinged.

Kakegurui is the manic psychological counterpart to Blue Lock. Elite students in a closed, Darwinian hierarchy risk their entire futures on high-stakes games, and the series captures Blue Lock's distorted, manic facial expressions, its intoxicating addiction to risk, and the total degradation of losers, reduced here to literal pets. That ecstatic, wide-eyed hunger for the gamble is the exact energy Blue Lock brings to its biggest moments on the pitch. It swaps soccer for cards and dice, so there is no sport, but the unhinged competitive psychology is a dead-on match. One note: it streams on Netflix, so availability depends on your region.

6. Classroom of the Elite

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Kiyotaka Ayanokoji enrolls at a prestigious government-run school that appears to offer total freedom, only for its students to learn that everything is a ruthless competition. Classes are ranked and rewarded based on cutthroat evaluations, and the lowest tier is treated as disposable. From the bottom-ranked Class D, the deceptively ordinary Ayanokoji begins quietly manipulating his way upward. It is a cold, cerebral game of strategy and hidden ability.

Classroom of the Elite is the institutional parallel to Blue Lock. It is a meritocratic, closed-off academy where students are assigned ranks through cutthroat evaluations, forced to discard dead weight and exploit every systematic loophole to climb from the bottom tier toward the top. That is Blue Lock's ranking-and-elimination engine applied to a school, right down to the hero who hides a genius-level ability and reveals it only when the stakes demand it. There is no sport and the tone is colder and quieter, but the rank-or-be-cut institutional build is a strong match.

7. The Prince of Tennis

Similarity Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Tennis prodigy Ryoma Echizen returns from America to enroll at Seishun Academy, home to one of Japan's top middle-school tennis teams, aiming to become the best in the country. Facing a parade of eccentric rivals, he unleashes increasingly outlandish signature techniques in brutal one-on-one matches. Each opponent brings a distinct, over-the-top playstyle. It is a blueprint for the flashy competitive sports anime.

The Prince of Tennis is the godfather of the exaggerated sports weapon, the show that made the “signature technique” a genre staple long before Blue Lock. Its arrogant prodigies unleash physics-defying special moves and sharply distinct playstyles, breaking their opponents' spirits across brutal singles matches, which is exactly the flashy, ability-driven, ego-forward spectacle Blue Lock delivers on the pitch. The one-on-one framing of tennis also mirrors Blue Lock's striker-versus-striker duels. It is an older, more straightforward sports anime, but the over-the-top signature-weapon build runs straight through it.

8. Eyeshield 21

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Timid, perpetually-bullied Sena Kobayakawa has spent his life running errands, and all that fleeing has made him astonishingly fast. The scheming captain of Deimon High's struggling American football team spots his talent and drafts him as a secret weapon, the masked running back “Eyeshield 21.” As Sena grows into the role, the team fights its way toward the national championship. It is explosive, funny, and relentlessly hype.

Eyeshield 21 was written by Riichiro Inagaki, who went on to create Dr. Stone, and it shares Blue Lock's love of turning a sport into a series of tactical, weapon-versus-weapon clashes. A timid kid with one devastating gift, blistering spatial acceleration, is drafted into an eccentric American football team, and the series frames every down and drive as a chess match between specialized abilities. Sena awakening his speed and sharpening it into a true threat mirrors Isagi weaponizing his own talent, all delivered with flashy, shonen-battle energy. It is more team-oriented and comedic, so it sits a bit lower, but the discover-your-weapon-and-duel build is a strong match.

9. Inazuma Eleven

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Netflix

Synopsis: Raimon Middle School's soccer club is on the verge of being disbanded, until the grandson of its legendary former goalkeeper, Mamoru Endou, rallies a ragtag team to rebuild it. As they climb through national tournaments, matches escalate into spectacles of outrageous special moves, elemental techniques, and summoned avatars. The soccer here bends the laws of physics without apology. It is a kids' franchise with enormous, over-the-top heart.

Inazuma Eleven is the extreme battle-shonen soccer counterpart to Blue Lock, taking the idea of individual signature weapons to its absolute limit. It features elemental special moves, summoned avatars, and team-annihilating strikes that turn a football pitch into a literal war zone, which is Blue Lock's flashy, ability-forward presentation pushed into full-on fantasy. The sport is the same, and the obsession with unique, match-breaking techniques lines up. It is aimed at a much younger audience and has none of Blue Lock's dark psychology, which keeps it lower, but the super-powered-soccer build fits. One note: Inazuma Eleven's streaming availability is unsettled right now, so check your region.

10. Ping Pong the Animation

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 5/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: Two childhood friends, the cocky natural “Peco” and the withdrawn, gifted Smile, move through the fiercely competitive world of high school table tennis, crossing paths with rivals who each carry their own burdens and dreams. Beneath a distinctive, scratchy art style lies a razor-sharp study of talent, ambition, and what it costs to chase greatness. It is less about winning than about who these players really are. It is a bona fide masterpiece.

Directed by the visionary Masaaki Yuasa, Ping Pong the Animation is a deep, psychological deconstruction of raw talent versus grueling effort. It sits with the painful reality at the center of Blue Lock, that the absolute summit of any sport has room for only one champion, and it studies what that truth does to the gifted and the hard-working alike. That obsession with talent, ego, and the loneliness of chasing the very top is the thematic soul Blue Lock hides under its flashy soccer. The scratchy arthouse style and slow burn are nothing like Blue Lock's spectacle, which keeps it lower on surface build, but on pure theme it cuts closer than almost anything.

11. Megalo Box

Similarity Rating: 3/5 ⭐⭐⭐

Anime Overall Rating: 4/5 ⭐⭐⭐⭐

Watch on: Crunchyroll

Synopsis: In a gritty near-future, fighters wield mechanical “Gear” to enhance their bodies in a souped-up version of boxing called Megalo Box. “Junk Dog,” a nobody throwing rigged matches in the underground circuit, decides to claw his way up to the official championship tournament, discarding his Gear entirely to prove himself against the best. His raw drive makes him impossible to count out. It is stylish, lean, and ferociously cool.

Megalo Box is a gritty, high-stakes tournament story in which an unsponsored underdog enters an elite, heavily mechanized boxing gauntlet, famously stripping away his protective Gear to fight with nothing but pride and raw survival instinct. That image of a nobody with overwhelming ego forcing his way through a brutal bracket to reach the top is pure Blue Lock, just transposed to the ring. The cool, stylized presentation and the single-minded, selfish drive of its lead echo Blue Lock's strikers chasing the number-one spot. It is grimier and more grounded, with no survival-program staging, but the ego-fueled rise through a cutthroat tournament fits well.

A couple of sports classics that watch differently

Search around for anime similar to Blue Lock and a couple of beloved sports anime come up that share the intensity but not the ruthless, ego-first philosophy. They are worth your time. We just want the match to feel honest.

Haikyuu!! delivers some of the most electric, high-stakes matches ever animated, but its entire soul is teamwork, trust, and bonds, which is the exact opposite of Blue Lock's worship of individual ego. Captain Tsubasa is the legendary soccer anime that inspired real-world pros, yet it runs on pure love of the game and sportsmanship, the warm heart Blue Lock deliberately rejects. Both are fantastic. Just go in knowing they deliver the thrill of competition with heart, not Blue Lock's cold, winner-takes-all edge.

FAQs

Is there an anime exactly like Blue Lock?

Not a perfect twin, since Blue Lock's survival-game-meets-soccer premise is genuinely unique, but two come very close. Kuroko's Basketball matches the flashy, ability-driven sports battles with signature weapons that echo Metavision, while Aoashi gives its soccer hero that exact bird's-eye spatial awareness inside a cutthroat youth-academy selection. Each nails a core piece of what makes it work.

What anime captures Blue Lock's survival-game feeling?

If the locked-in-a-facility, cutthroat-elimination side of Blue Lock is the draw, Kaiji, Tomodachi Game, Kakegurui, and Classroom of the Elite are the picks. Each traps its cast in a closed, ruthless system where only the clever and the selfish climb, exactly like Jinpachi Ego's program.

What is the best anime like Blue Lock?

For the closest overall experience, Kuroko's Basketball leads on the signature-weapon sports spectacle, and Kaiji leads on the ruthless survival-game structure. If you want the same sport with Blue Lock's exact signature ability, Aoashi is the one to beat.

Is there a soccer anime like Blue Lock?

Yes. Aoashi is the closest in spirit, following a gifted teen with Isagi-style field vision through a brutal pro youth academy, while Inazuma Eleven cranks the flashy special moves up to a fantasy extreme. For a warmer classic, Captain Tsubasa delivers the beautiful game without Blue Lock's cold edge.

Where can you watch anime like Blue Lock?

Most of these stream on Crunchyroll, including Blue Lock itself, while Kakegurui sits on Netflix. Inazuma Eleven's availability is unsettled, so check your region. Rights shift over time, so double-check what is available where you are before you settle in.
