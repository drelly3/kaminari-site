With over 1,160 episodes, catching up on One Piece can feel like a boss fight you're not sure you can win. The good news: only about 8% of it is filler (roughly 94 episodes), so the mountain is smaller than it looks. Even better, fresh filler basically stopped in 2026.

When the Elbaph Arc began, Toei switched One Piece to a seasonal schedule that adapts Oda's manga almost one-to-one, and the filler faucet shut off. For the first time in the show's history, the filler list sits still, which means you can finally map the whole thing.

So here's the plan. First, the complete skip list sorted by arc, the kind you can scan in about ten seconds. Then the filler arcs that are genuinely worth a watch, along with the "fake skips" that quietly carry real canon. Skip clean, miss nothing.

One Piece Filler Episodes That Are Secretly Peak

Filler doesn't automatically mean bad, and anyone who says otherwise probably hasn't seen G-8. There isn't a deep bench here, so instead of padding a list to hit a round number, here are the five filler entries that genuinely earn a spot in your watch. Everything else on the skip list above you can pass on without a second thought.

One quick warning before the picks. A couple of episodes get flagged as filler on the big trackers but are actually canon, so don't let a skip-list talk you out of them. The Davy Back Fight (207–219) comes straight from the manga, and its characters matter again later. Episode 1084 lands as anime canon on most trackers too. Neither is filler, so keep both in your watch.

1. G-8 Arc (196–206)

The undisputed king of One Piece filler, and it isn't close. Coming down from Skypiea, the Going Merry doesn't splash into open sea. It lands inside the sealed bay of a heavily fortified Marine base run by a sharp Vice Admiral named Jonathan. What follows is eleven episodes of infiltration comedy and slow-building tension as the scattered crew goes undercover to steal their ship back.

The character beats are the draw. Sanji talks his way into the base kitchen and duels the head cook, Usopp invents a fake identity named Condoriano that fans still meme about, and Nami runs a nurse disguise and a wounded-victim bluff to slip past the guards. Jonathan is the glue: a calm, genuinely clever antagonist who keeps predicting the crew's moves and nearly boxes them in, popular enough that fans have wanted him made canon for years. It plays like something Oda himself could have written.

2. Ocean's Dream Arc (220–224)

A seahorse called Noko steals the crew's memories overnight, and one morning they wake up not recognizing each other. On paper, that sounds like a lazy clip-show excuse. It isn't. The crew's bond is the whole engine of One Piece, and stripping it away has real weight. Some of them, memories gone, revert to who they were before they ever joined up, which makes for both comedy and a few genuinely tense moments between crewmates.

Without spoiling how it resolves, the final stretch leans on the crew's history in a way that lands emotionally, and it does it without endless recycled flashbacks. Adapted from an old PlayStation game, it's self-contained, with five tight episodes with no lasting consequences. Verdict: Great.

3. Z's Ambition Arc (575–578)

Set after Fish-Man Island, this is the direct prequel to One Piece Film: Z, one of the most loved movies in the franchise. The crew crosses a vicious stretch of the New World that throws mirage fleets and weather shifting from giant hail to avalanche snow to the sea splitting open beneath them. Along the way, they pick up a giant who can shrink herself and agree to help her out of a jam. In the background, a new group of antagonists is quietly introduced, the pieces that the film then pays off. The action punches above the usual filler grade, and it flows straight into the movie, which picks up almost exactly where these episodes stop. If you plan to watch Film Z (and you should), start here. Verdict: Worth It as a pair.

4. Toriko × One Piece × Dragon Ball Z (590)

For years, this was the "lost" One Piece episode. It's the second half of a one-hour crossover special (paired with Toriko episode 99) that aired in Japan in 2013 to celebrate Toriko's anniversary, back when the two shows shared Fuji TV's Sunday morning "Dream 9" block. Luffy, Goku, and Toriko all enter a gourmet tournament for a legendary cut of meat, which spirals into a three-way team-up against a power-draining sea beast. Watching Luffy and Goku throw hands side by side is the kind of thing that basically never happens in anime. Toonami skipped it for years, Crunchyroll never carried it, and it only reached Netflix with fresh dubs fairly recently, so it's been genuinely tough to track down. Watch it for the novelty and the slice of anime history. Verdict: Fun Detour.

5. Little East Blue Arc (426–429)

The lead-in to One Piece Film: Strong World, and the most "your mileage may vary" pick here, so I'll be straight about it. The crew lands on an island nicknamed Little East Blue because its residents are all East Blue transplants, which means the town is dotted with miniature versions of the places the crew calls home. That nostalgia is the charm. The villains are a mariachi-themed pirate crew, and their captain carries one of the more inventive filler powers, a Devil Fruit that lets him form nets out of anything he eats, including turning his own body into one. The fight gives Luffy a fun problem to solve. It ties into Strong World by setting up the movie's threat. It's not top-tier, and it interrupts the run to a major canon arc, so keep expectations in check. As a warm-up to a great movie, though, it does the job. Verdict: Worth a Look.

What Actually Counts as Filler in One Piece?

Confused about why some filler is safe to skip, and some isn't? Here's the breakdown. One Piece filler comes in four flavors.

TypeWhat it isSkip it?
Pure FillerAnime-original content not in Oda's mangaYes
Canon FillerAnime-original scenes that stay true to canon and expand it without changing outcomesSkim
Mixed CanonCanon episodes padded with anime-only scenesNo, watch
Recaps / CrossoversClip shows and promo tie-ins (Toriko, DBZ, movie prequels)Usually

One Piece keeps its filler percentage so low for a subtle reason. Toei tends to sprinkle small filler scenes inside otherwise-canon episodes instead of building whole filler arcs. That approach stretches the material without bloating the skip list, which is clever even if it occasionally tests your patience.

Hot Take: G-8 Should Be Made Canon

We'll say it a little louder for the folks in the back: the G-8 Arc (196–206) holds its own against plenty of canon arcs. Tight plotting, real character beats for the whole crew, and an "escape the Marine fortress" premise that delivers as much tension and comedy as a lot of the main story around it. A big part of the fandom treats it with the kind of affection usually saved for canon, and there's a long-running wish for Oda to fold it into the official timeline.

Our take? Filler this good challenges the whole "filler equals worthless" idea. The word describes where an episode came from, not how good it is. Plenty of filler is easy to pass on, sure. A blanket "skip all filler" rule, though, would cost you one of the most enjoyable stretches in the series. Watch G-8. That's the hill we'll stand on.

Skip Smart and Enjoy the Voyage

One Piece is one of the most rewarding stories in anime, and with the filler list frozen, it's never been friendlier to newcomers. Use the No-Filler Cut to move quickly, keep the fake skips in mind so you don't lose a thread, and give the strong filler like G-8 a real chance. The point was never to rush the journey. It's to spend your time on the episodes that reward it.

One Piece Filler FAQ

How many episodes of One Piece are filler?

About 94 out of roughly 1,168, which works out to around 8% of the series.

What arcs are filler in One Piece?

The main filler arcs are Warship Island, Goat Island and Ruluka Island, G-8, Ocean's Dream, Ice Hunter, Spa Island, Little East Blue, Z's Ambition, Caesar Retrieval, Silver Mine, Marine Rookie, and Cidre Guild, plus a handful of scattered one-offs and crossovers.

Are episodes 1029–1030 filler?

Yes, they're a Uta and Film Red tie-in. Watch them if you're seeing the movie. Otherwise, they're easy to pass on.

Are the Toriko crossover episodes (492, 542, 590) filler?

All three are. Episode 590 (the Toriko × One Piece × Dragon Ball Z special) is the most fun of the bunch.

Will there be more One Piece filler after 2026?

Almost certainly not. The seasonal, one-to-one chapter adaptation that started with Elbaph has effectively closed the filler era.
