Dragon Ball Z or Dragon Ball Z Kai?

The first real decision is Z or Kai. Dragon Ball Z is the original 291-episode run, famous for filler: long stretches of stalling and side quests that pad the manga's story out. Dragon Ball Z Kai is the 2009 remaster that cuts most of that, tightens the pacing, and sticks close to the manga at around 167 episodes.

For a first watch, we would go with Kai. It tells the same story with far less waiting, which matters a lot across a run this long. Watch full Z instead if you want the complete nostalgic experience, filler included, since some of that filler is beloved. If you do take on Z and want to know exactly what to skip, our Dragon Ball Z filler list marks every episode, and the original series has its own padding covered in our Dragon Ball filler list. This is the biggest single choice in how to watch Dragon Ball in order.

GT, the movies, and what counts as canon

Two things sit outside the main series and confuse people. The first is Dragon Ball GT. The anime made it without Akira Toriyama's manga, so it runs in its own alternate timeline and is not canon. Plenty of fans still enjoy it for Super Saiyan 4 and its ending, so treat it as an optional what-if to watch after everything else, or skip.

The second is the movies. Dragon Ball has more than twenty films, and most are non-canon side adventures you can watch any time for fun. Only four count as canon: Battle of Gods and Resurrection F, whose events Super later retells, then Dragon Ball Super: Broly and Dragon Ball Super: Super Hero, which continue the story after Super's finale. Watch Broly after Super episode 131, then Super Hero after that. Super itself has a little filler too, flagged in our Dragon Ball Super filler list, though far less than old Z.

FAQ

What is the correct order to watch Dragon Ball?

The correct order to watch Dragon Ball is Dragon Ball, then Dragon Ball Z (or Kai), then Dragon Ball Super, then Dragon Ball Daima. That is how to watch Dragon Ball in order through the full canon. Dragon Ball GT is non-canon and optional, and only four of the movies are canon, so most films are bonus viewing.

Should I watch Dragon Ball Z or Kai?

Kai is the better pick for most first-time viewers. It is a trimmed remaster of Dragon Ball Z that removes the heavy filler and follows the manga more closely, so the story moves faster. Watch full Z instead if you want the complete original experience, filler and all.

Is Dragon Ball GT canon?

No. Dragon Ball GT was made without Akira Toriyama's manga and runs in its own alternate timeline, so it is not canon. Dragon Ball Super is the official continuation instead. GT is still a fun optional watch for fans, best saved for after the canon series.

Do I need to watch the Dragon Ball movies?

Mostly no. Over twenty Dragon Ball films exist, and most are non-canon side stories. Only Battle of Gods, Resurrection F, Broly, and Super Hero are canon, and even the first two are retold inside Dragon Ball Super. Watch the rest purely for fun.

Where can I watch Dragon Ball?
