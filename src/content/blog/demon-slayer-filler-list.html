Does Demon Slayer have filler? Barely any. Across 63 episodes and four seasons, most filler databases put the count at zero. One pegs it at a single episode, roughly 2%. Either way, Demon Slayer holds one of the cleanest adaptation records in shonen anime history.

But the full Demon Slayer filler list tells a more interesting story than that stat suggests. The anime's final TV season, the Hashira Training Arc, stretched eight or nine manga chapters into eight full episodes, with almost 90% of the season consisting of anime-original content that Koyoharu Gotouge's manga never included. Technically filler-free. Practically debatable.

Since there's genuinely nothing to skip, a traditional filler guide isn't much help. What you actually need is a plan for catching up efficiently, whether you're prepping for the Infinity Castle trilogy (Part 1 hit digital streaming in July 2026, Part 2 confirmed for 2027) or you just want to know enough about Demon Slayer to hold a conversation without watching all 63 episodes. The Demon Slayer filler list is short. The catch-up guide below is where the real value lives.

How to Catch Up on Demon Slayer Fast

A Demon Slayer filler list can't do what a Naruto or Bleach filler list does, because there's nothing to cut. Every episode across all four seasons is canon (or close enough), so the traditional "skip these, watch those" approach breaks down immediately. What actually helps is a plan based on how much time you have.

Here are three watch paths depending on your situation. Pick the one that fits and you'll walk away understanding the story, the characters, and why Infinity Castle matters.

The Full Run (~26 Hours)

All 63 episodes. No trimming, no tough calls.

At 26 hours, Demon Slayer is shorter than a single season of many long-running shonen. You can finish it in a week of evening sessions or a dedicated long weekend. The only decision is how you handle the Mugen Train arc: the movie (1h 57min) or the TV version (Episodes 27–33, roughly 2h 45min). The TV version includes Episode 27, an anime-original Rengoku prelude that adds backstory to the Flame Hashira before the train mission. Both options work. Pick one, not both.

Skip the recap compilation films (To the Swordsmith Village, To the Hashira Training). They condense season transitions into theatrical packages and contain nothing you won't see in the episodes themselves.

If you have the time, the full run is the path we'd recommend. The series earned its zero-filler reputation by making every episode count, and the character moments you'd skip in the shorter paths are the ones that make Infinity Castle's sacrifices land at full force.

Is the Hashira Training Arc Filler?

No. And the question itself reveals a gap in how the anime community talks about adaptations.

Traditional filler exists because a running anime catches up to its manga and needs to stall for time. Naruto invented entire villages and villains to buy chapters. Bleach sent Ichigo on side quests. The content contradicts nothing, but it also leads nowhere. You can pull it out of the timeline and the story doesn't flinch.

The Hashira Training Arc doesn't fit that definition. Ufotable didn't add anime-original content because they ran out of manga. They added it because the manga's version of the arc was too thin to support a full season and too important to compress into a movie or a few throwaway episodes. Nine chapters of training montage became eight episodes of character work, and the result is a season that asks you to invest in every Hashira before sending them into the deadliest battle of the series.

Critical reception was divided. One review called it "a filler-filled season" and compared its pacing to One Piece at its slowest. Others argued the expanded scenes set a new benchmark for what anime-original content can achieve when a studio actually commits to it. Fan response, measured by the specific scenes people share and discuss, sides firmly with the expansion. The Tengen training sessions, the Giyu and Tanjiro soba eating contest (an entirely anime-original scene that fans called one of the best moments in the series), and the extended Hashira backstories are among the most talked-about moments from the franchise's final year on television.

The Season 4 finale, Episode 63 "The Hashira Unite," earned some of the strongest fan reactions of any episode in the show. Muzan's attack on the Ubuyashiki estate, Gyomei Himejima stepping into combat, and Tamayo's return gave the season a closing stretch that justified the slower buildup. If you watch only two episodes from Hashira Training, make them 62 and 63.

If you skip Season 4 entirely, you can follow Infinity Castle's plot. You'll know who's fighting and why. You'll know Gyomei Himejima is the strongest Hashira. You won't know what it cost him to become one. And when he steps into the Infinity Castle, that difference between knowing and feeling is the difference between watching a fight and caring who walks out of it.

The Hashira Training Arc is anime-original content done well. It's not filler. It's Ufotable forging the blade with extra folds in the steel.

FAQ

Does Demon Slayer have filler?

Technically, no. Demon Slayer has zero full filler episodes across its entire 63-episode run. The closest the series gets is a handful of anime-original scenes in the Hashira Training Arc (Season 4) and Episode 27, which is an entirely original Rengoku story not found in the manga. Some guides classify these as "mixed canon/filler" or "anime canon," but none of them are traditional filler in the way Naruto or Bleach fans would recognize the term.

Is the Hashira Training Arc filler?

No, though it's the most debated season in the series. The Hashira Training Arc adapted only eight or nine manga chapters into eight episodes, meaning the majority of its runtime is anime-original. Ufotable used the extra space to expand character backstories and training sequences that the manga condensed or skipped. The content doesn't contradict the original story, and most of it adds real depth to characters who play major roles in the Infinity Castle films. Anime-original, yes. Filler, no.

How much of Demon Slayer is filler?

Between 0% and 2%, depending on which filler guide you check. The disagreement comes down to whether anime-original scenes (content created by the studio that doesn't appear in the manga but doesn't contradict it) count as filler. Most databases say they don't, putting Demon Slayer at 0%. One counts a single episode, landing the percentage at 2%. Either way, Demon Slayer is one of the lowest-filler shonen anime ever produced.

Can I skip the Mugen Train TV arc if I saw the movie?

Yes. The Mugen Train movie (2020) and the TV arc (Episodes 27–33) tell the same core story. The only exclusive content in the TV version is Episode 27, an anime-original Rengoku prelude that follows him on a solo mission the night before the train. If you watched the movie, start the Entertainment District Arc at Episode 34. Consider going back for Episode 27 when you have time; it's a strong standalone episode that adds real texture to Rengoku's character.

You'll miss slower character-building episodes, but you'll understand the full story and every major player.
